# Coarse 3D anatomy-to-plane correction 148: development protocol

The frozen 132 direct pose bank has a near-truth candidate on 179/248 informative v4 sections, but its first-choice candidate is near on only 73/248. The 143 local field maps an exact plane well but cannot reliably repair a nearby wrong plane; its support-only arm is comparable. This experiment asks whether a *wider, anatomy-specific 3D correspondence search* can correct a candidate's global plane before the existing affine-free local warp. It is a development experiment, not a GUI qualification or a probability calibration.

Continue only the scratch-trained 132 lineage. Initialize a new source/atlas correspondence module randomly, with matched full-intensity and atlas-intensity-zeroed arms. Each batch independently samples a synthetic physical section from the v4/v3 TRAIN generator; no plane or artifact settings are carried between sections, and a chance repeat is allowed. Train on exact, nearby and wrong candidate planes, using the known observed-pixel CCF map for correspondence and the known rigid plane for global correction. Do not use either truth at inference. The 132 pose predictor is not trained or used during matcher warm-up; it supplies frozen blind DEV candidate beams for evaluation. Do not call this joint fitting-to-pose training until an anatomical DEV gate passes and gradients are actually enabled through candidate rendering into the pose predictor.

The correction searches a 3D finite-thickness atlas neighborhood around each candidate and fits one global plane from spatially distributed correspondences. This warm-up evaluates that plane correction alone. If its DEV gate passes, the joint model must rerender at the corrected plane and fit an affine-free local tissue warp, so the warp cannot silently absorb a wrong global plane. Tissue support may exclude invalid atlas keys, but the source/atlas interior anatomy—not raw support fraction, candidate coordinates, or a truth-provided mask—must make the full-intensity arm better than the intensity-zeroed control. Eventual candidate scores must use post-correction fitting, not initial pose error alone.

This is an experiment informed by differentiable correspondence-confidence plus global pose fitting in [Deep Global Registration](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Choy_Deep_Global_Registration_CVPR_2020_paper.html), and by the cross-modality structural-feature problem highlighted by [Mok et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Mok_Modality-Agnostic_Structural_Image_Representation_Learning_for_Deformable_Multi-Modality_Medical_Image_CVPR_2024_paper.html). Neither paper establishes that these ingredients work for a single damaged histological brain section or arbitrary cutting angle; the paired controls and physical tests above must determine that here.

Freeze the existing v4 and v3 synthetic DEV panels and the separate-donor real coronal/sagittal DEV records; never train on them. Evaluate corrected rigid error at all valid observed tissue sites for the exact state and the truth-best branch of the frozen blind 132 beam, with a separately identified near (initial error ≤1.5 mm) subset. Compare identical sections, branches and denominators across arms and checkpoints. The minimum scientific gate for further joint continuation is: on the v4 blind-near subset, full-intensity correction reduces plan-equal mean rigid error by at least 0.30 mm, beats the matched support-only arm by at least 0.15 mm, and does not worsen more than 30% of sections by >0.10 mm; on exact-plane cases its mean error remains ≤0.25 mm. Check v3 retention and real-donor weak-reference direction separately. These thresholds screen a mechanism; clearing them would not establish deployable blind selection, physical oblique generalization, or calibrated uncertainty.

If the correction works, train the *same* model end to end: rerender and fit after the corrected pose; use post-fit anatomy and deformation plausibility to update the candidate posterior and backpropagate into the direct pose head, alongside known synthetic pose/map supervision and real weak-label retention. Evaluate blind first-choice selection and complete point mapping. If it fails, diagnose anatomy signal and synthetic/real appearance rather than extending unchanged or claiming arbitrary-plane capability. A physical expert-aligned steep-oblique animal set remains required to know whether truly arbitrary cuts are reliable; synthetic normal coverage alone cannot answer that.

No final-test animals, DeepSlice public benchmark, expert physical-oblique truth, or calibration data are authorized for this experiment.

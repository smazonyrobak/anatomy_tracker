# 221: framing adaptation helps slightly but does not solve blind arbitrary-plane pose

The predeclared TRAIN-only comparison continued the project's randomly initialized 187 step-800 pose checkpoint for 400 updates in each of two arms. Both optimized the same encoder and pose/anchor heads with fresh AdamW, three independent synthetic sections, Lupori fluorescence rehearsal, and alternating older coronal/sagittal TRAIN rehearsal per batch. Two synthetic slots used new support-point full-sphere geometry 166 and v4/v3 artifacts. One arm passed each through 217 tissue-aware framing; the other left it in the original subject box. The third synthetic slot retained older v7 geometry. The two arm streams had no shared synthetic physical-section IDs; each had 1,200 unique accepted sections, including 800 new-geometry sections. AL1A was excluded from gradients.

The code/protocol was committed before outcomes (`ca96cee`), and the separate fixed readout before either arm finished (`6720a77`). Both 400-update runs exited normally. All checkpoint, draw-log, training-log and config hashes in their completion receipts were independently rechecked, as were the readout's three output and five source hashes. The fixed readout contains 64 independently sampled cases each for unframed and framed 166 geometry, plus 32 older-v7 cases; its 800 records evaluate the same cases on the parent and both arms at updates 200 and 400. These are synthetic TRAIN-inner planes from bases already represented in the parent lineage, not biologically independent animals. Errors are per-case mean CCF displacement at 128 surviving tissue sites, reported below as the median of cases; they are **not** the Lupori human-affine five-point metric.

On the 52-section AL1A TRAIN animal, first-choice disagreement with the human VisuAlign **weak** five-point reference was 1.344 mm for the parent, 1.224 mm for the unframed arm, and 1.338 mm for the framed arm. Thus the framed arm did not materially regress this one near-coronal guard, but it did not improve it as the control did. The fixed older-v7 synthetic first-choice median stayed within 2% of baseline in both arms. A separate older-real TRAIN guard selected one midpoint section per donor: 48 coronal and all 40 sagittal. The parent-to-framed-400 donor-equal mean five-point disagreement was 0.581 to 0.565 mm on coronal and 1.382 to 1.352 mm on sagittal; the unframed-400 arm reached 0.522 and 1.370 mm respectively. This is evidence of retention on weak TRAIN alignments, **not** an independent real-anatomy accuracy test. The guard exited normally, and its config, summary, and 440-row result hashes match its completion receipt.

The framed arm's gain on its intended panel is small: relative to the unframed arm at update 400, its paired per-case median improvement is only 0.071 mm (38/64 cases improved), despite a 0.161-mm difference in panel medians. At that checkpoint, just 16/64 framed cases were <2 mm at first choice, whereas 44/64 had such a branch in the top 16 and 53/64 somewhere among all 160. This separates a substantial ranking problem from a still-real proposal-coverage problem. The 64 framed cases averaged 28% visible tissue (range 4–72%); the angle/visibility strata are small, and even the 16 cases above 35% visibility retained a 3.485-mm first-choice median. Low tissue content is therefore not the sole observed cause, although it plausibly worsens ambiguity.

**Decision:** neither 221 arm is promoted, calibrated, benchmarked publicly, or installed in the GUI. Four hundred updates/800 new sections per arm cannot establish the asymptotic limit, but simply scaling the same recipe without explaining candidate geometry and ranking is not justified by this small gain. A read-only replay of the same 64 framed cases found that the nearest fixed anchor normal is only 7.74° from truth at the median; after the framed-400 learned residual, the nearest predicted anchor normal is 6.36° away. Nevertheless the first-choice normal is 39.44° away. All 64 first choices come from the 16 free modes, while 23 truth-best branches come from the anchor family; median *total* anchor probability mass is only 0.259. Thus geometric normal tiling is ample, and the immediate defect is the proposal/selection objective and family under-ranking, not absence of arbitrary-direction anchors. A per-case rigid-truth versus dense-deformation decomposition is the next fixed-data diagnostic before changing that objective. Physical steep-oblique animal validation remains unavailable; no synthetic readout can certify the user's any-angle deployment requirement.

# Global-correlation pose feedback 037 — frozen development result

The predeclared 037 pose-capture gate **failed**. The standalone scratch-lineage 019 predictor and its local mapper were frozen, while two identically initialized and trained full-frame correction heads saw the same 8,000 freshly generated TRAIN sections over 4,000 batches. One head used a finite-thickness candidate atlas render, the first-pass fit and 16×16 all-pair image–atlas correlation; the matched control used image–image correlation and image-only channels. Both also had exact synthetic global-residual and observed-tissue physical supervision. This tests whether candidate-specific atlas evidence improves correction; it is **not** the final joint-training step because the direct predictor was frozen.

The new DEV panel was frozen and audited before evaluation: eight previously unseen **synthetic deformation identities**, 32 independent random planes each. All 256 draws remain in its manifest; 177 met the predefined visible-tissue threshold for physical-error scoring and 79 did not (19–27 eligible per identity). This is not independent biological validation because all synthetic identities use the same Allen atlas and generator. All 64 weakly labelled real DEV images belong to six separate donors. No public benchmark, calibration animals or final-test animals were used. The independent read-only verifier passed 8,000 unique accepted TRAIN section IDs, training/checkpoint/source hashes, 256 panel identities/records, 1,928 raw DEV rows, group-weighted metrics and the gate calculation.

These are equal-identity synthetic means and equal-donor weak-real means, not per-slice-weighted pooled estimates. At batch 4,000 the atlas head beat the control by **0.161 mm** on selected synthetic error, satisfying that one comparison, but improved only **0.096 mm** against its parent selected error and **0.006 mm** on best-of-eight. The required improvements were 0.250 and 0.200 mm. Its worst weak-real donor selected-error regression was **0.518 mm**, beyond the allowed 0.200 mm. The atlas head's top-one plane-normal error remained **47.3°** versus **47.4°** at initialization (antipodal normal angle, not an AP tilt or a region error). It therefore cannot be promoted or described as accurate. On the 112 eligible synthetic cases whose original best-eight error was at most 1 mm, mean best-eight error stayed approximately **0.681 mm** after correction, rather than showing a useful local fitting basin. The raw/black/imperfect-brush strata did not reveal a large hidden success (best-eight mean changes approximately −0.041, −0.010 and +0.028 mm, respectively; negative means improvement).

The experiment gives a narrow but useful conclusion: atlas information affected the correction head more than image-only information, yet **a 2D render at a wrong plane plus global in-plane correlation did not teach substantial physical pose movement**. All-pair correlation is well established for optical flow ([RAFT](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/html/3526_ECCV_2020_paper.php)), but an off-plane histology image may not contain structures present in its candidate atlas section; optical-flow success does not imply useful through-plane pose gradients here. The next targeted change should expose **nearby 3D atlas anatomy across normal displacement and tilt**, then train physically supervised correspondence/fit energy on wrong-plane proposals and check its actual correction basin before allowing its fitting loss to train the direct pose predictor. [ProST](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10879149/) provides a separate precedent for shaping a registration similarity's pose gradient, not a guarantee for this modality. Do not extend the unchanged 037 run, claim calibrated probabilities, or run DeepSlice benchmarking.

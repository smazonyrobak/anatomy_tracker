# 212–214: native STPT anatomy helps the local map, not reliable full-plane pose

The frozen [212 protocol](ALLEN_NATIVE_STPT_212_MATCHED_PROTOCOL_20261011.md) compared two 400-update continuations of the same self-trained 187 checkpoint and optimizer state. Per update, the control saw three independently sampled atlas-derived synthetic sections; the native arm replaced one with a virtual arbitrary-plane cut through an acquired TRAIN donor's original red STPT stack. Both arms also saw the same acquired Lupori TRAIN section and alternating older coronal/sagittal TRAIN rehearsal. The six native-gradient donors and two inner-held donors were fixed before training. Four hundred accepted native examples took 448 attempts; no plane or background was deliberately reused as a pair. All model data, temporary files and results stayed on `I:`.

The paired trainer completed both arms at steps 0/200/400. The physical readout evaluated **all 140 originally acquired coronal sections** of each inner-held donor 14599 and 14600, against Allen's authored but *weak* CCF registration. The local-coordinate readout supplies the correct affine plane to the mapper at its trained 96-pixel resolution; it is not end-to-end predicted-pose mapping. Values below are millimetres of weak-reference disagreement, not expert landmark error.

The native arm slightly improves the *teacher-forced* local map on both donors, but worsens physical-section pose p50 by 0.106 and 0.079 mm versus control, and p90 by 1.259 and 0.954 mm. The >5-mm errors are confined to the <10%-visible-tissue stratum: donor 14599 has 11 such native-arm sections versus 4 control, and donor 14600 has 11 versus 2. A tiny surviving fragment can be genuinely ambiguous, so this is also a warning against forcing a confident coordinate for every slice.

The secondary 64-cut-per-donor virtual-oblique panel is **reconstructed from serial coronal acquisitions**, not physically acquired at those angles. At step 400 its top-1 *full-canvas five-point* p50 is 12.156→11.256 mm for donor 14599 and 10.653→9.055 mm for donor 14600 (control→native); 54/64 and 51/64 native-arm cuts remain >5 mm by that measure. On the virtual panel, the native mapper does not beat the zero map for donor 14599 (0.122 versus 0.118 mm); it does for 14600 (0.129 versus 0.131 mm). Neither is physical-oblique evidence.

A fixed post-failure 215 diagnostic re-rendered the **same** 128 virtual cases and reproduced every 213 top-1 branch and five-point error before inspecting all 80 pose modes × two reflections. The native step-400 pooled median was 10.59 mm for top-1 versus 4.44 mm for the best *existing* branch on the five-point canvas, and 3.05 versus 1.44 mm on a fixed 16×16 **visible-tissue-weighted** chart. The top-1 plane-normal-axis median was 39.16°; even the five-point oracle branch's normal median was 15.80°. The five-point oracle branch had median prior rank 11.5, not 1. Thus both candidate geometry and selecting among candidates fail. The huge full-canvas number is partly a field-of-view/support artifact: these virtual cuts expose <30% visible tissue, and a wrong pose evaluated at distant empty corners magnifies disagreement. Yet 3.05-mm *tissue* top-1 and 1.44-mm best-of-160 are still much too large for reliable electrode-site assignment. The oracle numbers used weak reconstructed truth and are diagnostic, not an inference result or independent physical validation.

The fixed retention readout also fails to justify promotion. At step 400, donor/plan-equal mean top-1 error is 3.07→3.26 mm on synthetic v3, 3.10→3.18 mm on v4, 1.01→1.19 mm on older coronal weak-real DEV, and 1.37→1.40 mm on older sagittal weak-real DEV (control→native). Lupori AL1A stays 1.77 mm in both arms. Native training loss fell only modestly across the run: its native-slot pose loss mean was 14.47 over updates 1–100 and 12.27 over 301–400, versus approximately 8.2 for the two synthetic slots. These per-batch losses are descriptive and do not substitute for held-animal accuracy.

**Decision:** the predeclared 212 advancement gate fails on both physical-section pose and p90, and the tissue-supported virtual-oblique localization error remains large. Do not promote either checkpoint, buy more native updates on this source without diagnosis, calibrate probabilities, replace the GUI model, or run the public DeepSlice benchmark. The observed local-map gain is useful but does not establish joint pose/deformation inference or anatomical-fit feedback into pose. The 215 diagnostic shows that a selector alone cannot achieve sufficient precision from the current candidates; next examine plane scale/crop and orientation representation against the acquired-volume proxy, then improve candidate capture and ranking without allowing deformation to hide wrong planes. Physical steep-oblique animal-separated truth remains necessary before claiming arbitrary-angle performance.

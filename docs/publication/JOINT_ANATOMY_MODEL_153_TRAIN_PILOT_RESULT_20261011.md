# 153 joint anatomy model: frozen TRAIN-only scratch pilot

The 1,800-section pilot completed normally. Its selected checkpoint is **update 1,300**, but it is **not a usable aligner**: on 16 fixed sections from four held-out synthetic TRAIN animals, neither of its two blind pose branches was within 1.5 mm of the true plane. Mean blind direct, corrected-rigid, and dense-map errors were **5.267, 5.101, and 5.102 mm**, respectively. These are anatomical distances in the Allen CCF, not pixel errors. The uncalibrated branch scores cannot support probabilities or electrode-region claims.

The sampler attempted 2,896 independently drawn sections and accepted 1,800 distinct physical-section IDs. Gradient sections came from 60 TRAIN virtual-animal deformation plans; checkpoint selection used four different TRAIN plans (four sections each). Every example received one independently drawn v3 or v4 appearance; none was deliberately duplicated to change its background. The accepted set comprised 1,290 raw, 264 imperfect-brush, and 246 exact-black cases. Of the 1,200 v4 cases, 647 received the low-exposure branch. Tissue occupied less than 5% of the image in 251 accepted sections and less than 10% in 492. These are challenging, sometimes intrinsically ambiguous images; this frequency has not been shown to match acquired histology.

The gate for allowing the fit to update the direct pose predictor failed at 1,300. With a supplied near-correct candidate, fitting reduced mean error from **0.899 to 0.699 mm** (gain 0.200 mm, above the required 0.100). But a supplied exact plane drifted **0.395 mm** after fitting, above the allowed 0.250 mm. Stage B therefore continued through 1,800; Stage C never ran. The candidate fit, correspondence module, and direct predictor are in one checkpoint, but this experiment **did not establish the intended feedback learning** from atlas fit into the pose predictor. In Stage B the pose module was frozen for each fitting pass; only its separate direct-pose loss trained it.

The direct loss declined modestly during training, and a near branch appeared in only 17 of the 1,800 training presentations. A separate frozen-checkpoint audit regenerated the same 16 held-out TRAIN sections and scored **all 80 modes × two reflections** against all valid tissue pixels. Even the truth-chosen best branch was within 1.5 mm in only **1/16** cases (mean best error **2.255 mm**); the top 16 and top two reached **0/16**. The top two were different modes in 15/16, so duplicate reflection branches are not the cause. The only near branch ranked 37th by direct probability with 0.76% mass. Among four sections with under 8% valid area, none had a near proposal; among 12 with at least 8%, only one did. These small strata do not establish an information threshold, but tiny sections are not the sole explanation.

This points first to **proposal coverage**, not merely a poor fit-based reranker: the true plane usually is not available to the fitter. The next bounded experiment should strengthen direct pose learning on many more independently drawn TRAIN sections, while retaining all-angle coverage and using an easier-to-harder support/exposure curriculum only early in training. Measure best-of-160 and blind top-two capture on a larger, fresh held-out TRAIN panel before resuming fit-driven pose feedback. The fitter's exact-plane drift must also fall below its gate; more fitting iterations or a wider blind beam alone cannot solve the missing-proposal result. Do not tune indefinitely against these same 16 sections.

This is an intentionally small feasibility pilot, not the eventual exposure scale or a comparison with DeepSlice. [DeepSlice](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-41645-4) trained on hundreds of thousands of already aligned **coronal** images and 0.9 million synthetic coronal sections; its authors also recommend multiple sections from one block for reliable alignment. Those conditions differ from our single arbitrary-plane input. [Slice-to-volume work such as SVoRT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10129054/) supports iterative pose updating after image/volume comparison, but does not establish that this particular synthetic dataset or learned matcher supplies trustworthy feedback. Sparse or damaged single sections may need broad uncertainty and user constraints rather than a falsely precise answer.

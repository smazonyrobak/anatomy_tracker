# Can one section be localized at any cutting angle?

**Decision now: keep arbitrary planes as the model's supported geometry. Do not impose a coronal/sagittal/horizontal family restriction yet.** The evidence does not show that steep angles are inherently impossible, but the angle-stratified diagnostics are mixed. The 64-case independent v2 synthetic stress gave fitted mapped error 2.242 mm for 36 heavy-oblique cases versus 2.655 mm for 28 less-oblique cases. On a different, more peripheral [099 DEV panel](CONTEXTUAL_MATCH_099_RESULT_20261008.md), the 24 most-oblique eligible cases had 3.454-mm selected mapped error against a 0.930-mm best-of-14 candidate. Thus the current selector may fail badly on steep planes even when a better candidate is available; neither panel proves physical all-angle transfer or an intrinsic geometric barrier. Both selected errors are far too large for a finished electrode-localization tool. The v2 best-of-eight candidate error was 1.507 mm versus the selected candidate's 2.423 mm; candidate selection remains a more immediate measured bottleneck than plane parameterization.

The newer [103 v3-input DEV gate](CROSS_CANDIDATE_MATCH_103_RESULT_20261009.md) failed: 4.028-mm selected error at its development-best checkpoint versus 2.271 mm for the physically best of 14. Its 19 cases at 45–54.7° from the nearest principal axis were poor, but so were all easier angle bins; this does not justify an angle-family restriction. The more concrete new concern is raw/no-brush candidate capture, including raw images whose exterior was exactly black. The parent pose/warp model was never trained on v3; a failed auxiliary scorer cannot settle the input-domain question.

There is nevertheless a hard information limit to the *single-section* promise. If a section is tiny, mostly featureless, missing landmarks, blurred, folded, or displaced, several 3-D planes can produce nearly the same visible image; a partial hemisphere can also leave a left/right reflection ambiguity. A flexible deformation can disguise a wrong plane by forcing a superficial fit. Thus no model can guarantee a unique exact pose on **every** possible image from image pixels alone; this is an inference from non-unique inverse geometry, not an experimental claim that arbitrary-plane recovery is generally impossible. A safe output must retain plausible alternatives and ask for a rough orientation or adjacent section, or abstain, when the image is insufficient. The researcher's rough cut direction, section order/range, insertion site/angle, and optional smart-brush mask should be *optional evidence*, not mandatory restrictions or labels silently treated as truth.

The literature is narrower than the requested guarantee. [DeepSlice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10514056/) was assessed on coronal slide-mounted sections and explicitly recommends grouping sections from the same block; pooled angle and cutting-order information improve its estimates. [AMBIA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10406728/) estimates AP position and two tilts for single sections and then deformably registers them, but does not establish unrestricted all-plane accuracy. [QuickNII](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6541252/) propagates anchor information across a serial section set. These results support an optional multi-section/prior route for ambiguous cases; they do not justify banning heavy obliques.

A more directly relevant 2025 result is [STAM](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09211-8): its authors released an isotropic 1-µm Nissl-derived 3-D mouse atlas with arbitrary-angle reslicing and a mapping to Allen CCF. They describe training a single-slice pose network from different-angle slices of two registered PI/DAPI whole-brain volumes and offer a single-image registration service. This is positive evidence that all-angle single-slice localization is technically plausible for informative images and matched modalities, **not** proof of robust accuracy on independently acquired physical steep-oblique slides, other staining methods, or our electrode task. Its atlas could be a higher-fidelity source for oblique synthetic training than the current CCF autofluorescence volume, but the [STAM data-use agreement](https://atlas.brainsmatics.cn/STAM/agreement.html) defaults open and controlled data to CC BY-NC 4.0; proprietary deployment cannot assume compatible rights. The complete 11,400×9,000×14,000 grayscale volume is roughly 1.44 TB uncompressed, so a resolution-limited subset rather than a full local copy would be the only sensible first experiment on I:. No STAM data were downloaded or used here. CCF mapping accuracy, storage/downsampling, rights, and a matched small-scale ablation need review before adoption; STAM is not a dependency of the present model.

A source-rights preflight found [DevCCFv1](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/DevCCFv1/26377171) under CC BY 4.0 with a P56 20-µm isotropic light-sheet template ([methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-53254-w)). Its native image and labels are a plausible, legally more permissive supplementary 3-D source for a small all-angle ablation, but it is an average template rather than independent Nissl-stained physical brains; its Allen-derived annotation layers require separate rights review. The [Brain Architecture Project's mouse collection](https://data.brainarchitecture.org/pages/mouse) has six reconstructed 20-µm Nissl volumes, but its [general policy](https://brainarchitecture.org/policies/) and [dataset listing](https://data.brainarchitecture.org/pages/mouse/datasets) give conflicting CC BY-SA/GPLv3 statements; do not ingest it into a proprietary training line without written clarification. The [Allen CCF's research-use page](https://brain-map.org/support/tutorials/intro-to-the-adult-mouse-atlas) invokes [Allen's terms](https://alleninstitute.org/legal/terms-of-use), which permit noncommercial research and require written permission for defined commercial uses. A proprietary internal research tool is not automatically a commercial use under those terms, but any sale/commercial service or commercial distribution needs a rights review. No alternative volume was downloaded in this preflight, and none replaces validation on physical oblique sections.

## Training-image reality

The current inherited checkpoint saw the earlier v1 synthetic stream and near-coronal real TRAIN images; it has not learned the v2 or v3 artifact generators. The complete Allen real TRAIN reserve is 263,754 images/1,885 donors, but the pose labels are weak automated affines and the angles overwhelmingly near-coronal. A separate physical sagittal sample broadens appearance and one orthogonal family, not steep-oblique ground truth. The full-sphere synthetic stream uses one atlas and 64 synthetic deformation bases with virtual global variants; those are not independent biological brains. More presentations of those bases cannot alone establish physical all-angle generalization.

The v1/v2 streams already include contrast/gamma/inversion, uneven illumination, texture/noise, smooth warping, missing tissue, tear or fragment displacement, folds, bubbles, stitching seams, and backgrounds/optional brush. The v2 generator additionally labels invalid overlap/occlusion. This is broad *coverage*, not measured realism. Stain/scanner-specific texture, resolution and thickness variation, dust, motion or focus patterns, large detached pieces, orientation-dependent cutting damage, and actual animal shape variability are not yet validated against representative physical slides. The [512-donor real-versus-synthetic appearance audit](REAL_SYNTHETIC_APPEARANCE_GAP_20261009.md) exposed a concrete black/no-brush imbalance; the [v3 TRAIN mixture](SLIDE_V3_APPEARANCE_CENSUS_001_RESULT_20261009.md) improves it without suppressing all non-black or optional-brush cases. It is not a trained improvement.

The artifact generator's event names also overstate what has been established. Its displaced fragment is a synthetic elliptical piece cut from one plane, its fold is a narrow mirrored band, its bubble is a smooth lens-and-blur patch, and its stitching error is a shifted/defocused half-image. Those are useful geometric stressors, **not validated models of real tear edges, overlapping fragments, coverslip bubbles, or microscope mosaics**. The currently available Allen images are an important acquired-image source, but their automated alignment labels and mostly coronal sampling cannot tell us whether those simulated defects occur at the right frequencies, scales, or anatomical locations in the target laboratory. [AMBIA's physical-section study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10406728/) explicitly separated uneven staining/shrinkage from severe tears, missing parts and folds, and found greater expert disagreement in damaged cases; this supports evaluating severity separately, not claiming that a list of synthetic event types proves realism.

There is an additional orientation-specific image-source risk: [the Allen CCF construction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8152789/) used 100-µm through-plane sampling before 10-µm interpolation, so generating a steep-oblique fine-texture image from it is not equivalent to physically acquiring one. [STAM](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09211-8) addresses this particular atlas-fidelity problem with isotropic Nissl imaging and trained its different-angle pose network from two registered PI/DAPI whole-brain volumes, but that narrow modality/source set does not prove transfer to our slides and its non-commercial data terms preclude silently adopting its pixels in this proprietary model.

A [six-plane grid from the actual v3 generator](I:/AnatomyTracker/reports/one_shot_slide_artifacts_v3_qc_20261009_001/qc_grid.png) was viewed once for visual QC. It compares the artifacted input, the unwarped atlas render at its true plane, and the valid/damaged-pixel mask; these are **not model predictions**. The six cases are distinct physical draws selected to show different event types, not an artifact-prevalence sample. Gross shape and defects are visible, but the anatomical texture still looks atlas-derived and sometimes conspicuously smooth or stylized; this reinforces that code-level artifact coverage is no substitute for physical-slide realism. The grid SHA-256 `bf8a5fdd162be0397ad9b01854d38a436b1d4ce73adab5fa0ba7abb2bd2b77a8` matched its receipt.

A separate [16-image acquired TRAIN montage](I:/AnatomyTracker/reports/acquired_train_input_montage_001/montage.png) illustrates why the appearance problem is wider than background color: its eight Allen coronal and eight Allen sagittal sections differ markedly in within-tissue texture, staining intensity and framing, and one sagittal image contains a visible dark rectangular acquisition region. This is illustrative, not a prevalence estimate or an image-matched comparison. The current generator's independent gamma/inversion/noise draws do not establish that it reproduces these joint modality and acquisition patterns.

## Decision gate, not a forced compromise

1. Continue all-angle synthetic training/diagnostics with independent planes, realistic everyday appearance plus separately retained artifact stress. Train the observable cross-candidate evidence needed to tell a good plane from a warped-but-wrong plane; the existing scalar HOG/warp-cost cross-fit did **not** improve the frozen selector and should not simply be tuned longer.
2. Obtain physically acquired steep-oblique sections with recorded specimen/experiment/animal identity and trustworthy 3-D pose/landmark supervision independent of the model (for example, blockface/3-D specimen geometry plus expert review). Reserve entire animals prospectively for calibration and final testing. Synthetic oblique cuts and weak Allen affines cannot substitute. Measure pose, surviving-tissue map, region/electrode-site error, and failure/abstention separately by angle, tissue visibility, and artifact severity. Do not touch the public DeepSlice benchmark until the internal model is promising.
3. If well-supported physical heavy-oblique images fail while principal-family/heavily tilted images succeed, offer an optional coarse family/direction input or a multi-section mode and report the validated operating range. If only low-information images fail, retain unrestricted cutting for informative sections and make low-confidence cases abstain. Restrict cutting as a **last, evidence-based** fallback, not because the problem sounds hard.

No present result licenses a calibrated “90% in TRN” statement, GUI model replacement, a claim of DeepSlice superiority, or an all-angles guarantee. Numerical probability claims require held-out-animal calibration and region/trajectory propagation after geometric accuracy is adequate.

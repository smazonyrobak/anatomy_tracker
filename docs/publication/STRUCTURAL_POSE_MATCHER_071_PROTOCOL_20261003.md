# 071 scratch structural pose matcher: frozen development protocol

The fixed-score and blind-search experiments 067–070 found that the physical candidate family contains good fits, but both generic image scores and the old learned matcher commonly rank wrong poses above them. The next isolated question is whether a matcher explicitly trained on **wrong full-pose candidates** can learn a useful image–atlas agreement signal. This is an experimental component, not a replacement model or a biological validation.

Use the 64 independent synthetic TRAIN deformation bases only for gradient updates. Draw a new random plane and one appearance/artifact realization per example; do not deliberately regenerate the same section with another background. Render eight candidate atlas slabs per image, including the observed affine pose, difficult normal/translation/roll/scale/reflection perturbations, and another batch image's pose. Randomize candidate order. Train the randomly initialized pair network against physical corner/centre error, with no external pretrained features, pseudolabels, or real-animal labels. The atlas rendering integrates the synthetic section's finite-thickness PSF.

For checkpoint selection, use eligible sections 16–23 of each of the eight frozen, synthetic-development identities in `one_shot_fresh_synthetic_dev_panel_001`. These identities are separate from all 64 TRAIN maps. They are **not biological animals** and their labels are synthetic truth; this is a development gate only. Choose the checkpoint with lowest mean selected physical error across this fixed panel, while recording exact-black, imperfect-brush, and raw modes separately. The 32 sections used by 067–070 are not used for checkpoint selection. Save at 500-batch intervals through 3,000 batches, then audit hashes, provenance, curves, and selected physical errors before any continuation.

Advance this metric to a separate blind-search experiment only if the best checkpoint selects under 0.5 mm mean error overall, under 0.8 mm for raw inputs, and beats the random-order expectation by at least 50%. A successful candidate-bank score still would not establish a deployable model: full arbitrary-plane capture, deformation, real-animal generalization, uncertainty calibration, GUI integration, and fair final benchmarking remain open. No public benchmark or held-out final-test animal is accessed in this phase.

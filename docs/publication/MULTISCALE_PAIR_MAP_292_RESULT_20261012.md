# 292: learned multiscale matching moves intact tissue incorrectly

The frozen 2,400-section TRAIN-inner exact-pose pilot **failed** its predeclared advance gate. Do not scale or promote this map, expose it in the GUI, or infer blind localization or calibrated probabilities from it. The newly learned descriptor pyramid could reduce error on displaced fragments, but its ungated correspondence flow damaged the much larger intact-tissue region. This is evidence against this *particular matching/output rule*, not against arbitrary-plane geometry or learned correspondence in general.

The frozen 289 control, evaluated on this same new panel, gained **+0.017470 mm** overall; 292 is **0.266982 mm worse** by the common baseline. Zero of eight synthetic deformation bases had positive overall gain. At batches 0/400/1,200/2,400 the whole-panel mapped errors were 0.931383/0.439838/0.394341/0.389031 mm: optimization helped but never approached the 0.139519-mm rigid baseline. The corresponding displaced-fragment gains were −0.340162/+0.086916/+0.082544/+0.071747 mm. The 275 first-choice pose readout remained identically 2.881558 mm at every batch, as required for this map-only mechanism stage.

The 1.5-mm in-plane output cap does not explain most of the failure: only 0.000523 of valid panel pixels and 0.007813 of sampled fragment pixels have true in-plane displacement above that cap; the true through-plane residual exceeds 250 µm on only 0.000062 of valid pixels. A separate read-only coordinate reconstruction check recovered the panel's CCF displacement from the supervised reflected-raster flow with <0.001 µm maximum residual, so a gross reflection/basis inversion error in target construction is unlikely. It does not prove that the descriptor or optimizer is correct. At the selected batch, 237/32,768 mapped all-sites fell below atlas support 0.5; they were counted in endpoint error, not silently discarded. The 289 control had 1,138 such sites, so unsupported endpoints alone do not account for 292's large error.

One plausible mechanism is that every pixel receives the cost-volume's soft expected offset, even when the evidence for moving it is weak. There is no learned, initialized-near-zero choice to retain the rigid location. The present readout does not establish that this is the sole cause; the next bounded comparison should explicitly test a learned move-versus-stay gate on a *new* frozen inner panel, while keeping the 292 matcher, pose lineage, and source distribution fixed. The gate should be trained only on fresh TRAIN synthetic sections, and must improve intact tissue without forfeiting fragment recovery. Continuing the same ungated 292 run is not justified by these curves.

This was one atlas with 64 virtual deformation bases, not 64 biological test animals. All 2,400 training physical-section IDs were unique and disjoint from the 64-panel IDs and prior 286/287/289 IDs. The panel had eight new random plane draws per synthetic base 56–63; there were no deliberately paired copies with only background changes. The full-angle geometry remains supported, but the acquired steep-oblique/generalization question is unresolved. The acquired DEV/CAL/FINAL animals and public DeepSlice benchmark were not used.

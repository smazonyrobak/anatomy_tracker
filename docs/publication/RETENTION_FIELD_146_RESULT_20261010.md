# Field 146: mapping retained at exact pose, but near-pose and feature gates failed

The matched 2,000-batch continuation **failed** its frozen synthetic development gate. Both arms retained the 143 parent's exact-plane point mapping, but both lost accuracy when given a plausible pose up to 1.5 mm from truth. Treatment's additional anatomy-feature contrast produced only a small v4 gain over the otherwise identical control and no v3 gain. Neither endpoint is suitable for pose feedback, the GUI, uncertainty calibration, or a public benchmark.

Both arms started from the same scratch-trained 143 field, optimizer and scaler; both kept its exact/near/wrong curriculum, correspondence and fit-ranking losses, and reduced learning rate to 5e-5. Treatment alone added known-CCF dense feature contrast on a separately drawn high-overlap plane. Crucially, this nearby candidate was **not** forced to have worse fit energy: a plausible local warp can sometimes correct a different rigid plane. The run accepted 6,000 distinct synthetic TRAIN sections from 6,573 attempts (4,000 v4, 2,000 v3); no same-section background variants were deliberately paired. The evaluation reused the 145 development pair bank and 491 eligible sections from eight synthetic deformation plans, not independent biological animals.

The prespecified treatment mapping guard allowed at most two percentage points loss against the parent on each exact and near subset. Exact mapping passed; v4 near lost 3.15 points and v3 near lost 2.40. The required v4 feature gain over control was five points; observed gain was 2.72. These are weak *known-correspondence* features, not a truth-blind plane selector. The control's near regression shows the ordinary continuation itself was not retaining the useful near-pose behaviour; contrastive treatment did not fix it. Reduced learning rate and the restored 143 curriculum avoided the catastrophic exact-plane collapse in 145, but that alone does not solve the central joint-learning task.

Do not extend 146 unchanged. The next decision is to compare the already-trained direct one-shot map head on these identical exact and near sections before investing in another atlas-conditioned field. A successful direct baseline would favour training one coherent pose-plus-map system and deriving fitting-quality feedback from its **composed point map**, with controls that distinguish atlas anatomy from support or pose priors. If direct mapping also fails near pose, the architecture must address that capture failure before fitting-derived gradients reach the pose head. Synthetic labels alone cannot establish physical arbitrary-angle performance or calibrated electrode-region probabilities.

# Positive fitted-plane feedback 012: frozen development result (2 October 2026)

The randomly initialized one-shot lineage continued from the frozen 011 checkpoint for 8,000 three-image batches: 16,000 distinct accepted arbitrary-plane synthetic physical sections plus 8,000 distinct real TRAIN sections from all 1,885 TRAIN donors. Another 1,574 low-support synthetic draws were rejected by the unchanged eligibility rule. The three appearance modes were sampled independently (5,339 raw, 5,336 exact-black, 5,325 imperfect-brush), not as paired copies of a fixed plane. All scheduled real identities were distinct and disjoint from 143,159 identities in the prior schedules. The one exact synthetic plane was appended as a **training-only positive** to the eight image-prior candidates; no exact plane is inserted at inference. The new fit loss backpropagates through actual predicted poses, but its first-batch nonzero gradient check establishes connectivity only, not that the gradient points toward the true pose.

The independent evaluator scored **all nine frozen checkpoints** on the same 185 eligible arbitrary-plane synthetic DEV sections from eight synthetic deformation plans and 64 separate weak-affine Allen DEV images from six donors. Values below are identity-equal means in millimetres; the synthetic metric is visible-tissue 3D placement after the predicted local map, while the real metric is five-point disagreement with upstream Allen affines. These are not comparable targets.

Batch 6,000 reduces fitted-choice synthetic error by only **0.060 mm** versus the parent, while its all-32 proposal oracle becomes **0.052 mm worse**. Relative to the image prior at the same batch, the fitted selector helps by 0.150 mm, but that image prior has deteriorated by 0.096 mm from the parent. Paired individual sections improve in 100/185 and worsen in 85/185; five of eight synthetic plans improve and three worsen (two materially). Median fitted error falls 2.805→2.578 mm, the 90th percentile 5.665→4.998 mm, and the count above 5 mm 23→19, but the maximum **worsens** 12.190→12.356 mm. The better weak-real fitted mean (0.622→0.549 mm) is against non-expert, mostly near-coronal affines and improves five of six donors. None of these results qualifies arbitrary-plane anatomical accuracy or electrode-site probabilities.

The last 1,000 training batches average 2.433 mm image-prior tissue error and 2.374 mm fitted error, with 0.106 mm at the supplied correct plane. That in-run advantage does not translate into a material matched DEV gain. All 8,000 pre-clipping gradient norms exceed the fixed 5.0 clip (median 438, 90th percentile 1,185; maximum 98,152); this is an optimization diagnostic, **not** by itself proof of a bug because adaptive optimization changes the interpretation of norm scale. The held-out correct-pose mapper remains good, so the remaining dominant failure is global candidate coverage and reliable selection, especially for partial slices.

**Decision:** retain batch 6,000 only as the least-bad frozen development candidate for a focused next diagnostic, not as a qualified replacement. Do not promote 012 to the GUI, calibrate its scores, run the public DeepSlice benchmark, or repeat its training unchanged. Before another long run, test whether its fitted score gives a *useful local physical pose direction* around actual predicted branches. Then expand/refine global pose candidates with atlas-conditioned, physically supervised updates if that direction is useful; if not, improve coarse cross-modal structural matching rather than adding freer deformation. Preserve the direct geometry anchor and monitor best-of-*k* coverage separately from selection. The accompanying literature review is `ONE_SHOT_FAILURE_LITERATURE_REVIEW_20261002.md`.

## Subsequent targeted diagnoses

On the same 185 eligible synthetic sections, a local 19-way perturbation of the fitted-selected pose (translations ±0.5 mm, rotations ±0.12 rad, small scale/shear changes) improved physical mapped error only 2.896→2.871 mm when selected by the learned fitted score, although a physical-error oracle could reach 2.686 mm. The mean within-case rank correlation between score and *negative physical error* was 0.323; it was approximately zero for the 24 sections with <5% visible tissue. Thus the fitted score contains some local signal on well-supported sections but is not a reliable corrective direction on the difficult cases. These results are frozen at `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_positive_fit_feedback_012_local_direction_diag`.

An independent coverage diagnostic expanded each of 32 pose/reflection predictions to 1,120 single-axis perturbations per section, then selected by *unavailable ground-truth physical error*. The all-32 oracle decreased from 1.125 to 0.778 mm; only 49/185 sections fell below 0.5 mm, and <5%-tissue sections still averaged 1.308 mm. This is an optimistic search ceiling, not an inference result. It shows that modest local search alone does not solve global localization. Frozen rows are at `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_positive_fit_feedback_012_proposal_coverage`.

One exploratory calculation on the same synthetic DEV set fitted a plane to *exact* 16×16 CCF coordinate labels using the *oracle* tissue mask. Mean visible-tissue error was 0.121 mm for the pooled deformed coordinates and 0.140 mm for the synthetic rigid pose field. An earlier 1.17 mm calculation was invalid because it gave missing cells a 0.01 minimum weight; correcting that weight resolved the discrepancy. The calculation establishes a useful upper bound for spatial-coordinate supervision, not that a network can predict those labels from images or that an inferred tissue-validity head will match the oracle mask. Its code is `training/diagnose_dense_coordinate_oracle_plane_013.py`.

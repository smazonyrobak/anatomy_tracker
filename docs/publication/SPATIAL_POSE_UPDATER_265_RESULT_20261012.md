# 265: blind spatial image–atlas pose updater fails the development gate

The preregistered 265 pilot completed. Its 6,144-state bank covered 64 unoriented normals, three centre offsets, two raster-span assignments, eight rolls and two reflections. A shared 86,770-parameter spatial pair network scored the full coarse bank, selected 32 candidates, and twice corrected each pose after an exact finite-thickness atlas rerender. The frozen 260 checkpoint supplied only an image-predicted centre; **this is not the requested standalone joint pose/deformation model**. A separately trained, identically initialized control saw atlas support but no atlas intensity.

Training presented 3,000 unique eligible physical synthetic sections from all 64 TRAIN deformation bases, with one independently drawn appearance per section. The 48-section checkpoint-selection panel contains six fresh sections from each of the *same eight synthetic DEV plans* as the older 248 panel; its physical-section IDs are disjoint, but it is not a new-animal validation set. Batch 2,000 minimized selected rigid error on that fresh panel for both arms. Full-intensity selected error at batches 1,000/2,000/3,000 was **3.953/3.941/4.236 mm**, versus the unchanged 260 **2.661 mm**. Thus more of this same training did not show a useful development trend.

On the exact frozen 227 eligible sections of the 248 panel, with equal weight to each of eight plans:

| Model, batch 2,000 | Image-selected valid-tissue rigid error | Unoriented normal error | Best corrected top-32 rigid error | Top-32 in residual box |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Frozen 260 direct pose | 2.755 mm | 54.16° | — | — |
| 265 full atlas intensity | **4.449 mm** | 50.96° | 2.497 mm | 44.35% |
| 265 trained support-only control | 4.596 mm | 52.63° | 2.528 mm | 44.87% |

The full-atlas arm worsened selected rigid error in **194/227** sections and improved **0/8** plans against 260. Its 0.147-mm advantage over the trained support-only control is below the preregistered 0.25-mm anatomy-specific threshold; it won 130/227 paired cases and six plans, but that small difference cannot rescue the absolute failure. Every one of the seven advancement conditions failed. No checkpoint is promoted.

The failure is not simply empty-atlas exploitation: zero selected candidates had <1% initial atlas support. The more important gaps are **capture and selection**. The full 6,144-state bank's nearest *finite-raster five-point* error averaged 4.391 mm; the image-ranked top 32's best initial five-point error was 7.130 mm. Their best corrected five-point error was 5.442 mm, while the selected candidate was 10.468 mm. The best corrected top-32 valid-tissue pose beat 260 in 145/227 individual sections, but the model failed to choose it reliably. The earlier 264 coverage pass measured an optimistic normal/centre/**sorted-span** correction box, not this finite-raster physical error or blind ranking. Visible-tissue fraction <10% was especially poor (6.048-mm selected rigid error across 27 sections), so low-information cuts need explicit uncertainty/abstention rather than a forced confident plane.

This does **not** establish that arbitrary-plane localization is impossible. It falsifies this particular small spatial-pair/bank/updater recipe and shows that scaling it unchanged is unjustified. Next, test actual physical correspondence/capture, including raster-axis order, roll, crop and translation, before another long training run. A more principled candidate is learned 2D-to-3D anatomical correspondences with robust multi-match pose fitting and a constrained local deformation cost, not another pooled or scalar similarity reranker. [SLIV-Reg](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18683) demonstrates blind single-slice 2D/3D feature matching on CT/MRI, but not mouse histology. [Athalye et al.](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2026/paper/6039_paper.pdf) show why single-fragment histology registration is underconstrained and stage affine localization before bounded nonrigid deformation; their same-brain MRI and mold prior do not transfer directly. [DeepSlice](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-41645-4) is a coronal comparator, not evidence that this full-sphere pilot is competitive. Preserve the option of acquisition priors and abstention for inherently ambiguous fragments.

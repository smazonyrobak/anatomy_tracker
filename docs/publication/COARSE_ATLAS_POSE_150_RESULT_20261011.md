# 150 contextual atlas matcher: fresh-plan synthetic DEV result

**The prespecified gate failed. Do not promote this checkpoint to joint training or the GUI.** This experiment tested whether a scratch-trained 2D/3D atlas matcher can improve a candidate cutting plane using image anatomy, beyond the tissue outline alone. It did not train a joint pose/deformation loop, validate biological oblique sections, calibrate uncertainty, or test DeepSlice.

The source and protocol were frozen at `594e1e3` before training and evaluation. The full-intensity and atlas-intensity-zeroed support-only arms started from identical random weights and saw the same 18,000 accepted independent TRAIN presentations (12,000 v4 exposure/artifact draws; 6,000 v3), from 19,812 attempts over 60 TRAIN deformation plans. Four other TRAIN plans supplied 16 fixed inner checkpoint cases. Step 6,000 minimized the prespecified full-arm inner exact-plus-near score. The frozen 132 pose model supplied DEV candidate beams only; its weights/features never trained the 150 matcher. A logging-key collision overwrote the top-level `appearance` string in TRAIN `draws.jsonl` with its nested appearance dictionary; the v3/v4 schedule remains reconstructable from `slot` and the v4 provenance branch, and the independent audit confirmed its 12,000/6,000 counts. It does not affect images or training.

The fresh evaluation panel contained 256 independently drawn physical sections on eight new synthetic DEV deformation plans, disjoint from TRAIN plans. Of these, 243 passed the **pre-frozen minimal** visible-support eligibility rule; 13 did not. Inputs were independently sampled as raw (168), exact-black (44), or imperfect-brush (44), each with one geometry/background/artifact realization rather than paired recolorings. The normal's angle from the nearest cardinal plane covered <15° (30), 15–30° (84), 30–45° (107), and ≥45° (35). These are synthetic orientations, not evidence of accuracy on physical steep-oblique cuts. Some eligible sections still contain very little visible anatomy, as the frozen [example grid](I:/AnatomyTracker/reports/coarse_atlas_pose_150_fresh_dev_examples/target_input_proposed_corrected.png) illustrates.

The initially-near branch is the **truth-best** candidate in the frozen 132 beam, restricted to ≤1.5 mm initial error. This is an oracle diagnostic, not a branch selectable by the deployed model. The score is mean Euclidean CCF displacement of the corrected versus true rigid plane over valid native-256 tissue pixels, averaged equally over the eight synthetic plans. The 150 full arm gained only **0.063 mm** against the required ≥0.300 mm; its advantage over support-only was **0.056 mm** against ≥0.150 mm. It worsened 28/181 cases by >0.10 mm (15.5%, within the ≤30% limit). From the exact true plane, full 150 introduced 0.232 mm error (within the ≤0.250 mm limit). Hence two of four gate criteria failed. The visual/metric gain over frozen 148 is real but too small to justify joint-loop training on this matcher.

Blind candidate selection remains a separate failure: on all 243 eligible sections, the 132 top-prior candidate moved from 2.993 to 2.943 mm after 150 correction; only 79/243 top-prior candidates began within 1.5 mm. There was no post-fit branch reranking. The inner TRAIN-plan near score improved from 1.010 to 0.466 mm by step 6,000, but the fresh DEV result did not transfer at that magnitude. This is a synthetic TRAIN-family-to-fresh-plan generalization gap, not a claim that any one cause has been isolated.

Next, diagnose the domain gap and the availability of informative anatomy by tissue extent/appearance/angle and by TRAIN-versus-DEV generator geometry before choosing a narrow intervention. Do not extend unchanged 150 training. An arbitrary plane that clips only a tiny, nonspecific fragment may be impossible to identify from the image alone; any eventual model must represent that ambiguity and use optional acquisition constraints rather than confidently inventing a precise pose. This result does not establish that all arbitrary brain-intersecting planes are identifiable, nor that the final architecture is impossible on informative sections. No public benchmark or final-animal set was touched.

Posthoc read-only stratification of the frozen rows narrows the next question; it does **not** revise the failed gate. Among the 181 near branches, gain was 0.093 mm on 117 raw inputs, 0.007 mm on 31 exact-black inputs, and 0.017 mm on 33 imperfect-brush inputs. The gain remained small even when ≥10,000 native pixels survived (raw 0.085, black 0.004, brush 0.034 mm). Low tissue area by itself did not explain failure: 17 cases with <8% valid pixels gained 0.120 mm, versus 0.059 mm on the other 164. On the fresh panel, 68/181 near branches had a reflection mismatch; gains were similarly weak with and without that mismatch (0.059/0.068 mm). Per-query key reachability, correspondence error and fit weights were not stored, so these stratifications cannot yet assign causality to matching versus weighting.

The strongest measured TRAIN/DEV shift is the *joint acquisition geometry and candidate process*, not marginal brightness or cutting angle. Of 12,000 accepted v4 TRAIN draws, 82.7% were forced through an atlas-support point and 50.2% used a TRAIN-acquisition field of view; all 243 eligible fresh DEV draws used box-uniform offsets and whole-box framing. The DEV median frame edges were 17.78/16.88 mm versus 14.63/15.41 mm in TRAIN, and median valid pixels 11,671 versus 15,365. Only 1,032/12,000 TRAIN-v4 draws (8.6%) had the DEV-style uniform-offset plus box-framing recipe; their <5,000-pixel rate was 21.2%, close to DEV's 20.6%. Exposure, input-mode mix, normal-angle and thickness medians were close. Inner checkpoint near candidates were hand-perturbed true planes with true reflection, unlike natural blind beams. A bounded next experiment should use TRAIN-only DEV-style geometry and candidate errors that include reflection ambiguity, without importing the frozen 132 model's weights, features or pseudolabels into training; use a new development panel for any new gate. First use a non-selective synthetic-truth oracle on the current panel to isolate correspondence/weighting ceilings. More unchanged updates on the existing sampler are not justified.

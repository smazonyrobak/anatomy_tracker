# 185/186: acquired coronal labels are usable as weak supervision; current pose model does not transfer

The [Lupori et al. source](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7419283) archive was downloaded on I: and independently matched its declared 48,652,184,187 bytes and MD5 `c8c57aa74182207db67aa0b8072be3a7`. Its seven mouse IDs were split by the frozen `training/lupori_animal_split_185.json` *before section inspection*: TRAIN `AL1A, CC2B, CC4B, CC4A`; development `CC1B`; calibration `CC3A`; final test `CC1A`. All results here use TRAIN animals only. No held-out section image, registration or field was opened; no public benchmark or prior external model was used.

There are 241 VisuAlign-registered TRAIN sections and matching thumbnails, masks and X/Y displacement CSVs. Source `info.xml` marks 240 valid; exclude `AL1A_052_B3_1`. The target is `ABA_Mouse_CCFv3_2017_25um.cutlas` with source XYZ dimensions `[456,528,320]`. Existing `arbitrary_plane_geometry.py::quicknii_to_allen_points` converts source `(ML,AP,DV)` to bound atlas `(AP,DV,ML)` by reversing AP and DV. This is not an optional image flip: applying the wrong directions produced grossly wrong atlas anatomy. An eight-way axis-direction sweep against TRAIN tissue masks favored AP+DV reversal in every animal; median mask/atlas-support Dice with the project's boundary-coordinate convention was 0.9293, 0.9186, 0.8849 and 0.8666 respectively. ML sign could not be resolved from near-bilateral support (median differences <0.001); the existing QuickNII contract keeps ML unchanged. The unwarped atlas plane overlays visibly match major structures after the correction, but mask Dice does not verify every internal region or 2017-to-bound atlas identity.

Source markers independently check local field units and direction: CSV `D(image_xy)=reference_xy−image_xy` in **thumbnail pixels**, sampled on a 0.2-resolution grid. Median per-section marker RMS discrepancy is 0.56 px (95th percentile 1.32 px); reversing the sign gives median 102 px. Upsample the CSV *grid*, not its displacement magnitudes. Ten valid sections have >0.1% fold area within tissue (worst 1.38%); 3 have marker RMS >2 px. Such local regions need exclusions or robust loss. VisuAlign is a human 2-D planar warp, not a measured 3-D deformation or independent electrode-site truth. All acquired plane normals are within 11.12° of coronal (median 4.24°); this source cannot validate arbitrary-angle slicing. Section numbering can increase or decrease along AP depending on animal, so use O/U/V rather than filename order for position.

The frozen random-initialized project-lineage 128 and 132 direct models were then scored blindly on the same 240 TRAIN images, with four input/checkpoint combinations and no adaptation. `raw_max_rgb` uses `max(red,green)/255`; `tissue_p99_max_rgb` divides that by the per-image 99th percentile. Median five-point CCF errors (mm) are:

| Checkpoint | Input | First choice | Truth-best of prior top 16 | Truth-best of all 160 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 128 step 6000 | raw | 7.329 | 4.060 | 3.202 |
| 128 step 6000 | p99 | 7.039 | 4.395 | 3.281 |
| 132 step 2000 | raw | 7.560 | 4.093 | 3.225 |
| 132 step 2000 | p99 | 7.233 | 4.502 | 3.257 |

For 132/p99 only 8/240 TRAIN sections have an all-160 candidate ≤1.5 mm from this weak human reference; 1/240 first choices meet it. The 52-section prespecified inner-guard animal `AL1A` has baseline median first-choice 7.198 mm, all-160 oracle 2.999 mm, and 3/52 all-160 candidates ≤1.5 mm. This is a severe acquired-fluorescence proposal/domain gap; a fitter or ranker cannot repair a missing nearby candidate. These are TRAIN-domain diagnostics, not animal-held-out performance. The next bounded experiment should adapt the existing final-family pose heads with acquired fluorescence while rehearsing fresh arbitrary-plane synthetic sections, then evaluate on an animal excluded from that adaptation *before* using official DEV `CC1B`. Only after candidate capture improves should joint fitting feedback or dense-map supervision be scaled.

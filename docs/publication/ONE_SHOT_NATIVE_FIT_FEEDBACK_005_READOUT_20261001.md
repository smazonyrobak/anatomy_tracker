# Matched native fit-feedback continuation (1 October 2026)

The frozen batch-8,000 candidate-energy model was continued in two arms from the **same** model weights and with byte-identical draws: a direct physical-pose-loss control and a fit arm that also differentiated the existing frozen atlas-match head through finite-thickness rendering of two **actual predicted** pose/reflection branches. Only the pose head was trainable in either arm; the encoder, atlas matcher, local mapper and uncertainty layers stayed frozen. This tests whether that scalar feedback improves pose capture. It does **not** test a newly trained joint pose/deformation network or whether a flexible warp genuinely needed little correction.

Each arm ran 8,000 three-image batches: 16,000 accepted independent synthetic physical-plane draws, 1,643 rejected low-support draws and 8,000 distinct real TRAIN sections. The control and fit draw logs have the same SHA-256 `90dd1596a5cfc1b38c8421132a6395f68460fadea520bff2d45d511dda62eb22`. The fit coefficient was fixed from the first TRAIN batch at 0.116747, making its initial final-pose-layer gradient 20% of the direct-loss gradient. No pretrained external model, final-test animal or public benchmark was used.

The independent frozen evaluator scored both arms at batches 0, 4,000 and 8,000 on the same 90 eligible synthetic arbitrary-plane development sections (four synthetic deformation identities) and 64 Allen images from six disjoint real development donors. Values below are equal-identity means. Synthetic error is mean Euclidean mismatch at visible tissue pixels, **not** the five canvas corners. Real error is against existing weak Allen affine labels, **not** blinded expert truth.

The fit arm beats its matched control by 0.101 mm on synthetic tissue at batch 4,000, but only 37/90 sections improve and two of four synthetic identities worsen. At batch 8,000 the fit arm is **0.031 mm worse** than control, with 44/90 sections improving and two identities worsening. Neither fit checkpoint beats the unmodified start on synthetic tissue. The final fit checkpoint improves the six-donor *weak* real-affine mean by 0.022 mm against control, a small result that cannot rescue the anatomy result. At the shared start, the selected synthetic median is 2.95 mm, 90th percentile 5.28 mm, and only 24/90 sections are within 1 mm; the final fit checkpoint is likewise 2.95 mm median, 6.06 mm 90th percentile, 24/90 within 1 mm, and 14/90 worse than 5 mm. This is categorically unsuitable for region-level electrode claims.

The oracle choice among the same 32 model branches is about 1.14–1.16 mm, so branch **selection** remains a major bottleneck; even that reference-best oracle is not a deployable prediction and is still not near-perfect. The historical 8–11 mm *canvas-corner* measure exaggerates error outside tissue, but the 3 mm visible-tissue and 35–40° plane-normal errors are independently serious. The synthetic panel has only four underlying synthetic identities and the real labels are weak; neither supports a calibrated biological accuracy claim.

Post-exit audit independently verified the pinned trainer/model/stream, parent checkpoint and real-schedule hashes; 8,000 training rows per arm; 16,000 unique accepted physical-section IDs and 8,000 unique real section IDs; identical 25,643-row arm draw logs; all six checkpoint hashes; development panel, real records, evaluator source, 1,152 raw rows and summary hashes. Both processes completed without stderr. Output trees: `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_native_fit_feedback_005` and `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_native_fit_feedback_005_development_eval`.

**Decision:** Reject this frozen scalar-fit-gradient continuation as a solution. A nonzero local energy gradient and a transient 0.1 mm matched difference did not improve the best physical-pose checkpoint. Moreover, the fit head was trained to regress physical distance from rendered-image features; minimizing that learned scalar is not itself a measurement of how much tissue deformation was required. The next architecture experiment should give a shared atlas-conditioned updater the image–render mismatch **and** the proposed local displacement/warp cost, directly supervise its pose correction on fresh synthetic physical planes, and compare a matched direct-only arm. Keep deformation bounded and score physical tissue placement, pose angle, catastrophic tails and weak-real donor retention. Only if that evidence improves should the mapper and pose predictor be jointly fine-tuned; probability calibration and DeepSlice remain later gates.

This decision is consistent with [Xiong et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fninf.2018.00093), who localize the histological plane before nonrigid registration and describe the danger of deforming a wrong match into apparent similarity. [SynthMorph](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2021.3116879) supports contrast-diverse synthetic geometry supervision in brain registration, but is MRI rather than mouse histology. [SVoRT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10129054/) motivates learned residual slice-to-volume updates, but uses multiple fetal MRI slices rather than an arbitrary single histological section. [DeepSlice](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41645-4) used far more coronal training images and cautions against relying on a single image; none of its reported performance is a valid benchmark for this unfinished arbitrary-plane model.

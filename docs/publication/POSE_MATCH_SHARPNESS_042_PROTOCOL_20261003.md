# 042 pre-run mechanism test: do diffuse 3D matches spoil the fit?

041 improved 1.5-mm soft correspondence recall on the frozen synthetic development panel but did not improve candidate-best pose, whereas unavailable exact correspondences in 040 did. A likely mechanism is that a soft average over several anatomically distinct 3D atlas points is a biased target for the geometric fit. Test this without training or changing data.

Freeze the batch-1,500 041 head and 019 parent. For each parent's top eight physical plane candidates, use the same 041 query/key logits, confidence, regularized three-point-frame least-squares equations, and one 1.5-mm robust reweighting pass. Compare (a) the existing full-soft expectation, (b) a normalized expectation over the four largest match logits, and (c) the single largest-logit key. Evaluate both the raw fit and the *unchanged* 041 gate's blend with the prior. Do not search over top-k values, fit penalties, gate thresholds, or checkpoints after seeing results.

Use the same 177 eligible sections from eight disjoint synthetic development identities and 64 weak-real sections from six development donors, strictly for **mechanism diagnosis**, not qualification or model selection. Report top-one and oracle-best-eight physical errors, the 112 near-true cases, appearance strata, and worst weak-real donor regression. Confirm that an independently reimplemented full-soft fit reproduces 041 physical outputs within numerical tolerance. A sharper match is promising only if it reduces identity-equal synthetic best-eight error by at least 0.20 mm without raising top-one error by more than 0.20 mm; this is a mechanistic direction criterion, not a deployment gate. Weak-real inherited Allen affine is not oblique expert truth. No public benchmark or untouched final animals are accessed.

This design follows the explicit-match/robust-pose logic of [2D3D-MATR](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Li_2D3D-MATR_2D-3D_Matching_Transformer_for_Detection-Free_Registration_Between_Images_and_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf) and [SLIV-Reg](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18683); neither paper proves that hard matching is better for histological atlas sections.

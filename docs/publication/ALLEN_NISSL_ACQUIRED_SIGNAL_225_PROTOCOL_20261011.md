# 225 predeclared TRAIN-only Nissl-vs-template anatomy-signal check

Question: does the newly bound Allen Nissl intensity make the **same** atlas planes more discriminable against already prepared acquired sections than the current average template? This is a small data-source gate, not model training or a claim of correct human plane labels.

Use exactly one lower-middle section from each of the 58 coronal TRAIN donors and 40 sagittal TRAIN donors prepared in 180. Retain their existing weak Allen affine, source preprocessing, source hashes, section thickness, and donor IDs. For each, render the same nine planes with the same finite-thickness PSF: the recorded positive affine, offsets ±1.5/±3 mm along its normal, and ±15° rotations about its in-plane u/v axes. Render each plane from (a) the pinned average template and (b) pinned/normalized Nissl; both use **the identical annotation-derived support**. Evaluate absolute Pearson correlation after 3×3 smoothing on the pair's common 5×5-eroded atlas support. A pair is eligible only at ≥64 common cells, support IoU ≥0.45, and support-area gap ≤0.15; eligibility must match between arms. Do not use an observed-tissue mask or weak reference to choose the score. For Nissl only, also score a deterministic within-support-permuted intensity control.

Primary comparison: equal-donor fraction of eligible sagittal **tilt** pairs where the recorded plane outscores the perturbation. Promote Nissl to a matched training comparison only if it reaches ≥0.63, exceeds average-template tilt by ≥0.10, exceeds its permutation null by ≥0.10, and does not reduce the coronal all-pair or sagittal offset equal-donor fractions by more than 0.05. Require at least 25 donors with eligible pairs in each acquired cohort; otherwise call inconclusive. Preserve raw per-pair scores and eligibility, source/volume/protocol hashes, and a completion receipt. The previous 180 average-template result is a replay check, not a tuning set. No synthetic same-plane appearance copies enter training; no DEV/CAL/FINAL/public benchmark or external trained weights are used.

Regardless of this gate, these near-cardinal sections have weak inherited affines and cannot establish performance on physical steep-oblique cuts, accurate tissue deformation, calibrated uncertainty, or GUI readiness. A positive TRAIN signal only justifies a fresh animal-separated model continuation; a negative signal stops Nissl as a presumed remedy for atlas-image mismatch.

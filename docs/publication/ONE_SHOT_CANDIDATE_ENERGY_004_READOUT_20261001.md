# Predicted-candidate anatomical match and gradient direction (1 October 2026)

The standalone model's existing atlas-match head was continued from the frozen batch-40,000 sixteen-mode checkpoint for 10,000 batches of two newly drawn arbitrary-plane synthetic sections plus one distinct real TRAIN section. This added 20,000 accepted synthetic physical draws, 1,999 rejected low-support draws and 10,000 unique real TRAIN sections, with no overlap in the earlier real schedules. Pose and local mapping weights were frozen. The head saw the supplied true plane, two physically perturbed planes and four **actual model-predicted** pose/reflection branches per section; its loss separately ranked those predicted branches by synthetic visible-tissue placement or weak-real affine agreement. The head scores are not calibrated probabilities, and this warmup alone does **not** send fitting gradients into the pose predictor.

The frozen readout `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_candidate_energy_004_development_eval` evaluated all six saved checkpoints on the same 90 eligible arbitrary-plane synthetic development sections and 64 images from six disjoint Allen development donors. Identity-equal means are shown below. The reference-best oracle is unavailable to inference, and real targets are existing weak Allen affines rather than expert anatomical truth.

At batch 8,000, the match head improved 41/90 eligible synthetic sections and worsened 32; it improved three of four synthetic deformation identities, while the fourth worsened by 0.059 mm. All six real development donors improved against their weak affine references. Even the best synthetic head choice remains about **3.1 mm** from visible-tissue truth, versus 1.164 mm for reference-best selection from the same 32 branches. This is useful but nowhere near deployment accuracy. Increased rank correlation at batch 10,000 did not translate into better selected synthetic error, so rank correlation alone is not the checkpoint criterion.

The focused post-exit direction diagnostic `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_candidate_energy_004_direction` tested the batch-8,000 head from **actual predicted poses**. It differentiated match energy through atlas rendering in a physically scaled nine-parameter local pose tangent, then compared small steps down and up that energy against the saved synthetic tissue-to-CCF target. With the head-selected branch, the energy and true physical-error gradients had positive cosine on 64/90 sections. A half-scale step down improved physical placement on 61/90, versus 21/90 for the opposite step. But mean tissue error changed only from 3.116 to 3.093 mm (23 µm). From the image-prior branch, it improved 55/90 versus 26/90 for the opposite direction, with mean 3.576 → 3.551 mm. Thus fitting contains a measurable local pose signal from predicted starts, but the observed step does **not** solve global capture or justify claims of successful joint learning.

Both processes and their evaluators exited without stderr. Post-exit audits matched all six match-head checkpoint hashes, pinned training/model/stream and evaluation sources, panel and geometry-row hashes, output rows and summaries. The 10,000 training rows, accepted/rejected synthetic draws and disjoint real schedule were counted. All 12 development summary groups and all 270 direction rows were independently checked against raw results; the direction diagnostic's checkpoint, input, source, protocol, row and summary hashes match its completion receipt. Synthetic identities reuse one atlas and are not independent biological animals; no calibration, final-test or public benchmark was used.

Decision: retain batch 8,000 as the best synthetic match-head checkpoint for the next **matched joint-continuation experiment**, not as a final model. Compare the same fresh training draws with and without bounded fit-energy gradients into the pose predictor from actual predicted branches, retaining direct physical supervision and weak-real pose training. Require improvement on held-out visible-tissue placement without real-donor regression. If the fitting arm does not outperform its control, revise the match/update architecture rather than repeating the same loss or treating a nonzero gradient as success.

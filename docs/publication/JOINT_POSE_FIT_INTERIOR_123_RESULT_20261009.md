# 123 interior atlas-fit diagnostic: blind gate failed

The frozen, TRAIN-only diagnostic used 128 independently sampled synthetic sections and the 122 step-2000 model. It did not train or select a checkpoint. Of 69 support-matched blind comparisons with enough common tissue interior, the proposed high-pass image–atlas score preferred the physically better plane in **48/69 (69.6%)**, versus **52/69 (75.4%)** for the older raw local-correlation score on the same comparison pixels. The predeclared gate required at least 75% and a 10-percentage-point advantage over raw; it failed. The blind discordance was five raw-only wins against one high-pass-only win. There was no clear implementation or receipt error in an independent post-exit audit.

The atlas-zero controls had exactly zero paired margin. In a separate truth-centred translation control, high-pass preferred the correct pose in 53/53 scored comparisons and raw in 52/53. Thus atlas intensity carries a *local* signal when a correct pose is supplied, but the tested score does not reliably select among the model's blind corrected planes. The raw baseline had access to synthetic oracle validity inside its local windows, unlike the proposed deployable high-pass score; its numerical advantage is not a fair claim that raw is the better deployment scorer. Neither score has been validated on real histology. Also, the diagnostic's rigid-gauge best plane need not be the lowest true mapped-tissue error.

The 59 unscored blind sections comprised 38 without a candidate within 1.5 mm, 12 without a support-matched wrong candidate, and nine with under 256 shared eroded interior pixels. This limits the conditional result. The 69 scored sections had median 712 common pixels and mean mapped-support Dice 0.894. The high-pass deficit persisted across common-pixel, support-area-gap and strain strata; the result was not confined to an obvious single subgroup. A strained map remains a descriptive warning, not a validated ranking rule.

**Decision:** do not use this simple interior score to train direct pose probabilities or promote the 122 checkpoint. A next experiment must make spatially coherent internal-anatomy evidence discriminate realistic support-matched blind candidates, and explicitly demonstrate a beneficial learning connection to the direct pose predictor while retaining real-image performance. More correct-pose-only score checks would not resolve the blind selection gap.

# 321: one-pass joint plane and tissue map — no promotion

The randomly initialized C4-equivariant model predicts a distribution over arbitrary brain-intersecting planes and a local tissue-to-atlas map from one image. It uses 6.23 million parameters. Exact quarter-turn input equivariance passed the geometry check before training. This is a direct prediction model: it does **not** inspect the atlas plane it predicts or feed registration difficulty back into its plane estimate.

The bounded run completed 12,000 optimizer batches: 48,000 independently drawn synthetic cuts and 3,000 weakly labelled acquired-section presentations. The frozen, previously unseen synthetic development panel contained 128 inner, 32 steep-oblique, and 32 low-visibility cuts. A frozen 309 model was evaluated on these **same** cases. The AL1A acquired-section check uses approximate human atlas labels, not independently measured physical plane truth. All development draws came from TRAIN bases; DEV, CAL, FINAL, and public benchmarks remained unopened.

The model learned something: inner first-choice mapped error fell from 5.150 mm at random initialization to 3.720 mm after 1,000 batches, 2.882 mm after 4,000, and 2.574 mm after 12,000. But the final model failed six of seven predeclared acceptance clauses. Inner first-choice errors above 5 mm increased from 10/128 for 309 to 13/128 for 321. The local mapping changed mean inner error by only 0.00025 mm relative to its own rigid estimate; the true-plane local training loss was nearly flat (0.03153 to 0.03114 mm across the first and last 1,000 batches). More exposure with this unchanged objective is not the next experiment.

Candidate generation and candidate choice must be separated. On inner cuts, 321's best of 16 candidates improved over 309 (0.831 versus 0.981 mm mean oracle error), and its ≤1.5 mm candidate capture increased (119/128 versus 114/128). Yet its selected candidate was worse. This supports **blind candidate selection** as the leading inner/steep failure mode, not a lack of nearby proposals. Low-visibility cuts are different: best-of-16 oracle error worsened (2.592 versus 2.086 mm), so better ranking alone cannot repair that subset. None of these synthetic or weak-label results establishes real-animal accuracy.

The post-exit audit verified the frozen output hashes, source hashes, training/panel provenance, row-level score calculations, stage clauses, and final non-promotion decision. It also found a runner bug: `stage_gates` has string keys, but the continuation and `best.pt` selection checks use integer `step in stage_gates`. The gate values were calculated and recorded correctly, and the 1,000/4,000-batch clauses happened to pass, so the completed training length and scientific comparisons are unaffected. However, `best.pt` was overwritten by the failed 12,000-batch checkpoint and **must not** be considered an accepted model. Preserve the frozen run; correct this check in the next runner before any training.

Decision: do not scale this run, deploy this checkpoint, claim calibrated uncertainty, or run DeepSlice. Prior learned full-intensity, oriented-slab, exact-point, texture-reliability, and joint spatial-fit selectors achieved at most tiny selected-error gains or regressed despite winning many isolated candidate pairs (studies 125, 128, 299, 300, 304, and 305). Training another generic atlas score would repeat that failure. Instead, run one cheap **no-training information diagnostic**: on frozen C4 candidates, ask whether internal anatomical boundary geometry supplies reproducible spatial evidence beyond exterior support and a shuffled-boundary control, and whether any gain actually closes first-choice mean/tail error. Stop selector work if it does not. Physical oblique-plane ground truth and strictly held-out animals remain necessary before a biological accuracy claim, calibration, GUI replacement, or public benchmark.

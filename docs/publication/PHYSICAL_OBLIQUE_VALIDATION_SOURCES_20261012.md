# What would validate arbitrary-angle physical sections?

No public adult-mouse collection located in this targeted search combines **steep physically cut oblique histology** with an independently measured 3D cut plane. This is an evidence gap, not evidence that the task is impossible. Synthetic all-sphere tests cannot close it. The distinction matters because atlas-registration-derived positions are not independent truth for evaluating a new atlas-registration model.

- The [Brain Architecture Project mouse datasets](https://data.brainarchitecture.org/pages/mouse/datasets) and its [multimodal atlas paper](https://repository.cshl.edu/id/eprint/41879/1/10.21203.rs.3.rs-6387518.v1.pdf) offer real serial Nissl sections in cardinal directions and same-animal volumetric imaging for some specimens. The reconstructed section coordinates are valuable for realism and cross-modal checks, but are registration-derived and do not supply steep independently measured cuts.
- [Slice2Volume](https://rodare.hzdr.de/record/3131) contains nine individually identified irradiated mice with raw H&E/immunofluorescent sections, MRI/CT and registered transformations (CC BY 4.0; 1.7 TB total). Its published pipeline aligns light microscopy to CBCT using registration; those transformations therefore cannot serve as independent pose truth. A small animal-separated subset may still be useful for staining/background realism and held-out registration stress tests, not an arbitrary-oblique accuracy claim.
- [Transcriptome Tomography](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0045373) describes slightly oblique serial cuts and block-face imaging, but its public expression accession is not a ready set of steep histology images with measured 3D planes. The [Waxholm/Neuroterrain horizontal series](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroinformatics/articles/10.3389/neuro.11.003.2008/full) offers another real-plane realism check, but only one brain and a cardinal orientation; [distribution terms](https://www.nitrc.org/projects/incfwhsmouse/) require separate review before any proprietary use.
- [DeepSlice's study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10514056/) evaluates useful acquired images, but its slide-mounted reference is human alignment consensus, not an independent physical measurement of a steep cut. It remains a later fair benchmark for its supported task, not proof of arbitrary-angle competence.

The clean prospective test is to hold out whole animals, obtain same-animal pre-cut 3D MRI/CT, record cutting-frame angle and serial block-face/fiducial geometry, and have a blinded evaluator produce 3D plane and landmark coordinates **without** using the model's output or atlas-registration estimate as truth. Deliberately include coronal-, horizontal-, sagittal-dominant and steep-oblique cuts, small partial intersections, tears and displaced fragments. Report errors and abstentions by angle, visible area, artifact severity, stain/background and animal. Until then, make no quantitative claim that arbitrary physical cuts are solved, even if a synthetic gate passes.

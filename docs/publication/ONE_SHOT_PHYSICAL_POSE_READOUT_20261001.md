# One-shot visible-tissue pose continuation: frozen development result (1 October 2026)

`I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_joint_physical_pose_002` completed 8,000 four-image batches from the standalone random-initialized one-shot lineage: 24,000 newly accepted arbitrary-plane synthetic sections and 8,000 distinct real TRAIN sections from the reserved Allen collection. Another 2,436 synthetic draws failed the predeclared tissue-support gate. The real schedule repeats none of the preceding 20,000-section mixed run or the 4,000-section matched fit run. This stage replaced target-reflection-only ranking with joint 8-pose × 2-reflection physical supervision, weighting visible-tissue mapping 75% and full-canvas geometry 25% on synthetic images. The atlas-conditioned local mapper was retained, but its pose was **replaced with ground truth during mapping training**. Therefore this run did **not** implement fitting-to-pose feedback; its uncertainty outputs are uncalibrated.

The independent frozen evaluation `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_joint_physical_pose_002_development_eval` covers all five saved checkpoints, 128 one-appearance-per-physical-section images from four held-out synthetic deformation plans, and 64 real images from six separate Allen development donors. Ninety synthetic sections meet the saved tissue-support criterion; the other 38 remain recorded as unscored low-information cases. The synthetic plans share one Allen atlas and are not independent animals. Real references are existing weak Allen affines, not blinded expert alignments.

Synthetic selected error improved by only 0.269 mm while normal error worsened by 6.18° and the weak-real donor-equal error worsened by 0.120 mm. The training process and queued evaluator exited without stderr. Independent post-exit audit matched all five checkpoint hashes, input-panel and evaluation hashes, pinned source hashes, all 8,000 training rows, the 24,000 accepted/2,436 rejected synthetic draws, the 8,000 unique non-reused real sections, and every reported identity-equal mean against raw predictions. The final checkpoint SHA-256 is `cd089cdb4c3c5c9b9dc2155a476602fb02f5e29763b955c60cbfee709a3b72e8`; exact raw rows and source/configuration receipts are retained on `I:`.

Decision: preserve all checkpoints but do not promote this continuation as the GUI replacement, scale the same geometry-only objective unchanged, calibrate its scores, or use the public benchmark. The 8×2 training/inference mismatch was repaired, but candidate generation, selection and orientation remain inadequate. Next build and test the missing learning link: render the model's **predicted** candidate atlas sections, fit observed anatomy with a constrained local map, measure anatomical agreement together with deformation cost, and backpropagate useful fit evidence into the pose predictor while keeping physical supervision and real-donor retention. The fit signal must help from the actual current candidate-error range, not only from an oracle near-correct pose. If it cannot, revise the matching representation rather than adding more batches of this loss.

The targeted predicted-candidate test `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_native256_candidate_fit_001` now answers that question for the current raw atlas-fit score. On the same 90 eligible sections, it rendered and fitted all 16 predicted pose/reflection branches with the frozen batch-8,000 model. It used the **known synthetic valid-tissue mask**, an optimistic diagnostic unavailable at deployment. Selecting the lowest fitted atlas mismatch gave 4.187 mm identity-equal tissue error versus 4.242 mm from the image-only branch prior; the reference-best branch was 1.953 mm. Fit beat the prior on 40 sections, lost on 35 and tied on 15; the geometry-best branch ranked sixth of 16 by fit on average. Mean within-section fit-versus-geometry rank correlation was only 0.187. The exact true pose had a better fit score than every predicted branch on 88/90 sections, so the score recognizes a correct match **when supplied** but gives little useful ordering among predictions still millimetres away. No predicted branch was within 0.5 mm of reference tissue geometry; only 10/90 were within 1 mm. This is why directly adding this raw loss to the current predicted poses would be a weakly justified global-feedback step. The next model change should improve coarse candidate coverage and/or learn a more discriminative candidate-conditioned anatomical matching energy before relying on its gradients. The diagnostic exited cleanly; the row, protocol, source and summary SHA-256 values match its frozen completion receipt. This remains synthetic development, not external validation.

## Candidate-conditioned learned matching energy

`I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_candidate_fit_energy_001` completed 4,000 batches, showing 12,000 **new independently drawn synthetic physical sections** once each. Six rendered atlas candidates per section were the true plane, two perturbed true planes and three existing image-predicted branches; these were not multiple image copies with different artifacts. Only the randomly initialized candidate-match head was trained, with the pose and local mapper frozen, so fitting-to-pose learning is still **not implemented**. All candidate scores remain uncalibrated.

Its post-exit frozen readout `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_candidate_fit_energy_001_development_eval` evaluated all five saved head checkpoints on the same 90 eligible synthetic sections and 64 weak-label real development images. Selecting the lowest match energy **without** the pose prior gave:

| Head training batch | Synthetic visible-tissue error | Real weak-affine five-point error |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Untrained head | 4.958 mm | 11.910 mm |
| 1,000 | 4.454 mm | 1.192 mm |
| 2,000 | 4.451 mm | 1.419 mm |
| 3,000 | 4.464 mm | 2.429 mm |
| 4,000 | 4.430 mm | 3.065 mm |
| Unchanged image-only pose prior | **4.242 mm** | **1.126 mm** |

Means are equally weighted over four synthetic deformation plans or six real donors, not independent biological validation. The untrained-head 11.910 mm real figure is an **untrained selector choosing poor pose branches**, not the trained image-only pose error. At batch 4,000, only 7/64 real energy choices beat the prior, and 11 sections exceed 10 mm. The head's per-image candidate-energy versus placement-error rank correlation rises only to 0.210 synthetic and 0.158 real. Combining prior log mass and energy after the fact produced small, unstable development gains depending on the weight, not a defensible improvement; these development data must not be used to claim calibration or set a final-test weight.

The separate held-out synthetic check `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_candidate_energy_truth_001` inserted the **known exact plane** alongside the 16 predicted branches. At both batches 1,000 and 4,000 its energy was below every predicted branch on all 90 eligible sections (90/90); the mean exact-plane versus best-predicted energies were 0.464 versus 1.663 and 0.207 versus 1.374, respectively. This establishes that the head can recognize the exact match on synthetic images, but not that its gradient has a useful capture range, that it transfers to real histology, or that its numerical scores are probabilities. The main failure is upstream candidate coverage: even retrospective best-of-16 visible-tissue error is 1.953 mm, and the image-selected plane-normal error is 49.47°. The immediate architectural priority is therefore stronger **direct global pose prediction**, not stronger ranking of these same far-away proposals or unconstrained nonrigid warping.

An independent metric audit found no millimetre-scale units, indexing or coordinate-convention error. The 10.353 mm synthetic five-point metric measures four full-canvas corners plus center; **all four corners are outside visible tissue in all 90 scored sections**. The visible-tissue error of 4.242 mm is the more relevant current localization measure and is still unacceptably large. The true affine itself differs from the dense synthetic target by only 0.156 mm on average, so synthetic deformation does not explain away the model's error. On real weak labels the image-only prior has a 0.510 mm section median but a 1.126 mm donor-equal mean because three catastrophic sections are 9.392–14.155 mm; the weak Allen labels cannot adjudicate biological truth. All source/row/summary hashes and raw-row aggregates were checked after exit, and the 180 exact-plane diagnostic rows match its completion receipt.

The scale and capture-range decision is consistent with, but not proven by, published work: [DeepSlice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10514056/) trained on 131k Allen histology, 443k Allen connectivity sections and 0.9M synthetic **coronal** sections, and recommends multiple sections rather than one when possible. [DiffPose](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12505627/) trained its randomly initialized direct pose encoder on 1M synthetic views, then relied on atlas/rendering refinement because direct regression alone was imprecise and image-based refinement has a limited capture range. Neither result licenses claiming our more general arbitrary-plane, single-section problem is solved by a particular sample count. We will increase independent-plane training exposure while monitoring frozen synthetic and donor-separated real development performance, and only enable match-to-pose feedback once candidate quality and a local gradient check justify it. No public benchmark has been used.

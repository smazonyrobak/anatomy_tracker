# 293: gating can limit damage, but the matcher still lacks a useful offset signal

The frozen [post-hoc TRAIN-inner diagnostic](MOVE_GATE_CEILING_293_PROTOCOL_20261012.md) used the selected 292 checkpoint and its existing 64 synthetic exact-pose sections. An oracle gate that sees each site's true CCF target could reduce whole-panel error from 0.139519 mm rigid to 0.113113 mm, a **0.026405-mm ceiling gain** for gating only the 292 displacement directions. On displaced fragments the oracle improves 0.770790→0.611671 mm; on intact sites it improves 0.122886→0.102506 mm. This oracle is not an inference model and is optimistically evaluated on the same TRAIN-inner panel used to select 292. It still falls short of 292's originally predeclared 0.030-mm overall advance target. A gate alone is therefore not a sufficient next scaling argument.

The raw 292 move helps at only **14.06%** of all sampled sites (13.02% intact, 54.71% fragment). Its median all-site move is 0.283 mm, larger than the rigid all-site error of 0.140 mm. Fine-scale match probabilities are nearly flat: median maximum probability **0.0248** over 49 offsets, versus **0.0204** for a uniform posterior. The highest quarter of this peak-confidence score has *worse*, not better, mean raw-move benefit (−0.313 mm versus −0.190 mm in the lowest quarter). Thus simply thresholding the current match peak is not a credible move/stay rule.

The 292 training log independently shows mean offset-classification cross-entropy 4.452, 4.452 and 4.450 over batches 1–400, 401–1,200 and 1,201–2,400, close to the approximately 4.472-nat uniform average for its 169/81/49 candidate volumes. Its loss weighting makes classification a weak auxiliary relative to endpoint regression. A one-case exploratory gradient probe on an independently logged TRAIN fragment draw found 62 displaced pixels among 15,078 valid pixels; the weighted classification gradient norms into the source and atlas stems were only about 0.5% and 1.0% of their endpoint-gradient norms. This single case is diagnostic, not a population estimate. Together with the flat posterior and class imbalance, it motivates **stronger, displacement-balanced descriptor supervision**, not more identical 292 training. A learned gate initialized near “stay” should be tested alongside that correction on a new frozen panel so intact sites cannot be harmed at initialization.

# Scored top-K correspondence 142: anatomical key scoring still fails

The four-arm, randomly initialized matching pilot finished 1,200 updates (3,600 distinct synthetic physical-section presentations: 2,400 v4 and 1,200 v3). The first 600 updates trained correspondence; the second 600 added analytic plane fitting. An independent post-exit audit matched 30 training hashes and six evaluation hashes, checked every draw and update, and found 6,874 evaluation rows for the frozen 248 v4 and 243 v3 eligible DEV sections. The evaluator verified identical step-zero head tensors, the frozen 132 blind beam, and all parent/panel bindings. Training losses fell during the correspondence phase, but that is not a spatial-accuracy result.

On the **original truth-best blind branch within 1.5 mm** (an oracle diagnostic, not a deployable selection rule), the exact frozen v4 result was:

| Arm | Globally supported sites within 0.5 mm | Available in selected local keys | Correctly scored within 0.5 mm | Correctly scored / global |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Full atlas intensity, top one | 6,094 | 4,779 | 508 | 8.34% |
| Full atlas intensity, top four | 6,094 | 5,825 | 602 | 9.88% |
| Atlas support only, top one | 6,094 | 4,747 | 470 | 7.71% |
| Atlas support only, top four | 6,094 | 5,837 | 578 | 9.48% |

Top four made many more anatomically nearby keys available (95.59% versus 78.42% of the global denominator in the full-intensity arm), but the scorer found the correct neighborhood for only 602/6,094 sites. Its advantage over top one was **1.54 percentage points**, and over support-only top four just **0.39 points**, both below the predeclared 5-point requirements. The v3 pattern independently agreed on the bottleneck: full-intensity top four scored 725/6,961 (10.42%), whereas support-only top four scored 748/6,961 (10.75%). No angle stratum showed a reliable accurate matcher; among the v4 near branches, full-intensity top-four scored only 9.22–10.77% across the four nearest-cardinal-angle bins.

More available keys did not translate to a useful correction. In the 179 v4 near-branch sections, the unchanged original plane was within 0.5 mm in 25 cases; full-intensity top-four fitting reached that threshold in **12** (−7.26 percentage points, versus a required +10). Plan-equal mean error went from about 0.837 to **0.943 mm** (+0.106 mm, versus a required −0.20). For the prior-selected branch, mean error decreased slightly, from 3.278 to 3.260 mm, but 0.5-mm capture fell from 2.02% to 1.61%; v3 capture fell from 4.53% to 2.06%. Thus the predeclared selected-branch non-regression condition also failed. The v3 whole-beam 1.5-mm retention condition did **not** fail materially; this does not offset the other failures.

Flattening source intensity on the unchanged near candidate reduced v4 fine-hit rate by 9.53 points; zeroing atlas intensity reduced it by 5.12 points. These are distribution shifts through a head trained on full content, **not causal evidence** that atlas grayscale improves performance: the matched support-only arm nearly tied it, and fitted-plane accuracy worsened. The narrower fine target or 1,200-update duration may contribute to lower absolute key accuracy than the earlier 4,000-update head, but extending this exact architecture without a better anatomical signal is not supported by this result.

**Decision: the predeclared gate fails.** Do not widen the search further or feed this matcher’s fit score into the global pose predictor as if it measures anatomical correctness. The next change should improve cross-appearance anatomical descriptors and the realism/diversity of the source–atlas pairing, then verify precise key selection and good-versus-wrong-plane discrimination before joint fitting feedback. Preserve full arbitrary-plane support; poor accuracy occurred across angle bins, not uniquely at heavy obliques. Continue from this standalone random-init lineage, but do not promote 142 to the GUI, call its scores calibrated probabilities, or run the public DeepSlice benchmark.

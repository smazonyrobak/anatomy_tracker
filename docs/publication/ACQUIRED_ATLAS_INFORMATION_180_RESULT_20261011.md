# 180 TRAIN-only acquired-versus-synthetic atlas information: sagittal tilt remains unresolved

The frozen 180 assay completed at `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/acquired_atlas_information_180_train_001`. Its predeclared gate is **inconclusive**. It compares the recorded positive atlas plane with eight fixed wrong planes using one unchanged, model-free image score; it is not a localization model, animal-level validation, or a public benchmark.

One section from each of 58 coronal TRAIN donors and 40 sagittal TRAIN donors was compared with 128 independently drawn eligible full-sphere synthetic TRAIN sections (two independent planes per 64 deformation bases). The acquired positives are upstream **weak Allen affines**, not expert-confirmed physical locations. All acquired source slices are approximately cardinal, so this assay cannot establish real arbitrary-angle performance. The cohorts also have different source preprocessing: coronal raw red/255 at 96 pixels versus sagittal inverted-red fixed gain downsampled from 256 pixels. Absolute correlation handles polarity and amplitude, not every domain mismatch.

The score is absolute Pearson correlation after fixed 3×3 smoothing on the common eroded atlas support, with normalized nine-sample finite-thickness rendering. Wrong planes are normal offsets ±1.5/±3.0 mm and tilts ±15° about each in-plane axis. Pairs with insufficient common support or mismatched support area were excluded by the frozen rule. The control independently permutes each atlas candidate's intensities within its support. The minimum group counts passed (64 synthetic bases, 58 coronal donors, 40 sagittal donors). Synthetic exact planes and acquired coronal weak references strongly separate these eight wrong planes. Sagittal weak references have some offset information, but the tilt comparison is at or below the permuted control. Thus neither the predeclared both-acquired-success condition nor the both-acquired-gap condition passed. This **does not show that arbitrary cutting angles are impossible**. It also does not show that sagittal weak affine labels are wrong: reference tilt bias, sagittal image preprocessing/appearance, and score sensitivity remain unresolved. Directional tilt wins are asymmetric (31/40 for +15° about u versus 16/40 for −15°), a reason to investigate the weak-reference/appearance pair, not proof of which is at fault.

The 178 learned spatial verifier also failed on synthetic material despite the strong exact-plane signal here. Therefore acquired appearance mismatch alone cannot explain its blind-selection failure. This assay tests broad fixed perturbations around a known positive; it does not demonstrate a useful score basin around *imperfect predicted proposals*. Another million presentations or promoting the current atlas verifier is not supported by these results. A small targeted comparison should distinguish sagittal weak-reference error from scoring/appearance and test capture of plausible-but-imperfect proposals before redesigning or scaling the joint model. Keep arbitrary-plane output support and abstention for unsupported predictions; do not weaken the scientific target to cardinal planes on this evidence.

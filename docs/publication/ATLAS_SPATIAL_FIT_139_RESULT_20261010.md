# 139: blind-beam spatial atlas fit did not supply useful anatomical feedback

The predeclared 139 mechanism gate **failed**. Keep the 132 parent; do not scale the 139 head unchanged, feed its score into the direct pose predictor, or present it as a qualified GUI model. The frozen panel is synthetic development material reused for design decisions, and the acquired references below are inherited weak Allen alignments, not expert truth.

The experiment froze the random-initialized 132 one-shot model and trained two identically initialized 2-D/3-D correspondence heads on the same 2,000 updates and 6,000 independently drawn physical synthetic sections. All candidates came from the parent's actual blind 16-branch beam. A near branch was available on 4,990/6,000 presentations; a support-matched wrong branch on 4,795/6,000. Both heads saw identical source images, geometry, atlas support and draw order. Only the treatment saw atlas intensity; the control received zero atlas intensity. Each head predicted matches/dustbins, fitted a plane and added a learned branch-score residual. The source, protocol and checkpoints were frozen before evaluation.

The small treatment mean change (0.018 mm) does not meet the predeclared 0.30-mm requirement, and its selected capture is one section worse than the parent. None of eight v4 deformation plans improved in selected ≤1.5-mm capture. On the older 243-section v3 panel, the treatment changed blind-16 ≤1.5-mm capture from 197 to 201 sections and selected capture from 74 to 75, but support-only was comparable. The donor-separated acquired coronal and sagittal weak-affine guard passed the 0.20-mm-per-donor non-regression threshold; this does not validate anatomical accuracy or all-angle physical histology.

The failure is mechanistically informative. Of 171 v4 sections with an initially near branch and support-matched wrong branch, the frozen prior plus learned score preferred the near branch on 138 (80.7%) for **both** heads. Fit score alone did so on only 54 (31.6%) for atlas intensity and 65 (38.0%) for support only. Source-image/feature swap and within-support atlas-intensity shuffle still preferred the near branch on the same 138/171 cases. Their median absolute score-margin changes were only 0.0145 and 0.0076, against a 1.661 median intact near/wrong margin. Across v4, the atlas-intensity head changed the parent's selected branch in only 6/248 sections. Its median absolute learned score residual was 0.0165 logit, versus a 6.08 median span of frozen prior logits over the beam. Mean absolute per-branch physical error movement was 0.075 mm, far below the roughly millimetre-scale precision gap. Thus the apparently decent near/wrong prior win rate is not evidence that atlas-image anatomy drove selection.

The training objectives did improve modestly: the full head's first-to-last 200-update mean total loss fell from 3.503 to 3.254, and coarse-match loss from 4.311 to 3.635; support-only total loss fell from 3.498 to 3.264. The treatment's small coarse-loss advantage did not translate into physical selection or fitting gains. Fixed-grid dustbin precision was about 0.90 on the v4 scored pairs, but most queried tissue lacked a supported key within the 1.5-mm label radius for a given candidate, so dustbin accuracy by itself is not a pose result. The matched controls and ablations make further unchanged rank-head training a poor use of compute. They do **not** prove that arbitrary-plane alignment or anatomical feedback is impossible.

The next intervention must address the demonstrated bottleneck directly: the frozen 132 blind beam already contains a ≤1.5-mm branch for 179/248 v4 sections, yet first choice captures only 73. A branch selector needs a signal that changes with *anatomical agreement*, at realistic source/atlas appearance, and must be trained at sufficient resolution to alter the pose itself. The present 139 descriptors and aggregate nine-number score did not provide that signal. Do not infer that a larger template catalogue or deformation penalty alone will solve it. Continue to support all brain-intersecting planes and distinguish genuine ambiguity from a correctable ranking failure; acquired steep-oblique, animal-held-out expert labels remain essential before claiming physical all-angle performance. The existing TRAIN-only appearance audit also found a substantial raw-background mismatch, and the v6 local-strain QC found stylized artifacts; do not scale synthetic exposure without addressing those limits.

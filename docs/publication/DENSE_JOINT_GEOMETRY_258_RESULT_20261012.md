# 258: spatial map improves coordinate error but still collapses pose geometry

The fresh-random correspondence-first model completed 5,000 batches of four independently sampled eligible full-sphere v7 TRAIN sections: **20,000 distinct physical-section IDs** in 20,000 accepted presentations. It predicted a 96×96 observed-pixel-to-CCF coordinate field and a correspondence-validity map. A differentiable weighted plane fit of that same field produced the global pose; the field residual about the plane is the proposed local deformation representation. No prior weights, weak real labels, atlas scorer, pretrained features, CAL/FINAL animals or public benchmark were used.

The frozen 248 synthetic DEV readout used all 227 eligible sections from eight identity-disjoint deformation plans at all three trained checkpoints. Means give each plan equal weight:

| Training batch | Fitted-plane rigid error | Plane-normal axis error | Dense valid-pixel error | Validity Dice | Minor FOV ≤1.2 mm |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1,000 | 3.983 mm | 55.00° | 3.864 mm | .760 | 227/227 |
| 3,000 | 3.635 mm | 55.06° | 3.644 mm | .882 | 227/227 |
| 5,000 | **3.135 mm** | **54.22°** | **3.193 mm** | **.903** | **57/227** |

The batch-5,000 fitted coordinate error is lower than 247's 4.247 mm, 254's 4.410 mm and 256's 4.773 mm on the same panel, and it improves on 256 in all eight plans. But this is **not an accurate plane**. The axis error remains near the 57.3° expectation for an unoriented random axis. The predicted minor physical field of view has median **3.01 mm**, versus **13.24 mm** for the true planes; 199/227 predictions are less than half their own true minor span. None of the 227 fitted planes is within 1 mm rigid error, and only 15 are within 2 mm. This repeats the shrinkage loophole: making a small plane near the brain centre can lower mean pixel-coordinate error while failing to identify section angle and extent. The direct map itself is still 3.193 mm off.

The prespecified continuation gate required fitted error ≤3.8 mm, dense error ≤3.0 mm and improvement over 256 in at least six plans. The first and third passed, but the dense-error gate failed. **Do not scale this unchanged loss or promote this checkpoint.** Training did show nonzero learning: first- versus last-500-batch dense error fell 3.891→3.198 mm and validity Dice rose .398→.903. Yet 98.4% of training batches hit the global gradient clip, and the geometric orientation remains unlearned. These are observations, not proof that clipping caused the failure or that arbitrary planes are unidentifiable.

The next targeted change should supervise the *fitted physical edge vectors* (O/U/V, centre, orientation and physical span), not just mean tissue-coordinate distance, while retaining direct dense correspondence and validity learning. This addresses the observed collapse and supplies pose-factor gradients through the same spatial map. A staged continuation or fresh matched run must check both field accuracy and realistic span/axis on the untouched synthetic DEV panel before increasing independent training exposure. This is analogous to the global corner-vector target in [DeepSlice](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-41645-4) and the dense-correspondence-to-pose coupling in [DSAC](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/html/Brachmann_DSAC_-_Differentiable_CVPR_2017_paper.html), but neither work proves the proposed arbitrary-plane model will succeed.

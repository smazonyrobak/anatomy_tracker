# Atlas evidence diagnostic 107 — reused synthetic DEV

The frozen 106 image–atlas matcher does recognize the **exact** cutting plane in many new synthetic sections, but its scores barely order the 14 planes actually proposed by the model. This explains why extending the same local updater is not the next priority: the candidate search is too far from the narrow region where the matcher supplies reliable anatomical evidence.

On the same 64-section reused DEV panel, the exact target plane was added **only as a diagnostic 15th candidate**. No weights changed and it was never selectable. With predicted tissue weighting, its zero-displacement image–atlas logit exceeded the direct model's prior-selected plane in 58/64 sections and every blind candidate in 50/64. With the known synthetic valid-tissue mask, those counts rose to 60/64 and 56/64. Atlas-support fraction alone did not distinguish the exact plane on the true-tissue mask: it was constant across candidates. Thus the exact-plane signal is visual, not merely silhouette support.

That signal is too sharply localized for the present beam. Mean within-section rank correlation between zero-displacement logit and *lower physical pose error* was only 0.024 among the 14 blind candidates (0.018 with the known mask); the corresponding 27-nearby-bin probability-mass log score reached just 0.051 (0.040 with the known mask). Peak-over-all-offset logits also failed to favor the exact plane over all blind candidates (1/64). The average physically best blind plane remains ~2.0 mm from truth. A broad-offset maximum can match unrelated structures and does not identify which candidate needs a plausible small correction. These exploratory measurements do not establish real-slide transfer or any calibrated probability.

The generation command processed all 64 sections and wrote 960 raw rows, then failed while serializing the summary because the true-mask support score had an undefined rank correlation: it was constant in every section. The separate summarizer treated this as `null`, verified all 64 groups and 15 slot indices, and completed without re-running GPU matching. Next intervention: improve the **global one-pass plane proposal** and its anatomical candidate scoring, with acquired-image retention and explicit withheld synthetic confirmation. Use a coarse-to-fine score with a basin large enough to bridge current millimetre-scale errors; train on correct, nearby and hard-wrong rendered planes, then measure physical error rather than logit loss alone.

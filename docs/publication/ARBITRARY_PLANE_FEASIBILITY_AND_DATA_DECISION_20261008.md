# Arbitrary-plane feasibility and synthetic-data decision

**Decision:** keep the model's cutting-plane domain unrestricted. Do not assert that a single image always identifies one exact plane, and do not impose a coronal/sagittal/horizontal family filter. For an ambiguous image, the deliverable must preserve multiple plausible planes, disclose uncertainty, and accept optional rough cut orientation or another section. Restricting permitted cuts is a last resort to be decided from physically acquired, animal-separated evidence, not from the current synthetic failure.

## What the present evidence separates

The active *training* generator samples each plane normal across the full antipodal sphere with independent geometry and appearance. It does not deliberately repeat a plane merely to change its background. That is the right *geometric domain*, but not a realistic acquisition model. Its folds are bright ridges without overlapping tissue; bubbles are intensity rings without optical distortion; tile seams change intensity without moving tissue; tear fragments have only a narrow, one-sided displacement; there is no explicit defocus; local resampling border padding can copy edge tissue. All synthetic virtual animals are deformations of one Allen template, and half the TRAIN frame choices borrow near-coronal acquisition framing even for oblique cuts. A separate TRAIN-only v2 artifact generator now moves and masks fragments, overlaps folds, lenses/blurs bubbles, shifts seams, and samples black-to-gray exteriors while preserving observed-pixel-to-CCF labels. Its deliberately high artifact-event rates are *not calibrated* to acquired slides, so it is not yet in training. Acquired TRAIN images and real donor virtual reslices are useful domain checks, but the latter are weakly aligned, interpolated virtual obliques—not physical steep-oblique histology.

One TRAIN-only acquired-image comparison already found a specific appearance mismatch: the 256 unsegmented Allen images had 43.4% exactly zero canvas pixels on average, while the earlier synthetic absent-brush rows had only 2.5%; their black synthetic cases generally signaled an available outline. This is not proof that lab slides have the same distribution, but it justifies independently drawing raw/no-brush black or near-black exteriors as well as gray ones, without manufacturing paired copies of a section. See `ALLEN_REAL_TRAINING_INPUTS_20260929.md` for exact cohorts and limitations.

Missing artifact realism is **not a complete explanation** for the current synthetic localization error. On the identity-separated synthetic development panel, the score-selected candidate had 2.580 mm mean mapped-tissue error and 31.26° normal error, whereas the physically best of the *same fourteen predicted candidates* had 0.832 mm and 13.16° (diagnostic oracle, not deployable). The gap persisted across AP-, DV-, and ML-nearest normals and grew at larger obliquity. The 102A contextual matcher improved its own predecessor but still harmed good fitted planes and failed to beat simply leaving the fitted plane unshifted. More examples from the unchanged simulator, or another ungated displacement/scalar scorer, do not directly repair candidate choice. The earlier million-synthetic-presentation run also worsened agreement with weak real-donor alignment labels. These readouts do not prove that full-angle localization is impossible; they show the present model is not ready.

A newly frozen physical-sagittal check is more decisive about the *current model*, not the task's feasibility: on 80 sections from four donors, the scratch-trained 019 pose head disagreed with Allen's weak automated alignment by 63.77° in plane normal and 8.695 mm at five points, donor-equal. Its best predicted branch was still 35.02° away. A single fixed gain estimated on separate sagittal TRAIN images improved the selected result to 52.35°/6.021 mm but missed the predeclared joint improvement gate. Those labels are weak, yet the magnitude and all-donor pattern establish that the current image-only model does not transfer even to this *cardinal* real orientation. A separate 56-donor sagittal metadata expansion was split before downloading new images: 40 TRAIN donors, eight weak-development donors, and eight sealed weak holdout donors. A bounded same-model pose continuation with TRAIN-only sagittal and coronal images is planned before judging whether real-orientation coverage can repair the failure; no held-out or public benchmark is being tuned against.

## Physical limit and operating target

A small tangential sliver, a mostly missing section, or a low-contrast section lacking distinctive structures can be compatible with several 3-D planes. In that case no image-only algorithm can guarantee a unique correct answer; increasing model size cannot create absent information. Even [DeepSlice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10514056/) recommends batches of sections over a single image and reports difficulty when landmarks are obscured. [AMBIA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10406728/) supports tilted atlas extraction but reports that severe artifacts and fine internal registration remain difficult. [QuickNII](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6541252/) shows that adjacent sections and operator anchors can supply missing geometric context. None of these establishes a universal full-sphere, single-section accuracy limit.

The viable target is therefore **all-angle support with conditional reliability**: recover an accurate plane and tissue map when the section contains enough information; retain a multimodal posterior when it does not; use optional rough cut direction, slice range, or a neighboring section to narrow the posterior; abstain from precise electrode-region probabilities until uncertainty is calibrated on unseen animals. A broad cut-family hint may help as a prior, but the 103 true-family hard-filter diagnostic worsened the present synthetic selection, so a mandatory family classifier is not supported.

## Minimum decision test before changing the product promise

1. Revise the generator's physical artifacts and labeling, inspect a small genuine-generator QC grid, then compare old/new training at matched exposure on an independently frozen synthetic panel; measure plane normal/offset and *mapped visible-tissue* errors, not only training loss.
2. Obtain physically acquired cardinal and deliberately steep-oblique sections from multiple held-out animals, with independent pose and landmark references. Virtual obliques and Allen weak affines cannot certify the full-angle claim. Report angular and mapped-site errors by angle, visible-tissue fraction, and artifact severity, plus posterior coverage and error among accepted versus abstained cases.
3. Compare image-only operation with optional rough-family/slice-range/neighboring-section information on those same held-out animals. A restriction to broad cut families is justified only if full-angle operation remains unreliable despite adequate acquired data and those hints produce a clear, repeatable gain without excluding heavily oblique cuts.

DeepSlice is a later comparator on the shared coronal subset, not a substitute for the full-angle test or a dataset to tune against now. No numerical electrode-region probability, GUI replacement, or claim of reliable arbitrary-angle operation follows from the current synthetic or virtual-reslice results.

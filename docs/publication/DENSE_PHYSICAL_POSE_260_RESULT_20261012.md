# 260: physical-span supervision helps coordinates, not arbitrary-plane orientation

The predeclared 260 continuation completed 3,000 batches of four independently drawn eligible full-sphere synthetic TRAIN sections. The 12,000 accepted physical-section IDs are unique and disjoint from the 20,000 used by its 258 parent. It continued only the project's own fresh-random weights and optimizer; no external weights/features/pseudolabels, weak real labels, CAL/FINAL animals or public benchmark were used. A prior read-only gauge preflight measured a 0.145-mm centre and 0.51° normal-axis discrepancy between the pre-artifact physical target and a visible-tissue fit on the frozen DEV panel; that mismatch was small relative to the parent error.

The evaluator used the exact same frozen 227 eligible v7 synthetic DEV sections from eight identity-disjoint deformation plans at new batches 1,000 and 3,000. Each plan receives equal weight in the means below. The readout selects batch 3,000 by the prespecified lowest fitted rigid error; the FOV is the minor singular value of the physical in-plane edge matrix, not the different raster-axis span logged during TRAIN.

| Checkpoint | Fitted rigid error | Dense valid-pixel error | Normal-axis error | Median predicted/true minor FOV | Predicted FOV < half truth |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Parent 258 batch 5,000 | 3.135 mm | 3.193 mm | 54.22° | 3.01/13.24 mm | 199/227 |
| 260 batch 1,000 | 3.073 mm | 3.208 mm | 54.27° | 4.25/13.24 mm | 153/227 |
| 260 batch 3,000 | **2.755 mm** | **2.935 mm** | **54.16°** | **7.07/13.24 mm** | **102/227** |

The selected checkpoint improves rigid error over 258 in all eight synthetic plans. But only 2/227 sections achieve ≤1-mm fitted rigid error, 36/227 ≤2 mm, and 147/227 have normal-axis error above 45°. The axis remains near the 57.3° mean for an undirected random axis, and the TRAIN normal-axis cost is essentially flat from first to last 500 batches (0.498→0.496). The model has learned some centre/field/scale information, **not** dependable all-angle orientation. The first-to-last-500 TRAIN dense loss falls 3.196→2.916 mm and mean raster minor span rises 7.23→8.56 mm, but all 3,000 batches hit the gradient clip. This is an observation, not proof clipping caused the orientation failure.

The four prespecified continuation conditions were selected fitted rigid ≤3.2 mm, dense ≤3.25 mm, normal axis ≤49°, and median minor physical FOV ≥6 mm. The first, second and fourth passed; **the normal-axis gate failed**. Do not scale this unchanged direct pixel-to-CCF regression or promote the checkpoint. More exposure or a larger CNN cannot be justified from this result as a remedy for near-random normals. The next step is a targeted blind pose-hypothesis/atlas-evidence diagnosis: determine whether the image encoder's predicted normals collapse to a few directions and whether a full-sphere renderer-based candidate mechanism can find near-true hypotheses on untouched synthetic DEV sections. Preserve multiple hypotheses/abstention for ambiguous slivers, then jointly predict local residual deformation, rather than forcing a single averaged coordinate map.

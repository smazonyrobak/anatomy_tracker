# 233: second registered atlas contrast did not improve the primary pose endpoint

The [frozen protocol](KIMLAB_LSFM_SOURCE_ABLATION_233_PROTOCOL_20261011.md) followed the [232 actual-generator preview](KIMLAB_LSFM_APPEARANCE_PREVIEW_232_RESULT_20261011.md). One 1,200-batch, TRAIN-only intervention continued the same randomly initialized 221 step-400 parent as the completed 226 control. Both arms used the same seed, optimizer schedule, 3,600 accepted synthetic physical sections, Lupori TRAIN sections and older weak-reference TRAIN sections. The 233 arm changed only the atlas intensity used to render the first two framed synthetic slots: 1,210 accepted sections used the registered KimLab LSFM average and 1,190 used the Allen average. Its third synthetic slot and all real slots were unchanged. The LSFM average is **not** an additional animal or an independently measured cutting plane.

The 233 runner exited normally after 391.6 seconds. An independent post-exit audit found **zero identity/order mismatches across all 6,120 control/intervention draw records**; the evaluator's stricter full-record comparison, excluding only source-specific appearance fields, also passed. Step-zero model tensors were identical. Both arms made 3,720 synthetic attempts, accepted 3,600 distinct physical-section IDs, and excluded AL1A from gradients. Four intervention checkpoint, configuration and draw-file hashes matched its receipt; all five readout-output hashes and all 1,440 readout rows matched its receipt. The evaluator also checked the parent, both arms' checkpoint/source/protocol hashes, frozen 226 input hashes and source-binding hashes before scoring.

Median fitted-final rigid error is in millimetres. Each synthetic panel contains 64 independently drawn TRAIN-inner physical sections; the AL1A guard contains 52 sections with an approximate human VisuAlign affine, **not** physical pose truth. Fit rendering used the same Allen CCF intensity atlas in both arms, including for the LSFM-input panel, as deployment cannot assume the input modality is known.

**Decision: fail the predeclared gate at every checkpoint; do not extend or promote this source-mix recipe.** On the required original panel, median fitted error regressed by 0.244, 0.116 and 0.276 mm after 400, 800 and 1,200 batches, respectively, instead of improving by at least 0.30 mm. At 1,200 batches the original-panel fitted local-map median also worsened from 3.546 to 3.912 mm, and fitted normal angle worsened by 2.69°, exceeding the 2° guard. The alternate-source and weak-real gains do not override these losses. This experiment tests contrast replacement during pose-head/shared-feature training, not a newly trained joint fitter; it neither resolves the pose/deformation feedback bottleneck nor shows that arbitrary physical cuts are impossible.

The six-image preview exposed very dim absolute inputs and one small surviving fragment. Such sections may be intrinsically ambiguous and ultimately require an honest broad/multimodal uncertainty output, not forced confident single-plane predictions. The current outcome still lacks independent physically oblique animal truth, site-level accuracy, calibrated probabilities, and safe GUI replacement. Keep DEV/CAL/FINAL animals and the public DeepSlice benchmark sealed; the next design must address blind candidate localization and genuine anatomical fit evidence rather than increase exposure of this same source mix.

# 190: the raw atlas-fit score cannot select the right acquired slice plane

The [predeclared TRAIN-only test](LUPORI_ACQUIRED_BLIND_FIT_RANKING_190_PROTOCOL_20261011.md) completed normally on the same 64 physical Lupori TRAIN sections and the exact frozen 188 top-16 proposals as 189. It made no weight updates. The independent post-exit verifier checked all raw rows, 64 source-image hashes, original 189 candidate identities/errors, output/source/protocol/checkpoint hashes and the gate arithmetic. No DEV/CAL/FINAL animal or public benchmark was read.

Among **51** sections with both a proposal ≤1.5 mm and one ≥3 mm from the approximate human plane, the near plane won on identical support-matched image pixels in **32/51 (62.7%)** using real atlas intensities. The within-support permutation control won **25/51 (49.0%)**; the original direct pose prior preferred the near plane in **39/51 (76.5%)**. Real-intensity near wins by TRAIN animal were 11/15, 10/15, 6/11 and 5/10. This misses the frozen 70% pair gate, despite a weak anatomy-specific advantage over the permutation null.

More decisively, blindly choosing the highest raw fit score over 16 proposals increased equal-TRAIN-animal mean five-site error from **1.652 mm** (original pose prior) to **3.088 mm**. All four animals worsened; no section lacked a score. This fails the separate blind-selection gate by a wide margin. The raw score uses each candidate's own atlas-support mask and can be biased by which parts of the image that plane covers. No post-hoc weight or threshold is tuned to rescue it.

The 189 finding remains valid but narrow: the atlas image often points in the right *local direction after capture*. It does **not** yield a reliable cross-candidate measure of fitting difficulty. Feeding this 190 score back into pose training would risk degrading the model. The next targeted step is to train and cross-check a spatial, cross-modality candidate correspondence readout on TRAIN animals, with held-out TRAIN-animal and support-only controls, while keeping the 188 pose and map weights frozen. Only after blind selection improves should constrained deformation cost and a matched fitting-feedback gradient be introduced. Physical steep-oblique sections and independent site truth remain missing.

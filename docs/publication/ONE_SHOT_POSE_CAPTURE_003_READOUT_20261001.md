# Sixteen-mode arbitrary-plane pose capture: frozen development result (1 October 2026)

`I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_pose_capture_003` completed 40,000 four-image batches in the standalone random-initialized lineage: 120,000 newly accepted independent synthetic plane draws, 12,319 rejected low-support draws, and 40,000 distinct real TRAIN sections that do not repeat the previous schedules. The run used 16 pose modes × 2 reflections and stronger physical/antipodal-normal supervision. The atlas-conditioned local mapper still received the **known true pose** during mapping training. Anatomical fitting therefore did **not** teach pose in this stage; uncertainty outputs remain uncalibrated.

The independent frozen evaluation `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_pose_capture_003_development_eval` scored all nine saved checkpoints on 128 synthetic sections from four held-out deformation plans (90 support-eligible) and 64 real images from six separate Allen development donors. Synthetic plans share one atlas and are not independent biological animals. Real references are existing weak Allen affines, not blinded expert anatomical registrations. Equal-identity means follow; the five-point canvas metric is not an anatomical-landmark error because its corners lie outside tissue.

The reference-selected oracle is unavailable at inference. More exposure improved candidate coverage but selected synthetic error plateaued near 3.55–3.57 mm after batch 15,000. At batch 40,000, 48/90 sections had a candidate within 1 mm of reference visible-tissue coordinates, but only 16/90 actually selected such a candidate. Reflection was correct on 51/90 sections. The full-canvas five-point figure was 8.454 mm and must remain a frame diagnostic, not be called tissue or electrode error. The 35,000-to-40,000 difference in selected synthetic error is only 0.021 mm, while the final checkpoint has better oracle coverage and weak-real retention; no checkpoint is deployable.

The separate frozen predicted-candidate diagnostic `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_pose_capture_003_fit_diagnostic` used that batch-40,000 model to render and fit **all 32 actual predicted branches** on the same 90 eligible synthetic sections. It used the known synthetic tissue mask, which is an optimistic diagnostic unavailable at deployment. Selecting the smallest raw atlas mismatch reduced equal-identity visible-tissue error from 3.573 to 3.387 mm; selecting the frozen learned match energy gave 3.404 mm. The reference-best branch was 1.164 mm. Raw fit beat the image prior on 39 sections and lost on 33; the learned energy beat it on 37 and lost on 34. The learned-energy gain was concentrated in one deformation identity and it worsened two of four identities. The geometry-best candidate ranked only 6.92 of 32 by raw fit on average; within-image fit/error rank correlations were 0.240 raw and 0.303 learned. Inserting the exact true pose gave a better raw fit than every predicted branch on 85/90 sections. This remains inadequate global ranking even though more candidate poses are now close to the reference.

The training and queued evaluator exited without stderr. Post-exit checks matched the hashes of all nine checkpoints, the evaluation source, raw rows, summary, panel manifest, real schedule, and pinned model/training/stream sources. Independent aggregation reproduced all 18 summary groups from the 1,728 raw development rows. The 40,000 training rows, 120,000 accepted synthetic IDs, 12,319 rejected draws and 40,000 unique non-overlapping real TRAIN schedule rows were checked against the frozen run. The fit diagnostic's checkpoint, panel, geometry rows, source, protocol, raw rows and summary hashes also matched its completion receipt.

Decision: keep this lineage and the improved candidate coverage, but do not scale the unchanged direct-pose objective, calibrate its scores, qualify the GUI model, or run the public benchmark. The dominant next problem is **selecting and improving candidates from actual predicted poses**. Train the atlas-match component against current predicted branches with physical supervision and real-image retention; then verify that its fitting signal gives useful pose-update directions from those predicted branches before joint pose/deformation continuation. A nonzero gradient or a match at a supplied true pose alone is insufficient. The eventual joint loss must keep direct physical anchors and deformation penalties so the fitter cannot conceal a wrong plane.

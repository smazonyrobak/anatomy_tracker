# Atlas self-similarity candidate probe 016

The direct and dense heads of 015 still leave 2.7–2.8 mm synthetic visible-tissue error, while its 32-branch physical oracle is below 1 mm and the correct-pose mapper is near 0.13 mm. A previous 64-case **constructed** candidate-bank pilot ranked true planes highly with MIND structural descriptors, but it did not test the current model's actual imperfect pose proposals, and imperfect outlines failed its frozen robustness gate. Plain intensity correlation and learned scalar fit scores already failed on the present lineage. Therefore atlas structural comparison is a bounded hypothesis, not a proven fix.

Freeze 015 batch 8,000 (its best direct mapped checkpoint on the fixed DEV panel). For each of the 185 eligible synthetic DEV sections, render all 32 predicted pose/reflection branches from the Allen atlas at 96 pixels with the case's through-plane PSF; do not change any model weights or candidate states. Score the same rendered bank using the existing physical MIND descriptor at 125 µm nominal pitch, with three target domains: whole image (no automatic mask), the model's predicted tissue-validity mask (threshold 0.2 with a top-1% fallback if empty), and the **known synthetic true mask only as a non-deployable upper-bound diagnostic**. For the masked scores, assign maximum mismatch to pixels predicted to contain tissue but lying outside the candidate atlas support. Select the highest structural score and compare its 3D rigid visible-tissue error against the direct prior and all-32 oracle, equally weighted over the eight synthetic deformation plans. Preserve every candidate score, physical error, identity and input binding.

The useful-mechanism gate is at least 0.2 mm plan-equal improvement from the deployable predicted-mask score over the direct prior, without worsening the <5%-visible-tissue group. If only the true-mask score helps, tissue support—not a ready pose scorer—is the issue. If neither helps, do not train another scalar fit-score head on this evidence. This remains DEV mechanism work; it does not touch final-test animals, the public benchmark, or probability calibration.

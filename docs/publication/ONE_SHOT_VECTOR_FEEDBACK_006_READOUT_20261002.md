# One-step atlas/warp correction versus image-only control (2 October 2026)

The new shared pose-correction-and-ranking head was trained from the same random initialization in two matched arms starting from the frozen batch-8,000 one-shot pose/match checkpoint. Both arms processed byte-identical top-four **actual predicted** pose/reflection branches and the same rendered finite-thickness atlas planes. The direct arm received only candidate-conditioned image features and pixel position; the atlas arm additionally received image–atlas comparison features, the frozen local mapper's **projected** displacement and its size/roughness, and atlas support. The parent pose predictor, image/atlas encoders, mapper, scalar match head and uncertainty outputs were frozen in both arms. This isolates the usefulness of the currently learned features for one supervised vector correction; it is not yet a jointly trained pose–deformation deliverable.

Each arm completed 8,000 three-image batches with 16,000 distinct accepted synthetic physical sections, 1,632 rejected low-support draws and 8,000 distinct real TRAIN sections; their draw logs are byte-identical (SHA-256 `35f56919812b36d7cf6fd96ea193615f2188a7b579026cc561a0c384430b066a`). The evaluator scored all five checkpoints per arm on the unchanged 90 eligible synthetic sections from four development deformation identities and 64 weakly labelled Allen images from six separate development donors. Identity-equal means follow.

The atlas arm is 0.090 mm better than the matched image-only arm at batch 8,000, but improves only 51/90 individual synthetic sections; two of four synthetic identities worsen, one by 0.543 mm. Its real weak-affine advantage is 0.008 mm, with only 30/64 images improving. Its 2.733 mm synthetic median, 5.757 mm 90th percentile, 22/90 within 1 mm and 16/90 worse than 5 mm are unacceptable. Crucially, **both** updated arms are worse than the unmodified parent's already-evaluated atlas-match selection of **3.098 mm** (24/90 within 1 mm). The best-of-four oracle itself moves from 1.597 to 1.636 mm in the atlas arm, so corrections sometimes damage a good branch. Averaging physical loss over all four candidates may contribute, but this experiment does not isolate the cause. There is no evidence to promote this correction head.

The earlier 8–11 mm five-canvas-point metric should not be read as anatomical tissue error, but a separate support-stratified check of the frozen parent shows that large **visible-tissue** errors are real on very partial sections. In the smallest visible-support quartile (548–6,328 valid pixels), atlas-match selection averages 4.732 mm, has an 11.186 mm 90th percentile and 47.96° mean plane-normal error; in the largest quartile (15,396–29,935 pixels), the corresponding means are 1.769 mm and 23.10°. Even the reference-best branch is 1.506 versus 0.741 mm across those quartiles. Tiny arbitrary-plane intersections are intrinsically more ambiguous, but that is a reason to quantify uncertainty or abstain, not to hide them from the denominator or claim the current model is accurate.

Post-exit audit independently matched the pinned trainer/model/stream sources, parent checkpoint and real schedule; both 8,000-row training logs and identical 25,632-row draw logs; 16,000 unique accepted physical-section IDs and 8,000 unique real section IDs; all ten checkpoint hashes; panel, real-record, evaluator-source, raw-row and summary hashes. All 20 identity-equal development summary groups were recomputed from all 1,920 rows (maximum absolute discrepancy below `5e-13`). Both processes completed without stderr. Frozen output trees are `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_vector_feedback_006` and `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_vector_feedback_006_development_eval`.

**Decision:** Do not use this vector updater or its scores in the GUI, and do not calibrate or benchmark them. The controlled extra atlas evidence is not yet worth its render cost. A single targeted follow-up is justified before abandoning the architecture: train the atlas feature encoder together with the updater on **actual predicted** branches, using a best-branch physical objective so a good proposal is not penalized for the other bad branches; compare against an image-only updater on fresh but matched draws. The existing experiment froze cross-modal features learned mostly from true-pose mapping, so it did not establish that atlas-aware evidence cannot work when trained for pose correction. Require a substantial held-out improvement beyond the parent's 3.098 mm, across identities and catastrophic tails, not merely a nonzero gradient or marginal control difference. If that gate fails, prioritize redesigning coarse pose representation/visibility and constraints over extending the same frozen-feature refinement.

# 219: broad anatomy correspondence did not improve blind arbitrary-plane pose

The TRAIN-only 219 pilot continued the project's own randomly initialized 187 step-800 lineage. Its direct 160-branch pose model was frozen. A new, randomly initialized 24-pixel source/atlas correspondence head saw independently drawn full-sphere slices from 60 synthetic TRAIN deformation bases. Each draw had an independently sampled plane, support-aware framing, and v4 image/artifact variation; no same-plane appearance pairs were deliberately made. The four remaining TRAIN bases supplied eight fixed inner readouts. The public DEV, calibration, final, DeepSlice benchmark, and physical oblique test material were not opened.

One TRAIN-native-211 smoke verified that framing preserved the pre-artifact physical pose to 0.00137 µm RMS over seven interior tissue sites, with unchanged reflection and slab PSF. A separate TRAIN-166 smoke verified finite correspondence output, zero-support penalty, bounded fitting and gradients to source features, atlas features and pose. The complete 219 preflight passed before training. These are numerical contract checks, not accuracy evidence.

The 300 accepted updates finished normally in 87.2 seconds after setup; all 300 plane IDs were independently drawn. The completed receipt lists eight output hashes, independently rehashed with no mismatch. The same eight eligible, low-exposure inner cases were measured at updates 0, 100 and 300; their visible-tissue fractions ranged from 0.084 to 0.614. Errors below are mean physical CCF displacement over surviving tissue sites, summarized as the median of eight cases. The normal angle is antipodal (the slice plane has no directed normal).

| Update | Existing prior top-1 | Anatomy-only top-1 | Anatomy + 0.3 prior top-1 | After fitted correction | Truth-chosen best of 160 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 3.006 mm | 4.098 mm | 3.601 mm | 3.682 mm | 1.755 mm |
| 100 | 3.006 mm | 3.908 mm | 3.601 mm | 3.689 mm | 1.755 mm |
| 300 | 3.006 mm | 3.996 mm | 3.601 mm | 3.770 mm | 1.755 mm |

The fused selected branch did not change on any of the eight cases. Its median normal-axis error stayed 53.6°, versus 60.0° for the existing prior and 27.2° even for the truth-chosen best available branch. Top-10 recall of a <2-mm branch stayed 4/8. The learned fit is not safe: for the 300 training draws, the truth-best available branch worsened after fitting in 219 cases, and its mean error rose from 1.73 to 2.01 mm across the three 100-update windows. The exact target affine gauge itself moved about 1.24–1.28 mm on average after the fit.

The head did update numerically, but its learned evidence did not become reliable. In the three 100-update windows, correspondence cross-entropy stayed about 5.91–5.96, counterfactual loss about 0.75–0.77, and the tissue/background reliability losses crossed near 0.69 rather than separating clearly. The exact target state scored above the best model branch only 21/300 times, and above the sampled wrong branch only 47/300 times. One plausible structural cause is the score itself: it subtracts each candidate's mean key similarity before log-sum-exp. That removes absolute match agreement and rewards a sharp accidental local maximum; the ±4-mm depth/3-mm in-plane search makes such maxima possible for wrong planes. The score also omits the coherent fitting residual and correction effort, so local fit difficulty cannot yet teach pose selection. This is a mechanistic hypothesis to test, not a retrospective claim of proven causality.

**Decision:** reject 219 for promotion, calibration or GUI use; do not extend this loss unchanged. A matched, short TRAIN-only ablation should first remove only the per-candidate similarity centering, keeping draws, parent, optimizer and blind readout fixed. Require improved true/wrong ordering *and* blind top-1/top-10 on separate inner draws before scaling. Independently improve the candidate geometry: the truth-chosen best of 160 is still 1.755 mm / 27.2° median, which a selector cannot beat without a correction that actually works. The eight-case pilot is too small and low-exposure-skewed for a general arbitrary-plane conclusion, and virtual sections are not physically oblique-cut animal validation.

# Trainable atlas evidence for pose correction (2 October 2026)

This is the planned follow-up to the failed frozen-feature vector correction. Both arms started from the same randomly initialized-lineage batch-8,000 candidate-energy checkpoint and saw byte-identical independently drawn synthetic sections, real TRAIN sections and four **actual predicted** pose/reflection branches. Each trained the same shared one-step pose-correction/ranking head; only the atlas arm also trained the atlas feature encoder and exposed rendered atlas, image–atlas and local-warp evidence to that head. The direct arm used image/candidate information alone. A pre-update, best-branch-weighted physical loss was intended to avoid dragging an already good candidate toward the other bad ones. The original global pose predictor and tissue mapper remained frozen: this tests whether learned fit evidence can help a residual correction, **not** the full requested end-to-end fitting-to-coordinate training mechanism.

Each arm completed 10,000 three-image batches: 20,000 distinct accepted synthetic sections, 2,002 rejected low-support draws and 10,000 distinct real TRAIN sections per arm. The matched draw log SHA-256 is `507b527b330eeeec6dc2df4da3873be326cf435fad822d45cf715516363b36b1`. Evaluation used the unchanged 90 eligible synthetic sections from four synthetic deformation identities and 64 weak-affine Allen development images from six separate donors. These synthetic identities are **not** independent biological animals, and the real labels are **not** blinded expert truth. Identity-equal results are:

| Shared start / 0 | 3.573 mm | 1.597 mm | 40.01° | 0.692 mm |
| Image-only / 6,000 | 3.404 mm | 1.603 mm | 37.12° | 0.624 mm |
| Atlas-aware / 6,000 | **3.169 mm** | 1.571 mm | 35.24° | **0.613 mm** |
| Image-only / 10,000 | 3.238 mm | 1.603 mm | 37.01° | 0.648 mm |

At batch 6,000, the atlas-aware arm beats its matched image-only control by 0.235 mm on the identity-equal synthetic mean. It improves 54/90 individual sections, but one of four synthetic identities worsens by 0.186 mm. The real weak-affine advantage is only 0.011 mm, with exactly 32/64 images improving. By batch 10,000 the synthetic advantage shrinks to 0.036 mm and three of four synthetic identities worsen. The atlas arm's best-of-four geometry modestly improves from 1.597 to 1.571 mm at batch 6,000; this is too small to resolve the dominant candidate-selection failure.

Most importantly, the earlier frozen parent's atlas-match-selected result is **3.098 mm** on this synthetic panel and **0.618 mm** on the weak-real set. Thus even the best atlas-aware checkpoint here is worse by 0.071 mm on the synthetic identity-equal mean, despite narrowly better weak-real error. This is an **operational** comparison, not an isolated correction-head comparison: the parent match head selected from all 32 pose/reflection branches (1.164 mm reference-best oracle), whereas this run corrected and ranked only the prior's top four (1.597 mm reference-best oracle). The restricted candidate pool itself explains part of the ceiling and must not be attributed to the updater. At batch 6,000 its synthetic median is 2.727 mm, 90th percentile 5.539 mm, only 22/90 sections are within 1 mm and 15/90 exceed 5 mm. A ~3 mm mean is a substantial anatomical error, not a satisfactory location estimate for electrode-site region claims. The separate geometry sanity check in the preceding readout found only 0.156 mm mean error when the **known generating pose** was applied, ruling out a general millimetre-unit conversion or inconsistent synthetic target as the explanation.

Post-exit audit matched the pinned source/model/stream hashes, parent checkpoint, real schedule and matched draw records; all 12 checkpoint hashes; panel and real-record bindings; evaluator source; and raw-row and summary hashes. Independently recomputing all 108 identity-equal metric values across 24 groups from all 2,304 raw rows found no discrepancies above `1e-5` µm. Both training and evaluation stderr files are empty. Frozen output trees are `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_joint_feature_feedback_007` and `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_joint_feature_feedback_007_development_eval`; the public benchmark was not used and uncertainty is not calibrated.

**Decision:** Do not promote this checkpoint, claim joint fitting-to-coordinate learning, calibrate its scores or run the public benchmark. Trainable atlas evidence provides a modest matched benefit at one checkpoint, so the hypothesis is not disproved; however, the current one-step residual head fails to improve the strongest complete selection path or control the catastrophic tail. The evaluator measured corrected pose geometry but did not re-render and re-map tissue at that corrected pose, so a coherent final image-to-atlas mapping was not established. Further small variants of the same frozen-pose setup are low priority. The next substantial experiment should change the actual source of global error: directly train the probabilistic pose predictor with tissue-aware image evidence and a differentiable atlas/fitting objective, while keeping geometric synthetic supervision and limiting the warp so it cannot conceal a wrong plane. Evaluate candidate coverage and selection separately, including low-support partial slices and optional user constraints. The fitting loss must demonstrably update the global pose predictor before this can be called the requested joint model.

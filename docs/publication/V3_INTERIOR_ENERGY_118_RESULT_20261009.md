# 118: atlas intensity has a local signal, but did not correct rigid pose

The frozen assay in [the 118 protocol](V3_INTERIOR_ENERGY_118_PROTOCOL_20261009.md) completed without training or checkpoint promotion. It used the 112 step-512 model on 64 identity-disjoint synthetic DEV sections. The output is `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/v3_interior_intensity_energy_118_dev`.

Only 14/64 sections met the prespecified support-matched wrong-pose and common-interior criteria: 24 had no qualifying support-matched wrong pose, 25 had too little shared eroded interior, and one failed mapped-support matching. Seven of eight synthetic plans contributed a scored section. On those 14, the exact pose had better anatomy-intensity energy than the wrong pose in 14/14; the local perturbation energy fell toward truth in 13/14 and rose away from truth in 14/14. The zero-intensity readout gave zero differences by construction. This is evidence of a *conditional* local contrast signal on atlas-derived synthetic images, not blind candidate ranking or a real-slide result.

Thirteen sections had a top-14 proposed branch within 1.5 mm by the five fixed canvas points, but only six retained enough interior for the one-step diagnostic. Ground truth chose the nearest proposed branch **only for this assay**; the step direction used image-versus-atlas intensity and the predicted-support mask, not truth. All six steps reduced image mismatch. Rigid pose error improved in only 4/6 and worsened by **0.0016 mm on average**. The learned warped-map chart improved in 5/6 by 0.0092 mm on average; it is not a substitute for rigid-pose improvement. All six retained 100% of the baseline support mask. The prespecified correction gate required at least 16 steps and a mean rigid improvement of at least 0.05 mm, so it is **inconclusive and not met**. An anatomy-energy descent that looks better in image space must not be called a successful pose correction.

This does not establish that arbitrary planes are impossible. It does show that another unqualified local-correlation feedback loop is not justified. The next model change should address global candidate capture and anatomical matching while auditing the synthetic-to-real appearance gap. The current v3 generator does sample arbitrary normals and independently randomized plane/appearance/artifacts, but its anatomy comes from one grayscale Allen template; defects are stylized (smooth warp, ellipse-like fragment, narrow fold-like band, bubble, half-frame seam), and their frequencies are not measured from real slides. All-angle sampling alone is not all-angle accuracy. Prior sparse real-oblique labels also remain inadequate to certify physical arbitrary-plane precision. Preserve strict animal-level validation and postpone DeepSlice comparison until independent real results are credible.

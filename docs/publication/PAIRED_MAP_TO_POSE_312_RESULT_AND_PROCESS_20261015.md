# 312: a direct map-to-pose gradient is real but too small to justify scaling

**Decision: do not extend 312 unchanged or promote either checkpoint.** The [predeclared short comparison](PAIRED_MAP_TO_POSE_312_PROTOCOL_20261015.md) resumed the same randomly initialized-lineage 309 one-pass pose-plus-warp model twice. Each arm received 256 matched four-section batches: 1,024 accepted, distinct virtual plane IDs from 1,066 attempts. The `draws.jsonl` files are byte-identical, the 512 training rows have finite losses/gradients, and both final checkpoints exist. Synthetic planes, tissue warps and appearance were drawn independently; intensity sources were the pinned atlas average plus three donor-distinct **TRAIN** red-background volumes. The only arm difference was whether local-map cost could update the predicted plane state and branch probability. No true plane replaced a predicted branch, no external pretrained/original-app weights or pseudolabels entered, and no acquired or DEV image was used for gradients.

Joint feedback beats the matched control by **0.0286 mm** in first-choice rigid error, but the paired 10,000-resample case-bootstrap 95% interval is **−0.0116 to 0.0716 mm**. Its mapped-site advantage on the first 64 cases is **0.0093 mm**, interval **−0.0032 to 0.0298 mm**. Both intervals include zero. The mapped readout uses a common 62.5-µm PSF approximation because the old 309 panel did not retain individual section thickness; compare arms, not that number as an exact-mapping claim. The panel is TRAIN-inner and has informed earlier development, not an untouched biological test.

A separately frozen, donor-held-out virtual-cut panel then used two other individual registered red-background sources, 64 independently drawn full-sphere cuts from synthetic inner bases 56–63 per donor, and each cut's actual generated thickness. The source ZIPs are SHA-bound in `I:/AnatomyTracker/reports/paired_map_to_pose_312_heldout_red/config.json`. All 128 case IDs are distinct and disjoint from 312 gradient IDs; 384 per-model rows and the saved panel hash check passed. The mapped inference uses the pinned average atlas, not the donor's own source volume. These are still **synthetic cuts from approximately atlas-registered donors**, not physically cut oblique sections or exact biological warp truth.

Combined donor-held-out rigid and mapped advantages over the detached arm are **0.0279 and 0.0279 mm**. Their paired case-bootstrap intervals are **−0.0167 to 0.0820 mm** and **−0.0170 to 0.0824 mm**. Both arms have 10/128 cuts >5 mm and 101/128 top-16 capture ≤1.5 mm. The common-source 309 parent has 12/128 >5 mm and 100/128 capture. Source mixing plus a short update may help, but this design cannot attribute the parent difference to donor diversity alone. It does not establish an acquired-animal improvement, so the predeclared extension gate remains closed without spending a further run on real guards.

This also changes the development priority. The frozen 309 diagnosis attributes roughly 1.54 mm of its v10 mean first-choice error to choosing the wrong branch despite a better top-16 candidate; exact-pose synthetic local residual is about 0.109 mm. A prior acquired-image check found that the inherited synthetic-trained local field was *worse than zero deformation* against approximate VisuAlign residual fields on all four TRAIN Lupori animals (mean RMS 0.172 versus 0.118 mm). Thus map difficulty is not yet a trustworthy real-image feedback signal. Another longer pose-head, likelihood, scalar fit-score, or map-gradient continuation would optimize a weak target while leaving the main blind-selection and acquired-domain problems in place.

The next decision should require **new information, not another small loss change**: audit a small, donor-separated set of anatomically rich real sources and human field/pose references for label and appearance quality; require a local map that improves over zero warp on an animal-held-out TRAIN guard; and require a spatial anatomy-based first-choice gain on fresh full-sphere cuts that survives support-only and acquired-image guards. Only then combine these into the same direct model and scale presentations. Keep the synthetic generator's strongly speckled/inverted examples under visual realism review, retain optional brush/acquisition hints and multiple uncertain modes, and leave calibration/final animals and DeepSlice untouched. Physical steep-oblique validation still needs independently measured cuts; no public dataset located here supplies that truth.

For scale context, [DeepSlice's study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10514056/) used an approximately 21-million-parameter **ImageNet-pretrained** Xception, about 131k Allen slide-mounted and 443k S2P images, plus roughly 0.9 million virtual *coronal* sections; it recommends multiple sections per brain for angle integration. Our 22.1-million-parameter model is random-start by requirement and tackles a harder single-slice full-sphere task. Parameter count and merely matching presentation count are therefore not evidence of comparable accuracy, and its coronal result is not a fair current benchmark for arbitrary physical obliques.

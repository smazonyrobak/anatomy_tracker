# Joint pose–deformation continuation with native atlas-fit feedback (2 October 2026)

This is the first matched continuation in this one-shot lineage where the **global pose predictor and atlas-conditioned tissue mapper both trained at actual predicted poses**. Both arms started from the same batch-8,000 candidate-energy checkpoint, used the same 16 pose modes × two reflections, and saw byte-identical independently drawn sections. Their shared objective combined all-branch physical pose supervision, mapped visible-tissue CCF error from the image-prior branch and the reference-best **predicted** branch, and bounded, affine-projected local-deformation penalties. The `fit` arm alone added differentiable finite-slab atlas image agreement on valid synthetic tissue for branches already within 2 mm by the detached reference metric. It did not use a supplied true pose as an inference candidate. This is a training curriculum inside the intended joint architecture, not a deployable model.

Each arm completed 10,000 three-image batches (20,000 distinct accepted synthetic sections, 2,006 rejected low-support draws, and 10,000 distinct real TRAIN sections). The matched draw-log SHA-256 is `2744c591ca5f4a48669818065819331830925a7775654162a14c8632481edb5b`. The real schedule excludes the previous training schedules through experiment 007. The independent evaluator read all 32 predicted pose/reflection branches at each checkpoint on the unchanged 90 eligible synthetic sections from four held-out **synthetic deformation identities** and 64 weak-affine Allen images from six separate development donors. Synthetic identities share one atlas and are not biological animals; real affines are not blinded expert references. Means below weight development identities equally.

The fit arm beats the matched control by 0.141 mm in selected pose error at batch 8,000, but only 47/90 synthetic cases improve; one of four synthetic identities worsens. Relative to the unmodified parent, the 0.115 mm identity-equal gain is concentrated: only 40/90 cases improve, and two of four synthetic identities worsen, one by 0.593 mm. The fit arm's synthetic median is 2.430 mm and 90th percentile 5.332 mm; 12/90 sections exceed 5 mm, versus 11/90 and a 5.279 mm 90th percentile for the parent. Weak-real error is 0.033 mm worse than the parent across equal donors, with four of six donors worse. At batch 10,000, synthetic and weak-real results deteriorate further. Thus the best checkpoint is a small, unstable development gain, not evidence of robust accuracy.

The first-batch gradient audit establishes a real learning path: mapped-tissue loss had nonzero gradients into both the global pose head (`55.29`) and the local warp head (`1.04`); native fit loss likewise reached both (`11,450.37` and `132.55` before weighting). The fit weight was clamped at its `0.01` floor, making its first-batch **weighted** pose gradient about `114.50`, comparable to the base loss gradient `112.23`—stronger than the intended 20% ratio. This limits causal interpretation of the fitted arm and is a consequential scaling issue, not proof that native fit is intrinsically harmful or sufficient. A nonzero gradient alone is not a quality result.

The known-pose mapper remains accurate near 0.15 mm, but after selecting a predicted branch its error is still about 3 mm: local deformation cannot and should not hide a wrong global plane. The 1.149 mm best-of-32 pose versus 2.983 mm selected pose identifies **candidate selection and global localization** as the dominant remaining bottleneck. The output is uncalibrated; the public benchmark, final-test animals and GUI qualification were not used.

Post-exit audit matched the pinned trainer/model/stream and schedule hashes, parent checkpoint, byte-identical draw logs, all 12 checkpoint hashes, panel and real-record bindings, evaluator source, and raw-row/summary hashes. Independently aggregating all 132 metric values across 24 equal-identity groups from all 2,304 raw evaluation rows found no discrepancies above `1e-5` µm. Training and evaluation exited without stderr. Frozen runs are `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_joint_native_fit_008` and `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_joint_native_fit_008_development_eval`.

**Decision:** Preserve the 8,000-batch fit checkpoint as an experimental lineage point but do not promote it into the GUI or claim anatomical accuracy. Do not tune repeatedly on the same 90 synthetic sections. The next substantial stage should address selection among plausible planes with a stronger, jointly trained image–atlas match representation and a properly scaled fitting signal; assess it on a freshly generated, disjoint synthetic development panel as well as the separate weak-real donors. Maintain a hard distinction between synthetic physical truth, weak Allen real labels and eventual blinded expert truth. Only after genuinely stronger localization should constraints, calibrated joint uncertainty and full GUI deployment be qualified.

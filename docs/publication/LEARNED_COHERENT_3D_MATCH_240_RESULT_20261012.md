# 240: falling descriptor loss did not produce a reliable pose correction

The randomly initialized 2-D/3-D descriptor completed 1,024 unique physical-section training presentations. The independent training verifier passed: the frozen 226 proposer and 240 source/protocol hashes matched, the checkpoint was the best on the fixed 32-section TRAIN-inner development validation set, and no reserved animal, public benchmark, external pretrained feature, or calibration data was used. The validation loss fell from 2.372 at presentation 256 to 2.188 at 1,024. This was **not** sufficient evidence of better registration: on the same validation sections, near/wrong raw-score wins were only 9/19, 10/19, 10/19, and 10/19 at the four checkpoints.

After freezing the 1,024-presentation checkpoint, a separate development-only mechanism check ran on those same 32 sections. It made no training updates and did not open the predeclared fresh 64-section readout. The known-true pose served only as a diagnostic control, never as an inference input.

The intact mean near change was **−0.023 mm** (worse), not the required ≥0.25-mm gain. The null advantage was 0.124 mm, below the required 0.15 mm. Most decisively, known-true error rose to 0.425 mm, far above the required ≤0.20 mm; 15 of the 17 accepted known-true moves worsened their physical-site error. A threshold on score gain is not a physical-accuracy safeguard here. This development failure is sufficient to stop the **unchanged** 240 recipe and not spend the sealed readout or extend its training unchanged. It is not a result on the sealed panel or on real animals, and it does not establish that arbitrary-plane registration is impossible.

The code audit found two concrete score/geometry defects that must be fixed before a successor run. First, the score averages the best 64 similarities after filling unsupported sites with `−1`; on sections with fewer than 64 supported sites, simply moving toward more atlas support can raise it. Known-true hypotheses had only 25–172 supported query sites (mean 71.8), and supported-site count correlated with score (`r = 0.647`) on this small panel. This association is diagnostic, not proof that support alone caused every bad move. Second, the source CNN's stride-8 feature at index `2j` is centred at input pixel `16j`, but the training/scoring query chart labels it at pixel `16j + 3.5` (and rounds its positive label to `16j + 4`). This systematic source-feature/CCF label offset should be corrected; its isolated impact has not yet been measured. The unmasked top-64 selection and weak near/wrong ranking objective also let a lower local contrastive loss coexist with almost chance plane ranking. More iterations of the same objective are not justified by these data.

The next bounded work is a matched, development-only test of **correctly centred 2-D-to-3-D correspondences with robust geometric consensus**, rather than another smooth ascent on the current score. It should fix site geometry, make source-site selection and support normalization explicit, retain a no-shift option, compare intact anatomy with the support-preserving shuffle on exactly the same cases, and measure both near correction and known-true drift. If that mechanism is useful, train its matching/pose objective from random initialization and then build the shared joint pose/deformation checkpoint; if not, reconsider the descriptor/data signal before scaling. The predeclared 240 sealed panel remains unopened. Acquired-animal validation and calibrated electrode-region probabilities remain future requirements.

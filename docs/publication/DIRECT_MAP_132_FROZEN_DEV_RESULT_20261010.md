# Frozen direct-map 132 baseline: correct pose works; nearby pose does not

The existing scratch-trained 132 step-2000 one-pass model was evaluated without retraining on the same 491 eligible synthetic DEV sections and globally supported tissue sites used for the 143/145 field comparisons. The known exact state is an oracle diagnostic. The nearby state is the truth-best member of the model's frozen blind 16-branch beam, also an oracle diagnostic rather than a deployable selector. The direct map received the section image, candidate state, finite-thickness atlas and section PSF, produced a native 256-pixel tissue-to-CCF map, and was scored at the matched 64-grid sites. Frozen checkpoint, panel, prior-evaluation and per-section support counts were verified before scoring.

| Sites in sections whose best beam pose is ≤1.5 mm from truth | Direct map exact-state hit ≤0.5 mm | Direct map nearby-state hit ≤0.5 mm | 143 field exact | 143 field nearby |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| v4, 179/248 sections | 95.22% | 31.37% | 96.93% | 29.45% |
| v3, 197/243 sections | 95.67% | 38.20% | 96.84% | 35.32% |

The table uses the identical near-available subset and matched support denominators. The 143 field requires a non-dustbin prediction for a hit; the direct model has no dustbin and all its finite points are scored, so its figures are generous rather than a strictly identical output rule. Across *all* eligible sections, direct exact-state hit is 94.36% v4 and 94.62% v3; direct nearby-state hit is 28.94% v4 and 36.00% v3. Direct point-error means on the near-available, globally supported sites are 0.166 mm v4 and 0.160 mm v3 at the exact state, but 0.744 mm and 0.665 mm at the nearby state. The local map offers no escape from a materially wrong plane. Its affine component is explicitly removed, so it cannot simply absorb global pose error as deformation; that gauge choice is appropriate, but means the pose updater must actually correct the global state.

**Decision:** retain this direct-map head as an informative exact-pose baseline, not a shipped model. Do not continue the separate 143–146 field unchanged: it is at best comparable on nearby pose and has not provided anatomy-dependent pose feedback. The next training change must give the pose head a learnable correction from atlas–section anatomical evidence while retaining a local deformation that cannot hide global misplacement. Judge it by paired physical point error, blind branch selection, exact-map retention and an atlas-intensity/support-only control, not by training loss alone. This result does not establish arbitrary-angle performance on real animals, calibrated uncertainty, electrode-region probabilities, or GUI readiness.

# BM03 coordinate-chain audit (TRAIN-only)

The frozen 162 BM03-to-DAPI and published DAPI-to-Allen transform arithmetic is internally consistent, but this does **not** establish anatomical coordinate truth for BM03. The audit read the frozen source manifest, 162/163/164/229 reports, and two text transforms; it read no image pixels or BM15–18 image files. Source: `training/audit_dapi_bm03_coordinate_chain_20261012.py`; exact report: `I:/AnatomyTracker/reports/dapi_bm03_coordinate_chain_audit_20261012_001/report.json` (SHA-256 `2c379676a44777ec4919a2bc24e5fb02b3cc97c080713c1559f413926fd0ca69`).

For the native centre and six face-centre points, the explicit chain—assumed native BM03 mm, inverse 162 to DAPI RAS mm, one RAS/LPS x/y flip, then the published DAPI LPS mm to Allen LPS µm pull—agrees with the independently saved 164 composed matrix to within 2.45×10⁻¹² µm. Maximum round-trip error is 1.26×10⁻¹⁵ mm. The centre maps to Allen index (234.4790, 160.4117, 230.2216), matching 164. These numbers verify matrix composition, **not** anatomical landmarks; some face probes are outside the atlas.

The BM03 selected NIfTI affine is identity with an unknown spatial unit. Physical 100-µm coronal sections make native axis 1 a plausible stack/AP axis, but available acquisition evidence does not independently fix its positive AP order, the native left/right sign, or the world origin. Existing 229 overlays show ventricular, hippocampal/deep, and edge offsets without signed landmark residuals. Therefore BM03 is not admitted as weak CCF-coordinate pose/deformation or region-label training truth. Next, resolve AP order/LR sign from original acquisition evidence and preregister bilateral cortex, ventricular, corpus-callosum, hippocampal, and deep-structure landmarks across multiple AP levels; quantify signed residuals and exclude unreliable regions before extending to other TRAIN animals.

BM15/BM16 stay DEV and BM17/BM18 sealed. The DAPI population average includes all 12 animals, so it is not an atlas-independent holdout. Interpolated virtual obliques are not physical-oblique validation.

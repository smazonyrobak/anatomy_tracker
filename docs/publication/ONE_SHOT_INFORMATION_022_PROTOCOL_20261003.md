# Fitted atlas image-information diagnostic 022 — frozen protocol

021 failed: its spatial descriptor head learned pixel and hard-negative losses but did not improve selected-plane error. Before building another network, test whether the *rendered atlas images themselves* carry usable rank information at higher resolution, and whether losing the tissue mask is the limiting factor.

Use exactly the 019 batch-18,000 model and the unchanged 185 eligible synthetic DEV sections. Reconstruct the same top-eight predicted/refined candidate planes and check their branch indices against the frozen 019 candidate diagnostic. For each candidate, render the finite-thickness atlas at the model's fitted tissue coordinates. Compare local contrast after 9-pixel-window mean removal and normalized cross-correlation. Rank by mean local disagreement plus missing-atlas-support penalty. Repeat at 96 and 256 pixels; at each resolution compare an *oracle* known synthetic surviving-tissue mask with the parent's own predicted tissue reliability. The oracle mask is diagnostic only and cannot be used at inference. Score all candidates using the same fixed physical tissue-coordinate errors from 019. Preserve panel, checkpoint, source and row hashes. No images need to be opened or displayed; no training or benchmark occurs.

Readout: identity-equal selected mapping error, top-one physical-best fraction and low-support stratification for four conditions (96/256 × oracle/predicted tissue support), beside the 019 fitted-score baseline and physical best-eight oracle. If 256-pixel atlas evidence improves *only* with oracle support, prioritize tissue/support estimation. If both resolutions fail even with oracle support, another atlas-similarity head is unlikely to help; examine proposal geometry, modality gap and information ambiguity instead. If high-resolution evidence helps with predicted support, a higher-resolution conditional model is justified. These are diagnostic interpretations, not a post hoc model-selection gate or permission to touch final-test animals.

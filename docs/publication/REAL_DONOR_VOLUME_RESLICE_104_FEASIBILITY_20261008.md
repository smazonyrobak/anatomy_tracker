# 104: can the reserved real TRAIN images supply arbitrary-angle anatomy?

A bounded, read-only pilot stacked the 140 consecutive 100-µm Allen Product-5 sections of one **TRAIN** donor (`111213883`) using their registered per-section CCF affines. The two in-plane affine columns are constant across this donor's sections and the third coordinate advances by a constant physical step, so this donor forms a regular 3-D image lattice. The CPU-only source `training/probe_reserved_train_volume_reslice_104.py` rendered native coronal, horizontal, sagittal and oblique 14.08-mm fields. The contact sheet and exact affine/section receipt are under `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/reserved_train_volume_reslice_104_probe`.

The four views contain coherent gross anatomy at all sampled orientations. This is a promising way to diversify **training** beyond arbitrary cuts through one Allen template: actual donor-specific internal structures, texture, background and acquisition imperfections can be virtually resliced, with one independently sampled plane/appearance per presentation and donor-level split tracking. It may be more useful than simply increasing the frequency of hand-drawn rings, ridges and ellipses in the current generator.

This is **not** a verified arbitrary-cut dataset or proof of model accuracy. All four views come from the same donor; the non-coronal views are interpolations of near-coronal acquired sections, not slides physically cut in those orientations. Their CCF coordinates inherit Allen's weak affine registration, not blinded expert truth; fine texture is limited by 100-µm between-section spacing and pre-existing tile artifacts can become unnatural 3-D stripes. An image that looks plausible at 256 pixels is not enough to promote it directly into the million-presentation curriculum.

Next data gate: audit affine regularity and coverage across a fixed, donor-diverse TRAIN subset; compare a small grid of virtual cuts against independently acquired cardinal/oblique histology for tissue shape and artifact statistics; then test whether mixed real-volume/synthetic training improves **blind** selected error on new synthetic identities and separate biological development donors. Keep real-volume affine labels weak, synthetic exact deformation targets separate, and final animal-level validation untouched. Full-sphere inference remains the target, with ambiguity surfaced instead of silently forcing a cut family.

The preselected 64-donor geometry audit (`training/audit_reserved_train_volume_geometry_105.py`, raw rows under `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/reserved_train_volume_geometry_105_audit`) has now completed: 63/64 stacks contain consecutive sections on a constant affine 3-D lattice; the remaining donor has one missing section but its in-plane affine columns are also constant. The median stack has 140 sections and median physical between-section step is 96.17 µm. This makes subject-specific virtual reslicing technically feasible at useful scale, while leaving the image-realism and weak-registration limitations above unresolved.

The bounded 106 QC (`training/qc_reserved_real_oblique_provenance_106.py`, four-view image and receipt under `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/reserved_real_oblique_provenance_106_qc`) reused the existing arbitrary-plane real-donor sampler on four different TRAIN donors. The four randomly drawn sections retained 10.7%, 39.5%, 20.9%, and 33.2% valid observed tissue. Its receipt lists the exact animal/specimen/experiment/section identities and source image hashes for every section in each *potential* interpolation span. The visible cuts have coherent but sometimes partial gross anatomy and near-black exteriors; source tile defects can appear as stripes. The current sampler fixes the exterior background at zero. These are virtual 50-µm slab views interpolated from approximately 100-µm serial sections, not physically acquired oblique histology, and their pose labels remain weak Allen affines. The QC establishes source traceability and a visual sanity check, not target accuracy or a realistic-artifact pass.

Scientific feasibility remains conditional. [AMBIA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10406728/) demonstrates tilted atlas extraction on real histology, but its published localization evaluation is largely framed by AP labels and near-coronal angle conventions; its data are available on request, so it is not a ready all-angle benchmark. [QuickNII](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6541252/) uses multiple manually anchored sections and propagation across a series, which is useful evidence for exploiting optional neighboring sections when one view is ambiguous. Neither source proves that an arbitrarily damaged *single* section uniquely determines an exact 3-D plane. We should retain the full-sphere model and allow multimodal/low-confidence outputs; if future blinded oblique-animal validation exposes an operating boundary, ask for a rough cut family or nearby serial slice as an optional clue rather than hard-restricting what the researcher may cut.

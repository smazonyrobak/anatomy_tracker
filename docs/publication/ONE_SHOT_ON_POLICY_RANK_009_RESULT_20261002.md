# On-policy atlas-scoring comparison: frozen development result (2 October 2026)

The preregistered improvement gate in `ONE_SHOT_ON_POLICY_RANK_009_PROTOCOL_20261002.md` **failed**. This is a controlled development comparison, not qualification of the model or a DeepSlice benchmark. Both trained arms continued the same standalone random-initialization lineage for 8,000 matched three-image batches. Their synthetic draws, real TRAIN schedule, initialization and first-step loss weighting matched; the only arm difference was whether the candidate scorer saw its 64-pixel rigid atlas rendering and image–atlas correlations. Both arms retained atlas-conditioned local mapping and a train-time fit loss. The frozen evaluator scored the last checkpoint of each arm plus their common parent.

The primary quantity is the mean over eight equally weighted synthetic **deformation identities** of per-section physical displacement at visible tissue pixels. These identities are not eight biological animals. The 256-row frozen panel used 32 independently drawn arbitrary planes per identity; 185 had enough visible tissue to score. All 71 low-support rows remain recorded: 65 had zero valid tissue pixels and six had 1–587. They are unscored or abstention candidates, not successes. All sections had one preassigned appearance: raw background, exact black exterior or imperfect-brush exterior. The atlas scorer's paired mean is **0.0009 mm worse** than direct; its selection regret falls from 2.119 to 2.112 mm, only **0.3%**, against the preregistered 0.5 mm and 25% improvement requirements. Four of eight identities improve; 87 of 185 scored sections improve and 98 worsen. Its 90th-percentile error improves from 6.328 to 5.685 mm and the >5 mm count falls from 31 to 25, but this tail gain does not make the mean or regret gate pass. Selection is worse than the arm's own image-only prior in both trained arms.

The atlas scorer uses a *rigid candidate rendering before deformation*. It does not directly receive the final anatomical agreement and amount of deformation needed to fit each candidate. Moreover, the initial atlas-fit loss supplied only about **0.93%** of the base pose-gradient norm after the 0.02 weight cap, well below its nominal 20% target. The frozen experiment therefore rejects this specific expensive candidate scorer (~27% more training time than direct), **not** the requested hypothesis that a meaningful fit-versus-distortion objective can teach pose prediction. The best-of-32 candidate error of ~1.08 mm and true-pose mapping error of ~0.14 mm isolate global candidate coverage and especially selection as the main measured bottlenecks. But the low true-pose error alone does **not** establish that the local mapper learned a useful correction; see the zero-warp comparison below. Neither a normalized score nor the current unused probabilistic pose-scale output is a calibrated probability.

A post-hoc, descriptive support-stratified read of the frozen atlas-arm rows confirms that the error is not only an empty-slice or canvas-corner artifact. The 185 scored slices have median visible-tissue error **3.43 mm**, and **119/185 exceed 2 mm**; the worst visible-tissue case reaches **11.21 mm**. Restricting descriptively to the 96 slices with at least 15% visible tissue gives pooled selected/prior/best-of-32 errors of **2.79/2.34/0.76 mm**. At at least 25% support (23 slices), they are **2.39/1.65/0.72 mm**. Thus the rigid atlas scorer hurts the better-supported slices relative to its own image prior, while a plausible branch often exists. These are unplanned panel subsets, not independent biological animals, proof of a causal support effect, or new model-selection gates; the full 256-row accounting above remains primary.

An independent post-hoc computation from all 185 saved synthetic target states, reflection flags, exact CCF target fields and visible-pixel masks gives **139.38 µm** identity-equal error at the true pose with *zero* predicted local displacement. The frozen evaluator's same-pose atlas-arm map gives **142.91 µm**; it improves 77 sections and worsens 108. The ~0.14 mm true-pose result is therefore mainly the strength of the synthetic plane/affine target, not evidence of successful local-deformation prediction. This calculation uses only frozen panel arrays and frozen row outputs; it does not load or change the checkpoint. Its implication is that both global selection and useful local correction remain to be demonstrated.

A targeted post-exit capture diagnostic on the same scored panel showed that the atlas arm's *direct image prior* has 2.938 mm top-one error; an oracle allowed to choose by synthetic truth among its top two, four, eight and all 32 candidates would achieve 2.128, 1.599, 1.287 and 1.082 mm respectively. Thus even perfect reranking of four proposals cannot meet a submillimetre goal. An additional diagnostic actually mapped the prior's top eight, re-rendered the **deformed** atlas and scored local correlation using the *true synthetic valid-tissue mask*—a generous, non-deployable advantage. This picked 3.052 mm, **worse than the 2.938 mm prior**; the learned pre-warp match energy picked 3.172 mm. The oracle-mask fit improved 67 sections, worsened 75 and tied 43. These frozen-panel probes were exploratory, not a new external test. They specifically disfavor using the current raw correlation or learned scalar as a global fit-difficulty likelihood, even if given ideal tissue masking. Diagnostic receipts and raw rows are under `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_rank009_prior_capture_diagnostic` and `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_rank009_fitted_evidence_diagnostic`.

The separate 64-image, six-donor real guardrail is measured against existing weak Allen registrations, **not blinded expert anatomical truth**: donor-equal errors were parent 0.618, direct 0.631 and atlas 0.602 mm. It cannot validate electrode-site or region assignment, and the available real TRAIN sections are overwhelmingly near-coronal. No claim of arbitrary-plane biological generalization or superiority over DeepSlice follows. The historical 11.44 mm number used five canvas positions, including locations outside visible tissue; it overstated anatomical tissue error, but this panel's ~3.2 mm *visible-tissue* error is itself unacceptable for region-level interpretation.

**Decision:** Do not promote either checkpoint or repeat this rigid-render ranking variant. The next priority is stronger coarse pose capture and a learned, robust *post-fit* anatomical score whose correlation with physical correctness is demonstrated before a large run; raw cross-correlation alone is not sufficient. Once candidate capture is useful, train a constrained pose–deformation feedback path with material gradient contribution while preserving direct pose supervision and candidate diversity. Broaden real appearance exposure to donor-separated oblique reslices only after visual/provenance review, recording that their orientations are synthetic constructions from weak Allen stacks, not measured oblique ground truth. Keep the public benchmark untouched for model selection, and do not report calibrated anatomical probabilities or GUI readiness.

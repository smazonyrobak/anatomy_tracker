A dense full-brain atlas bank over both normal and roll is therefore expensive. [SLIV-Reg](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18683v1) uses rotation-equivariant 2D features to reduce this burden, and [CoMIR](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/hash/d6428eecbe0f7dff83fc607c5044b2b9-Abstract.html) emphasizes rotational handling in cross-contrast registration.

Make one controlled change to 025: independently rotate every 2D atlas key patch through a uniformly random in-plane angle during scratch training. Keep the same dual-stem/shared 2D architecture, finite-thickness known-surface positives, 64×64 patch size, two fresh TRAIN synthetic sections and 32 positive plus 32 extra-negative points per section per batch, contrastive loss, optimizer, 3,000-batch exposure, provenance and background/artifact generator. Use a new independent section stream; never load 025 weights, features or pseudolabels. Save optimizer and both sampler/augmentation RNG states and checkpoints at 0/1,000/2,000/3,000.

On the identical untouched 185-section synthetic DEV identities, compare the best nonzero 028 checkpoint with frozen 025 under **exactly** the 025 oracle-plane 288-patch retrieval and 027 known-point angular test. Selection priority: maintain ≥80% top-1 retrieval within 0.5 mm at the correct deformed atlas plane across raw/black/brush, while raising 30° roll top-1 within 0.5 mm by ≥20 percentage points over 025's 28% without losing more than 10 points at 10° normal tilt. If the change fails, retain 025 and test explicit rotation-equivariant/group-pooled features or hierarchical roll search; do not extend training blindly. Passing still only licenses a feasible unknown-plane bank search and later integrated pose/deformation training. It is not real-data, calibrated-probability, GUI or DeepSlice validation.

# 228: simple atlas contrast did not establish a blind fit-feedback signal

The [frozen protocol](BLIND_PLANE_CONTRAST_228_PROTOCOL_20261011.md) was committed as `5dcbc21` before the read-only runner. The 226 step-1,200 pose model stayed frozen. The runner scored 192 independently drawn, eligible physical sections: 96 v4 and 96 v7, 24 per appearance on each of four procedural TRAIN bases. All 192 attempts happened to be eligible; IDs and image hashes are distinct. Each section's blind top 16 pose/reflection branches, plus its true synthetic gauge as a diagnostic render, used the same fixed 50-µm nine-sample atlas PSF. No perfect synthetic tissue mask or true pose entered any candidate score. The physical error and near/wrong pair labels were computed only after scores and blind choices were fixed.

**Frozen decision: inconclusive pairwise gate, no progression.** Both pair counts miss the preregistered minimum 24. No appended true-gauge/wrong pair met the same strict support-matching criteria, so that conditional exact-plane comparison is unmeasured, not a failure of exact-plane image information. On the nine eligible v7 near/wrong pairs, the intact score tied the shuffled-intensity null (6/9 wins); this tiny subset cannot support a positive anatomy-specific claim. Operationally, the prespecified fused blind selector gained only 0.034 mm on v7 versus the required 0.300 mm and regressed 0.213 mm on v4 versus the 0.200-mm tolerance. On v4 it improved 11 cases and worsened 16; on v7 it improved 10 and worsened five, leaving most choices unchanged. The score-only selector was worse than the pose prior on both appearances. These numbers do **not** justify feeding this contrast loss into pose or scaling it as-is.

The low pair eligibility is scientifically relevant: even when the top 16 contained a <2-mm candidate, a candidate ≥1 mm worse with nearly identical rigid atlas support was available in only 16/57 v4 and 9/50 v7 cases under the frozen definition. That is not proof that tissue outline alone can solve the other cases; the current raw-background input may not reveal the outline reliably, and the own-support contrast score already failed as a practical selector. The 180 exact-pose-versus-fixed-perturbation signal and this imperfect-candidate result answer different questions. Do not relax support thresholds or tune the score coefficient on these same 192 cases and call it validation. The next useful step is a new, independent TRAIN-only conditional analysis that separates candidate support/framing error from internal-anatomy error and tests a spatially coherent fit under those real blind proposals. A simple global correlation score is not yet the user's joint model.

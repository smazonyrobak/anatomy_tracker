# 134: fitted-anatomy descriptor did not justify pose feedback

The predeclared 134 stage trained two identically initialized descriptor arms on the **same 4,000 independently drawn synthetic TRAIN sections** from all 64 TRAIN deformation bases. The full arm saw image features and atlas intensity/support at each of the frozen 132 model's 16 actually fitted candidate surfaces; the control saw the same image features, atlas support, fitted maps and penalties, but zero atlas intensity. All 5,265 attempted draws, 4,000 accepted distinct physical-section IDs, 1,333 v3 and 2,667 v4 presentations, five checkpoints, optimizer states, code, protocol and parent-checkpoint hashes passed the post-exit audit. Pose and local mapping weights did not change.

The independent evaluator used the unchanged 248 eligible fresh-v4 and 243 eligible older-v3 synthetic DEV sections. It chose candidates without labels, then opened the frozen synthetic coordinates to measure error. The final step-4,000 **prior-plus-fit** score was the sole primary gate; earlier checkpoints and fit-only scores were descriptive. The table gives synthetic-plan-equal mean fitted-tissue error and first-choice rate within 1.5 mm, not real-animal accuracy.

| Frozen 16-candidate selection | Fresh v4 error | Fresh v4 ≤1.5 mm | Wins / 145 support-matched v4 pairs | Older v3 error | Older v3 ≤1.5 mm |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Direct pose prior | 3.271 mm | 29.4% | 91 | 3.13 mm | 31% |
| Full atlas-intensity plus prior | 3.217 mm | 31.8% | 95 | 3.10 mm | 31% |
| Support-only plus prior | **3.204 mm** | 31.0% | **96** | 3.12 mm | 30% |

The full-intensity arm changed the direct choice in 37/248 v4 sections, but was **0.012 mm worse** than its support-only control and improved the ≤1.5-mm rate over that control by only 0.8 percentage points. Its pair win rate was 65.5%, versus 66.2% support-only. The predeclared requirements were ≥0.15-mm and ≥5-point gains over *both* direct and support-only, plus ≥60% pair wins and a ≥10-point pair advantage over support-only. The 145-pair minimum was met; the accuracy and anatomy-specific advantages failed. The separate v3 retention guard passed, but cannot rescue the failed primary signal.

Fit-only (without the direct prior) was a useful diagnostic, **not** a substitute primary rule: on v4 it gave 3.13 mm / 33% for full versus 3.28 mm / 32% for support-only, but pair wins differed by only about two points (72% versus 70%), and on v3 the full arm was worse (3.05 versus 2.95 mm; 34% versus 38%). The apparent v4 fit-only benefit is therefore not stable evidence that atlas anatomy is supplying generalizable alignment feedback. The 134 TRAIN fit-only cross-entropy also moved only from 2.669 to 2.650 in the full arm between its first and last 500 presentations (support-only 2.670 to 2.653). TRAIN loss alone was not the decision rule.

**Decision:** reject this learned fitted-image energy as a teacher for the pose predictor. Do not promote a descriptor, tune its weight on this DEV panel, or continue the same route unchanged. The current bottleneck remains reliable selection of a near-correct arbitrary-plane candidate, with real-image appearance and the weak contribution of the frozen local map still requiring targeted work. The independent v3 confirmation panel, expert/final animals, uncertainty calibration and public DeepSlice benchmark remain untouched. Neither synthetic panel establishes physical steep-oblique accuracy. The known synthetic PSF was supplied to every arm, an optimistic condition relative to unconstrained real inference.

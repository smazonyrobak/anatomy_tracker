# 310: direct plane likelihood did not improve blind selection

**Decision: reject this loss addition and do not extend it unchanged.** A matched, bounded TRAIN-only continuation tested whether directly rewarding the known synthetic plane would fix the 309 model's first-choice error. Both arms resumed the same internally random-initialized-lineage 309 step-20,000 model and optimizer, kept the local map frozen, and saw the same 1,500 batches of three independently drawn synthetic sections (4,500 distinct physical-section IDs). The control retained the 309 physical site/five-point/normal and candidate-mass objective. The treatment added a Gaussian/full-frame mixture likelihood of the known post-artifact plane, marginalized over the exact reflected-raster alias. The alias changed sampled physical coordinates by at most 0.00098 µm in the FP32 preflight; the additional gradient was finite and fixed at 25% of the first-batch physical-loss gradient norm. Both arms received identical inputs in every batch by image hash. This is a *loss comparison*, not a newly trained complete joint model or a probability-calibration study.

The fixed TRAIN-inner readout has 128 v10 cuts, 64 v9 cuts, 32 v10 cuts with <15% visible tissue and 32 steep v10 cuts from synthetic bases 56–63; no readout identity entered either arm's gradient stream (bases 0–55). Results at the predeclared 1,500-batch endpoint:

| Blind synthetic readout | Unchanged loss | Added plane likelihood |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| v10 first-choice mean surviving-site error | **2.573 mm** | 2.684 mm |
| v10 first-choice 90th percentile | **4.629 mm** | 4.784 mm |
| v10 first choices >5 mm | **7/128** | 9/128 |
| v10 top-16 with a ≤1.5-mm candidate | **117/128** | 112/128 |
| v9 first-choice mean | **2.312 mm** | 2.489 mm |
| sparse-v10 first-choice mean | **4.580 mm** | 4.678 mm |
| steep-v10 first-choice mean | 2.453 mm | **2.350 mm** |

The treatment was better on 45/128 v10 cuts, worse on 77 and within 0.01 mm on six; its paired mean was 0.111 mm *worse*. Only one of eight v10 base means improved. All five prespecified v10 advancement clauses failed: ≥0.25-mm first-choice gain, nonregressing >5-mm count and 90th percentile, nonregressing top-16 capture, and improvement in at least six bases. The steep-slice mean improvement is not a substitute: its >5-mm count rose from zero to one and top-16 capture fell 29→27/32. The unchanged-loss v10 top-16 oracle mean was 0.984 mm, leaving 1.589 mm of first-choice-minus-oracle regret; this remains a *diagnostic chosen with unavailable truth*, not inference accuracy.

The added loss optimized its own training target: its first-to-last-batch negative log likelihood fell 2.281→−1.628, and final physical training loss was 6.441 versus 6.539 for control. The worsened fresh-panel result is concrete evidence against using lower training loss to authorize another long run. It does **not** rule out every proper posterior or anatomical-evidence model: this likelihood is a density in one finite-frame parameterization, with only the principal reflection alias marginalized, and has not been calibrated as a physical-plane distribution.

**Process correction:** stop repeated pose-head, scalar-score and loss-only variants on this lineage. Keep 309 as the research reference. Before more GPU scaling, use existing frozen failures and independently acquired TRAIN imagery to decide whether the obstacle is missing visual anatomical signal, synthetic-to-real mismatch, or inadequate conditional representation; do not infer that a top-16 oracle can be realized by a deployable selector. A genuinely different pose-plus-local-map architecture must demonstrate a *fresh* first-choice and mapped-site gain without larger tails, then pass donor-separated acquired checks before animal-level calibration or GUI integration. Tiny or damaged sections may need optional acquisition constraints, multiple sections, broad uncertainty or abstention. No physical oblique animal truth, calibrated electrode probability, GUI replacement or DeepSlice benchmark was used or earned here.

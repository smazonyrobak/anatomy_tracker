# 072 joint pose correction: frozen development result

**Decision: reject 072 for deployment and do not continue this correction/ranking branch unchanged.** The 10,000 training batches finished, but the independent development readout found essentially no gain in selected synthetic physical accuracy. The 019 research reference remains better. No public benchmark, final-test animal, calibration set or GUI model replacement was used.

The randomly initialized 059-lineage joint model was continued with a fresh shared pose-correction head. Training presented 20,000 unique eligible synthetic arbitrary-plane sections from 64 TRAIN deformation bases and 10,000 distinct weak-Allen-labelled real TRAIN sections, all disjoint from the 059 real schedule. Another 2,009 synthetic draws were rejected for insufficient visible tissue. Appearance was independently sampled per section: 6,602 raw, 6,762 exact-black and 6,636 imperfect-brush. The accepted draws also included 4,429 tear, 3,274 missing-tissue, 4,022 fold, 3,013 bubble and 5,045 tile-seam events; events may co-occur. Source hashes, animal/specimen/experiment/section lineage and the real schedule were retained.

All four frozen checkpoints were evaluated on the **same** 185 eligible synthetic DEV sections from eight held-out synthetic deformation identities and 64 weak-Allen-reference DEV sections from six separate real donors. Distances are identity-equal means in millimetres. “Best of eight” is a truth-selected, unavailable-at-inference bound over the model's actual corrected beam.

Selected synthetic error improved only **0.003 mm** from this run's initial checkpoint, remained worse than the 059 result (2.850 mm), and missed the 019 research reference (2.591 mm) by 0.379 mm. Best-of-eight corrected rigid error improved only 0.118 mm. At batch 10,000, just 50/185 synthetic sections had selected tissue error below 1 mm, while 19/185 exceeded 5 mm. Sections with <5% surviving tissue averaged 5.290 mm (24 sections). Raw, black and imperfect-brush section-weighted means were 3.387, 2.755 and 2.734 mm; these independently drawn groups differ in geometry, so this is not a causal background ablation. Real DEV disagreement remained near the 019 weak-reference level, but inherited Allen affines are **not expert pose truth**, especially for arbitrary obliques. The eight synthetic identities are not biological animals.

The update can move a candidate modestly, yet the selected beam remains far outside the accurate-map regime: the correct-pose map is approximately 0.14 mm, versus approximately 2.97 mm for the selected prediction. A nonzero fit-to-pose gradient (38.409 in the recorded batch-scale calculation) did not yield better selected physical accuracy. There is an important narrower interpretation of this experiment: after warmup, the fitted-map loss was applied to the **truth-best corrected branch**, not the branch selected by the model, and the atlas score was not recomputed after correction. Therefore this run tested one-step atlas-conditioned pose correction, **not** a successful closed fit→pose→refit feedback loop. Training loss or oracle-best geometry cannot substitute for blind selected-pose accuracy. Repeating this correction/ranking loss or simply increasing deformation freedom is not supported by these results.

The next architecture decision should target *coupled full-plane proposal capture* from spatially distributed image–atlas anatomy, with an explicit test of blind near-truth candidate recall and selected physical error on these held-out identities and separate real donors. Prior single-patch atlas-key, fixed-score and dense-coordinate experiments failed; they should not be rebranded and repeated. Rotation-equivariant single-slice 2D/3D feature matching is a relevant precedent ([Brandstätter et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18683)), but its same-modality CT/MRI angle result does not establish mouse-histology translation or deformation accuracy. Differentiable-rendering pose refinement is another precedent ([DiffPose, Gopalakrishnan et al., 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Gopalakrishnan_Intraoperative_2D3D_Image_Registration_via_Differentiable_X-ray_Rendering_CVPR_2024_paper.html)); its X-ray setting has different information content. No probability or electrode-region claim is justified here.

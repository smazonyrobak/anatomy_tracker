# Why the next change is local anatomical evidence, not more of the same training

## What the fixed experiments establish

On the 019 eight-identity synthetic development panel, the 16-mode model's fitted selection averages 2.591 mm tissue-coordinate error. Its best proposed plane is much better (1.166 mm rigid error), and mapping from the *known* plane is 0.129 mm. These are different endpoints, but the large proposal-to-selection gap is enough to make selecting a good proposed plane the immediate priority. The 019 continuation used 30,000 additional batches without clearing the predeclared 0.25 mm improvement gate. In 020, 6,000 batches updating only the scalar fitted scorer likewise failed; the best checkpoint remained the 019 parent. A lower training loss therefore does not establish better localization.

This is not a call to repeat earlier approaches. The pooled rendered-atlas score in 009, the direct absolute CCF-coordinate head in 013–015, the MIND score in 016, and the correct-plane-only pixel contrastive features in 018 all failed to provide the required selected-plane improvement. In particular, the 018 direct descriptor cosine did not rank *predicted* candidates reliably. The 021 experiment changes both the representation and its supervision: it measures spatial correspondence on each of the same-slice candidate planes that the model would actually have to choose among.

A separate read-only exact-pose control limits what the 0.129 mm mapper figure means. On the same 185 sections, the known pose with **no** local warp has 0.139 mm identity-equal error; the learned warp lowers that by only 0.011 mm and helps 62.2% of sections. The gain rises to 0.035 mm in the strongest synthetic-warp quartile. Thus this panel proves neither robust fragment recovery nor substantial nonrigid benefit: global pose dominates the present error, and deformation needs a harder dedicated stress evaluation before any final claim.

## Relevant published methods and limits of analogy

- [DeepSlice (Carey et al., 2023)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-41645-4) used an ImageNet-pretrained Xception backbone with about 21 million parameters, 131,000 Allen slide images, 443,000 connectivity images, and about 0.9 million synthetic sections. Its nine outputs encode an affine plane, without a local deformation field. Its main data and recommended use concern whole *coronal* sections; it recommends grouping multiple sections from a tissue block for angle integration. Its training scale is a useful warning against assuming that our current exposure is sufficient, but its published errors are not a fair arbitrary-plane, single-section comparison.
- [CoMIR (Pielawski et al., NeurIPS 2020)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/d6428eecbe0f7dff83fc607c5044b2b9-Paper.pdf) learned dense shared features to register microscopy modalities whose raw intensities do not match. [HardNet (Mishchuk et al., NeurIPS 2017)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/831caa1b600f852b7844499430ecac17-Paper.pdf) specifically trains local descriptors against hard lookalikes. Both support the *mechanism* of a correspondence representation with difficult negatives, not a claim that mouse histology will be solved by it.
- [A 2D histology-to-3D micro-CT study (2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12271352/) uses learned differentiable feature similarity to initialize a plane, then optimizes plane pose and deformation. Its roughly parallel section prior and microscopy modality differ from our unconstrained brain-intersecting planes, but it reinforces the importance of a capture range, spatial evidence, and a plane/deformation fit evaluated together.
- [Generative diffeomorphic mouse atlas mapping (Tward et al., 2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12645051/) estimates pose, deformation, local contrast and missing/outlier signal jointly. It reports a DeepSlice comparison on five Nissl sections, but it is an iterative multi-image framework, not a directly interchangeable single-slice neural model. The pertinent lesson is to prevent background, contrast variation and torn/missing tissue from dictating pose while maintaining physically constrained deformation.

## Decision and next gate

The 021 branch keeps the current pose/warp model frozen and learns only a 119,000-parameter spatial match head atop the 16.35-million-parameter combined model. It contrasts the observed slice against the finite-thickness atlas render at *actual predicted top-eight poses*, with a training-only known-plane example and pixelwise hard negatives identified from synthetic CCF truth. This isolates whether better anatomical evidence improves candidate choice, without hiding the result behind changed proposals or warps. Its fixed development gate is a ≥0.25 mm reduction in selected synthetic mapping error, with no >0.20 mm donor-mean regression on real weak labels. If it fails, do not merely train the same head longer: inspect information content, resolution and proposal capture. If it succeeds, integrate it into joint pose/deformation training and then test whether gradient feedback into pose adds value.

Neither synthetic error nor inherited Allen real affine agreement validates a clinically meaningful electrode-region probability. That requires accurate expert-checked alignments on untouched animals, then animal-separated calibration of the full trajectory/region event, while keeping the final test animals untouched until the model and calibration procedure are frozen. Public DeepSlice comparison follows only after those internal checks look promising and must use a genuinely comparable coronal subset as well as a separate arbitrary-plane evaluation.

## Subsequent fixed-gate update, 2026-10-03

021 failed its selected-pose gate: its best DEV checkpoint was 2.605 mm versus the unchanged 019 parent at 2.591 mm. 022 showed that the **exact** atlas plane has strong local image agreement but neither a true tissue mask nor 256-pixel comparison can identify the present proposed planes; 023 checked all 32 parent branches and found no plane within 250 µm of truth in 185 cases. The central problem is now **global pose capture**, not merely ranking a sufficient candidate set. See the separate frozen 021–023 result files for their exact endpoints and limits.

024 tested a scratch-learned 2D-patch/3D-cube descriptor as a global atlas-point proposal. The fixed 100,000-position bank covered all sampled DEV truth points within 250 µm, yet the best recall@16 within 500 µm was only **1.872%**, and three separated matches occurred in **0.521%** of sections; the predeclared gates failed. Closer reading of [SLIV-Reg](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18683v1) corrected an important analogy: its 3D volume yields many **oriented 2D patches** encoded by a 2D network, not one invariant 3D cube per point. The next isolated necessity is to demonstrate cross-contrast 2D tissue↔atlas patch matching at the true plane before paying for a global multi-orientation search. The 024 result is documented in `ATLAS_POINT_PROPOSAL_024_RESULT_20261003.md`.

025 established strong cross-contrast patch matching on a known synthetic atlas surface, and 027 measured its narrow but usable orientation capture. A roll-augmented retraining (028) and naive rotation-group average (029) both hurt known-plane retrieval, so the first global search kept explicit roll bins. The truth-independent 2.06-million-patch atlas bank in 030 placed a correct point within top 16 for 15.42% of sampled DEV tissue patches and supplied three separated *correct available matches* for 67.49% of sections. But the fixed geometry-only consensus in 031 selected a 3.668 mm mean-error plane; only 8.73% of the supports it chose were true within 0.5 mm. In contrast, fitting the **oracle-selected** available matches gave a 0.621 mm mean full-tissue error where at least three were present (74.05% of sections). Thus the current bottleneck is selecting anatomically true correspondence groups and linking their fit evidence to pose, not merely producing more atlas candidate patches. None of these synthetic diagnostics qualifies real accuracy or calibrated uncertainty.

The contextual match selector 032 collapsed toward "no match". A balanced conditional ranking, availability and physical-pose continuation 033 improved its weighted rigid plane error from 3.585 to 3.100 mm over another 12,000 fresh synthetic sections, but the fixed match/pose gate still failed. A secondary grouped decision recovered only 12.90% of available matches at its best recall checkpoint while falsely matching 28.76% of unavailable queries. The 034 frozen-pool audit found that even the best of the geometry-ranked top 32 seeds was within 1 mm on only 46.55% of sections. Do not build the final system around this large bank and a small beam of geometry-ranked patches. The immediate architecture change should put a physically supervised, atlas-conditioned updater directly on the 019 probabilistic pose modes, reuse the scratch 025 cross-modal patch representation as fit evidence, and train actual fitting loss to influence pose; keep local deformation anatomically constrained. The need for stronger global pose capture remains, and the new update must be judged by candidate movement and selected physical accuracy, not just a nonzero gradient.

035 sharpened that last condition: mean diagonal cosine of the current 025 patches identified the exact plane when supplied, yet selected **3.116 mm** rigid error from the 019 top eight versus **2.902 mm** for the prior top-one, and its local ascent improved physical error in only 11/24 frozen DEV cases. The unchanged 025 cosine must not simply be wired into a gradient-feedback loop. A [fully differentiable 2D/3D registration study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10879149/) explicitly trained a learned similarity's *pose gradient* toward the geometric-loss gradient (double-backward), rather than only regressing a scalar match score; [DiffPose](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Gopalakrishnan_Intraoperative_2D3D_Image_Registration_via_Differentiable_X-ray_Rendering_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf) used sparse multiscale correlation for test-time refinement. Both concern X-ray/CT, not arbitrary-plane histology, so they motivate an off-plane gradient/capture experiment rather than establish transfer. The current 036 stage first tests a smaller correction to a concrete 019 training defect: its recurrent pose loss took the **minimum** over predicted branches, leaving most wrong branches without direct physical-update supervision. It now trains every branch plus known-pose perturbations against observed physical coordinates. Only if that actually improves frozen DEV pose capture should a fully joint atlas-connected fitting objective be pursued; if not, use off-plane-trained spatial cost/gradient supervision instead of another exact-match ranker.

036 has now **failed** that movement gate: after 3,000 batches, corrected prior-top-one rigid error was 2.942 versus 2.957 mm at start, and best-eight rigid was 1.141 versus 1.166 mm. Its learned scalar residual improved score-selected synthetic error to 2.692 mm, but did not yield the needed pose correction and worsened the weak-real donor-mean endpoint. More identical per-candidate training is therefore unsupported. The next design needs a different evidence representation and off-plane pose-gradient or residual supervision; its gain must be demonstrated on new synthetic identities with a matched no-atlas-feedback control, followed by real-oblique validation rather than interpreting this historical DEV panel as final evidence.

037 supplied that stricter comparison on eight *new* synthetic deformation identities (177/256 draws physically scorable). Atlas-conditioned global 16×16 all-pair correlation did outperform a matched image-only control by 0.161 mm in score-selected error after 4,000 batches, but moved the original best-eight oracle only 0.006 mm and selected error only 0.096 mm relative to parent; weak-real donor nonregression failed by 0.518 mm. The independent frozen-row verifier passed. This isolates an atlas contribution without a usable off-plane correction basin. A further 2D fixed-plane score or unchanged local updater is unlikely to solve the demonstrated global-capture problem. The next experiment should supply nearby through-plane/tilt atlas context and train the fit signal on wrong-plane proposals, measuring physical candidate movement and weak-real transfer before any full joint unfreezing or GUI promotion. Synthetic deformation-identity separation is not biological animal-level validation.

038 measured the required 3D search extent: the prior top-ranked proposal's tissue truth is inside ±6 mm normal/tangent for 93.1% of sampled valid pixels; the physically best of eight fits within ±2 mm on both axes for 94.9%. 039 then trained a finite-thickness 3D atlas correspondence model with a robust geometric pose fit, but after 1,000 fresh TRAIN sections its frozen DEV top-ranked error improved only 2.499→2.476 mm, best-eight worsened 1.001→1.074 mm, and weak-real selected error worsened 0.584→3.533 mm. Its synthetic correspondence recall within 1.5 mm was only 30% on top-ranked and 48% on physically best candidates. 040 supplied unavailable oracle tissue-to-CCF matches to the *same* fit: nearest-key matches reduced top-ranked error to 1.254 mm and best-eight to 0.422 mm. This isolates learning reliable, globally coherent cross-modal correspondences (and real-domain reliability) as the obstacle; the 3D window and fit geometry themselves have substantial capacity. Do not scale or deploy unchanged 039, and do not repeat the failed direct-coordinate 013–015 recipe. See `POSE_FEEDBACK_3D_039_040_RESULT_20261003.md`.

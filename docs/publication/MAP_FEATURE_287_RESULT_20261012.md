The atlas arm gained only **0.003115 mm over the frozen control**, short of the predeclared 0.010-mm advantage, and its absolute 0.005034-mm map gain fell short of 0.020 mm. Its source adaptation also worsened first-choice pose error by **2.229965 mm** from the common step-zero readout; the direct arm worsened by **2.152642 mm**. The derivative and per-base conditions passed, but the map-gain and pose-retention conditions failed for both branches. Atlas minus direct gain was 0.004591 mm, below the predeclared 0.005-mm threshold for a conditioning preference. Numerically larger atlas gain is a weak mechanism signal, **not** evidence that this atlas branch is ready or that direct prediction is generally inferior. The atlas arm reached its derivative cap on at least one panel case. The 3,000-batch training loss trace alone cannot establish generalization.

**Decision: neither branch advances.** A map-only loss updating the shared encoder cannot be allowed to erode the pose head; merely extending these 3,000-section schedules is not a principled fix for the small held-section map gain. The next design must train a map-relevant, spatially resolved image–atlas correspondence while preserving pose supervision/retention, then test whether anatomical fit distinguishes correct from plausible wrong planes before sending fit feedback to pose. A controlled direct path should remain alongside the atlas path until a larger matched difference appears. The newly preflighted AIND SmartSPIM registered volumes may later add independent-animal image diversity, but their unresolved ML handedness and exact 25-µm origin preclude exact plane labels for now; they are not physical oblique-section validation.

This was a true-pose conditional-map mechanism test on synthetic TRAIN material, not blind joint inference. No DEV/CAL/FINAL animal, public DeepSlice benchmark, independently annotated steep-oblique tissue, calibrated uncertainty, electrode-region probability, or GUI model promotion was involved.

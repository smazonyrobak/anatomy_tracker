# Real-section evidence needed for arbitrary-plane claims

The model's full-sphere normal parameterization is not the evidence that it will work on physical cuts from every orientation. Our current new development panel is synthetic; the reserved real TRAIN material is overwhelmingly near-coronal; its virtual oblique reslices are not physical oblique sections and carry weak Allen affine labels. The existing synthetic diagnostics locate a large candidate-selection gap even when the physically best candidate is substantially closer to truth, but do not prove that arbitrary orientation is intrinsically unsolvable. A small or heavily damaged single section can also be genuinely non-identifiable, so the scientific target is calibrated multi-hypothesis output or abstention, with optional rough cut direction/adjacent sections to resolve ambiguity.

No verified public cohort currently combines **physically steep-oblique mouse histology**, **independent expert 3-D pose/local-landmark truth**, and **enough distinct animals for honest held-out calibration and testing**. This is a validation gap, not evidence that those cuts cannot be modeled. Do not quietly substitute virtual reslices or automated Allen alignments for expert truth. Before numerical region/electrode probabilities or an all-angle claim, obtain/verify such physical cases and reserve animals prospectively. Until then keep all orientations in model support, report uncertainty, and retain an optional user-provided rough plane or neighboring section as a clue—not a mandatory restriction to coronal cutting.

The [Duke Mouse Brain Atlas](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12042906/) offers several registered individual 3-D light-sheet volumes, but its stated **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** data license is not a safe default for a proprietary deliverable. Do not import it into training without separate licensing authority.

## Targeted follow-up, 2026-10-10

The [BAP Mouse Head atlas preprint](https://repository.cshl.edu/id/eprint/41879/1/10.21203.rs.3.rs-6387518.v1.pdf) is the strongest small cross-plane lead: six physically sectioned mice across coronal, transverse and sagittal Nissl series, registered using same-animal MRI and manually marked landmarks. The [dataset page](https://data.brainarchitecture.org/pages/mouse/datasets) does not verify a downloadable native 2-D image/per-section-pose bundle. The page states GPLv3 while the [project policy](https://brainarchitecture.org/Policies/) states CC-BY-SA 4.0; resolve both the bundle and rights before proprietary use. Even if available, about two animals per plane and no steep-oblique cohort cannot support held-out calibration or an all-angle claim. Published [Allen ISH tilt estimates](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-018-0241-y), [QuickNII's tilted mouse example](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0216796), and physical horizontal [Mouse Brain Library](https://www.mbl.org/atlas.html) images are useful stress-test leads, not verified independent full-3-D pose ground truth. No data were acquired or final benchmark consulted in this follow-up.

## Individual 3-D training-source lead, 2026-10-15

[Humbel et al.'s EBRAINS whole-brain microCT](https://doi.org/10.25493/JWM7-2G1) is one actual 12-week-old female mouse, imaged isotropically and registered to CCFv3, rather than another reslice of the Allen average. The [paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12302633/) describes the specimen and its atlas registration; the [public volume metadata](https://data-proxy.ebrains.eu/api/v1/buckets/d-256fb2ff-93b2-4f3f-abbd-ccee0e52b020/info) specifies a 20.8-µm level of 560×384×640 uint16 voxels; [DataCite](https://api.datacite.org/dois/10.25493/JWM7-2G1) records CC BY 4.0. The paper's Neuroglancer view includes an explicit signed atlas-to-display transform `[[0,0,-1,456],[0,1,0,0],[-1,0,0,528]]` in 25-µm display units; voxel-centre and scale composition must still be checked before treating a CT index as a pose label. Anonymous 1-KiB byte-range reads of the 20.8-µm shards and a few remote orthogonal slices succeeded, with no bulk volume acquisition. This is a candidate for a *small TRAIN-source diversity ablation* because its 3-D anatomy and texture are from a real individual. It is **not** physically cut oblique histology, an independent expert pose label, or animal-separated validation; no separate specimen code beyond the one-brain dataset DOI was verified. Do not promote or bulk-mix it before a bounded source/coordinate QC and a blind selected-error comparison.

## Bounded independent-volume check, 2026-10-15

The three [gross-axis comparison planes](I:/AnatomyTracker/reports/ebrains_microct_ccf_three_plane_qc.png) are anatomically oriented and approximately located as expected under the published signed transform. This is a visual axis check, **not** a quantified landmark or voxelwise registration accuracy check. After that check, the full 20.8-µm level and a trilinearly resampled 25-µm CCF-grid intensity volume were cached only on `I:` with DOI, license, transform and SHA-256 metadata. No microCT image entered any model gradient or reserved cohort.

With 309 frozen, we then repeated the **same 256 TRAIN-inner virtual cuts** using this other animal's registered microCT intensity in place of the Allen average intensity, leaving the atlas support, synthetic deformation, plane, artifact random draws and coordinate truth unchanged. The generator replay asserted identical post-artifact states and sampled CCF sites. Blind first-choice mean surviving-site error rose **2.469→4.149 mm**, >5-mm cases **17→66**, and median unsigned normal error **21.9°→52.7°**. A ≤1.5-mm candidate in the model's top 16 fell **226/256→48/256**. The original and CT inputs are paired *diagnostics*, not duplicate training presentations. The source modality/contrast, real individual anatomy and registered-volume boundaries all change together; therefore the drop cannot be attributed to individual variation alone and is **not** a prediction of performance on actual physical histology. It is enough to reject the assumption that more unchanged Allen-template examples are a complete route to robust all-angle localization. The concise raw rows and source binding are at `I:/AnatomyTracker/reports/ebrains_source_shift_309_train`; reproducing scripts are `training/qc_ebrains_microct_ccf_axes.py`, `training/acquire_ebrains_microct_20p8um.py`, `training/resample_ebrains_microct_ccf_25um.py`, and `training/preflight_ebrains_source_shift_309.py`.

## Individual optical-volume lead, 2026-10-15

[BICCN's Brain Image Library](https://www.brainimagelibrary.org/download.html) contains individually imaged whole mouse brains. One [fMOST record, brain 418245-182727](https://api.brainimagelibrary.org/web/view?bildid=ace-cap-fix), identifies a specific brain and lists CC BY 4.0 on its record, but describes raw images and CCF-registered *neuron morphologies*, not a verified downloadable CCF-registered intensity volume or image-to-CCF transform. A [204-brain multi-modality study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-54745-6) reports registering individual fMOST/STPT/LSFM images to CCF, but its public-data statement points to source images and derived morphometry; it does not establish that the image transforms or useful downsampled aligned volumes are actually available per animal. BIL's [general download page](https://www.brainimagelibrary.org/download.html) says CC BY-SA 4.0 while some individual records say CC BY 4.0: check the *specific file/record* license and contributor terms before proprietary training. Likewise the [Allen connectivity API](https://brain-map.org/support/documentation/api-allen-brain-connectivity-atlas) documents individual red/blue background-fluorescence volumes resampled to the 25-µm atlas, but the [Allen Institute's default terms](https://alleninstitute.org/terms-of-use/) limit commercial incorporation unless a specific exception or permission applies. These are source leads, **not acquired training data or independent physical-cut validation**. The next useful decision is a metadata/transform/license check for one bounded optical specimen, not bulk downloading or another architecture run.

## Individual MRI anatomy lead, 2026-10-15

The [Liang and Zhang Dryad dataset](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.1vhhmgqv8) describes ex vivo MRI from ten normal C57BL/6 P60 mice registered to Allen space; [Dryad's terms](https://datadryad.org/terms) publish deposited datasets under CC0. A byte-range read of the 10.7-GB `MRI_C57_data.zip` directory, without downloading the archive, confirmed ten distinct subject folders (`C1`, `C3`–`C6`, `R2`–`R6`). The small C1 fractional-anisotropy header reports a 200×280×128 image at 62.5 µm isotropic resolution. It is a legacy Analyze header with zero originator bytes and no NIfTI signed affine, so the dataset's statement that images are aligned to Allen space does **not** yet bind voxel centers or left/right direction to this project's pinned 25-µm CCF. The `training/inspect_dryad_mri_c57_zip.py` range preflight reproduces these metadata observations. These are individual MRI anatomy sources, not physically sectioned histology or independent oblique validation; none entered model training. Use them as exact-coordinate synthetic sources only after an authenticated signed grid mapping and a bounded modality-transfer gate, rather than bulk-downloading ten brains on the strength of an archive title.

# 296: smooth image-fitting difficulty does not improve blind plane choice

The [predeclared TRAIN-inner test](BLIND_CANDIDATE_SMOOTH_FIT_296_PROTOCOL_20261012.md) completed normally in 1,363.5 seconds. It drew 32 **different physical synthetic sections**, four from each held-gradient synthetic base 56–63, with full-sphere plane sampling and one appearance per section. The frozen, project-trained 275 pose model supplied 16 blind candidate planes per image. Each candidate and an unranked exact-plane control received the same 120-step smooth image fit; candidate selection used only its best image objective, never coordinate truth. The fit used a single candidate-independent mask made from the blind candidates' atlas-support union and optional brush. Exact synthetic slab thickness was supplied, which favors this diagnostic over actual unknown-thickness use.

The independent post-exit `training.verify_blind_candidate_smooth_fit_296` audit **passed** after one verifier-only correction for Python tuple versus JSON list provenance. It checked frozen source/protocol/checkpoint and input/output hashes; replayed draw provenance and ID disjointness; reproduced all 32 blind top-16 branch sets; re-rendered and re-scored all 544 selected fits; and independently recomputed the predeclared gate. Neither the runner, saved result, score, threshold, nor checkpoint was changed. The `completed.json` SHA-256 is `a0b42605ee67706f1424d2e7ade0e9f817356719e8815202b9325051455c2a89`.

Selecting the apparently easiest fitted plane **worsened** mean rigid error by 0.494 mm versus the original pose ranking and 0.240 mm versus the same image score without fitting. Its selected mapped-site error was 2.462 mm, compared with 1.977 mm when fitting the original first-choice plane. Four of eight base means improved, below the required six. In 27 cases with a ≤1.5-mm candidate and a support-matched ≥2.0-mm candidate, the fitted image score preferred the nearer plane in **17/27 (63.0%)**, below the required 70%. Only the minimum matched-case count passed; the **conjunctive advancement gate failed**.

The visual image objective did improve numerically when used for selection (mean 0.616 for the prior-first fitted branch versus 0.518 for the selected fitted branch), but that lower score did not mean a better anatomical plane. The unranked **true synthetic plane** had 0.123-mm rigid error yet 0.136-mm error after the same fit under the blind-candidate mask; 18/32 true-plane maps worsened. This differs from 295's positive exact-plane result because 295 used the known-plane support to form its observable mask. The true-plane objective beat the blind selected score in only 22/32 sections. Among selected blind fits, 31/32 reached approximately the 0.35 derivative cap, and their mean warped-atlas support exit on the common mask was 40.3%. These observations indicate a serious image-objective/mask confound; they do not establish a unique causal mechanism or prove that an anatomy-aware learned fit signal cannot work.

The 16 more-oblique synthetic cases (≥30° from the nearest principal axis) had a descriptive +0.204-mm mean selection gain, whereas the other 16 had −1.192 mm. Neither the paired gate nor six-base condition passed, and all these cuts come from one atlas image source plus synthetic deformations. Do **not** infer that heavy-oblique physical cuts are solved, or restrict the model to cardinal planes based on this small synthetic stratum. Eight sparse (<15% valid) cases had −0.643-mm gain; optional-brush and no-brush strata both worsened.

**Decision:** do not feed this scalar fitting energy into pose training, scale the same optimizer, promote a model, or claim calibrated electrode-region probabilities. The useful conclusion is narrower: 275's top 16 often contain a substantially better plane (28/32 here), but this intensity/warp objective fails to identify it even with privileged exact thickness. The next model-development change must provide discriminative, spatial off-plane anatomical evidence and more credible acquired/independent-specimen appearance while retaining full-sphere geometry and honest ambiguity. Existing CC0 DAPI and CC-BY AIND individual 3-D volumes are not yet accepted as exact CCF pose labels because their signed native/reference coordinate chains and internal landmarks remain unverified; do not silently train on them as truth. Independent animal-level real and physical-oblique validation, uncertainty calibration, GUI integration, and a fair DeepSlice comparison remain open.

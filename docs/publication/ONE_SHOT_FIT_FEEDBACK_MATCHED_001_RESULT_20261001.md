# Matched one-shot fit-to-pose continuation (1 October 2026)

The completed run `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_fit_feedback_matched_001` compared two continuations of the **same** random-start one-shot batch-20,000 model and optimizer. Each arm trained for 4,000 four-image batches: three freshly sampled arbitrary-plane synthetic sections and one distinct real TRAIN section. The two arms used exactly the same draws (`draws.jsonl` SHA-256 `6657967f2e241fe177adf7bbb45b777a8cf41c7bfdf4c05e21f99d2fca4c4f30` in both), as required for a matched comparison. The 4,000 real sections were unique, drew from all 1,885 TRAIN donors, and did not overlap the preceding 20,000-section schedule. The run exited with no stderr; 4,000 training rows and 384 development rows per arm are retained.

Both arms replaced truth-reflection-only ranking with a geometry-soft target over all 16 pose/reflection branches. The fit arm alone added finite-thickness atlas mismatch through a bounded true-to-predicted pose bridge, with a known synthetic tissue mask and zero local warp. A nonzero fit-to-pose-head gradient was observed at the first batch (norm 2.89), so the learning connection exists. This is **oracle-guided local fit feedback**, not fit-based correction of the deployed top branch at its often distant predicted location.

At batch 2,000, the fit arm improved synthetic selected error versus control by 0.73 mm, but 2.15 mm of one synthetic identity's error accounted for most of that four-identity average difference. At batch 4,000 the fit arm was **worse on three of four** synthetic identities; its selected error was 0.27 mm higher than control. The real weak-reference differences were 0.058 mm and 0.017 mm in fit's favour, respectively, which cannot establish anatomical improvement. The control and fit best mixed-development scores occurred at batches 4,000 and 2,000, respectively, but neither is close to acceptable arbitrary-plane accuracy. The 21 originally ineligible synthetic development sections remain in the raw file as no-information cases; checkpoint scoring used only the 43 eligible sections. These synthetic identities are not independent biological animals.

Conclusion: a local fit loss can reach the coordinate predictor, but this experiment does **not** show a robust useful global alignment gain, and it does not repair the independently observed near-zero benefit of the image-only deformation head. Do not promote a checkpoint, calibrate confidence or use the public DeepSlice benchmark. The next targeted comparison is whether a one-pass deformation head that sees the **rendered atlas section at the candidate pose** can learn useful spatial correction with the correct plane supplied, and whether any benefit survives actual predicted planes. A true final joint system would then need fitting feedback from non-oracle candidate poses into global prediction.

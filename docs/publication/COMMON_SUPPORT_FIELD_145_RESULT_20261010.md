# Common-support anatomy field 145: failed development gate

The 2,000-batch continuation **failed** its frozen synthetic development gate. The added contrastive anatomy objective reduced its sampled training loss but did not improve correct-plane selection or tissue-to-atlas mapping against the matched control. Both arms also lost substantial mapping accuracy relative to their identical 143 starting checkpoint. Do not use either 145 endpoint for pose feedback, GUI inference, uncertainty calibration, or a public benchmark.

The committed trainer (`7ac9c3e`) continued the internally random-initialized 143 field from the same weights, optimizer and scaler in both arms. It processed 6,000 accepted, distinct physical-section draws (4,000 v4 and 2,000 v3) in 6,582 attempts over 2,000 updates. There was no deliberate reuse of a section with changed background. The treatment alone received common-pixel-support exact-versus-wrong energy ranking and known-CCF dense feature contrast; the control received the same ordinary correspondence, offset and visibility training. The contrastive loss averaged 4.904 over batches 1–100 and 4.306 over batches 1,901–2,000, but those windows contain different random sections. That decline is not evidence of generalization. The wrong-pair objective was applied in about 92–93% of update slots.

The independent verifier (`1bff9b4`) tested checkpoints 0, 500 and 2,000 on the previously frozen 248 eligible v4 and 243 eligible v3 development sections. The wrong plane was selected from a section-seeded 1–3 mm candidate bank for high *pixelwise* central tissue-support overlap. Exact and wrong atlas inputs then shared the identical support mask at every depth, with intensity zero outside it. The v4 primary cohort retained 187/248 sections meeting central intersection fraction ≥0.08 and IoU ≥0.45; v3 retained 193/243. These are eight reused synthetic deformation plans, **not independent biological animals** or fresh final validation.

For the treatment's ranking, zeroing atlas intensity produced exactly 50.00% (all ties); independently scrambling each candidate's within-support intensity gave 57.22%, only 4.28 points below intact treatment. Thus there is some intensity dependence, but not the required anatomy-specific effect. The 500-batch treatment rank was 50.80% versus 63.10% control, and its mapping had already degraded. V3 exact-plane globally supported hits went from 96.17% at parent to 82.52% treatment / 82.99% control; v3 blind-near hits went from 35.32% to 11.50% / 12.43%. The predeclared v3 *relative-to-control* regression guard passed, but it did not catch this large absolute loss. That is a limitation of the 145 protocol, not a reason to accept the models.

The collapse was predominantly a matching/abstention change, not proof that the predicted continuous offsets became 11 mm wrong: on v4 exact-plane sections the non-dustbin fraction fell from 97.49% to 84.40% treatment / 85.23% control, and on the near subset from 74.61% to 16.27% / 17.60%. The frozen development mapping criterion counts a site only when the match head does **not** choose its dustbin and the CCF point is within 0.5 mm. Mean point error over valid v4 exact sites changed from roughly 0.18 to 0.20 mm, while near-subset mean remained roughly 0.81–0.83 mm; these means include dustbin sites and cannot replace the counted hit rates.

The two-arm design establishes that the **added 145 objective did not help under this continuation**. It does not isolate why both arms regressed. Relative to 143, the 145 base curriculum used high-overlap 1–3 mm wrong candidates instead of the earlier support-*fraction*-matched ~2.2 mm candidates, removed the inherited ordinary energy-rank term, and continued AdamW at the inherited learning rate. The independent test cannot apportion the absolute regression among these simultaneous changes. It also cannot show whether feature contrast learned a transferable anatomical descriptor that the existing match/energy head failed to use. A focused frozen-feature positive-versus-wrong similarity readout is the next diagnostic. A further training continuation should restore a parent-quality retention guard and change one mechanism at a time; simply adding batches to 145 is not justified.

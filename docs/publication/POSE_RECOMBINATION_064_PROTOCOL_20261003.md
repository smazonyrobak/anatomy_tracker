# 064: can existing candidates be recombined into a better full plane?

The frozen 063 audit reproduced the 061 selected and best-of-14 physical errors exactly (2.656 and 1.022 mm). A truth-selected candidate with the best normal alone scores 1.706 mm, best center alone 1.593 mm, and best reflection-sensitive in-plane frame alone 1.314 mm. These factors are individually present but not reliably in one candidate. Before training a conditional pose network, measure whether the existing beam contains a good *centre and frame in different branches*.

On the same 246 eligible synthetic DEV sections, freeze the 059 checkpoint and 061 panel. For each actual 14-branch proposal, express its physical map as image-centre point plus two effective in-plane vectors. For each of the 14×14 centre/frame combinations, use the centre from one and the complete orientation, scale, shear and reflection-sensitive frame from the other. Compute mean visible-tissue CCF error against the synthetic dense target. Compare the truth-best recombination with the best original 14 and the old image-prior selection, weighting the eight synthetic identities equally. This uses truth *only to measure an upper bound*, not a deployable chooser. Preserve exact section provenance and hashes; do not view images.

A ≥0.25-mm gain over the original truth-best beam would justify a factorized pose-generation experiment. A smaller gain would point back to missing or inaccurate pose factors, not just their coupling. Either outcome is synthetic development only and cannot validate a real-animal model, calibrated probability, GUI replacement or public comparison.

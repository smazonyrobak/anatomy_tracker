# 124 learned atlas-anatomy ranker: development gate failed

The predeclared 124 test asked whether fitting each blind candidate's **predicted tissue map** to a finite-thickness atlas rendering could provide image-specific anatomical evidence for selecting its plane. The 122 step-2000 pose, matcher and mapper were frozen. Two newly initialized, otherwise identical rankers saw the same 1,500 independently drawn synthetic TRAIN sections: one received atlas intensity and support, while the control received support but zero atlas intensity. The run had 1,638 attempted draws and 958 support-matched TRAIN comparisons. Checkpoints at 0, 500, 1,000 and 1,500 presentations are frozen; the failed first attempt after one presentation is preserved separately and was not used for evaluation.

The independently audited DEV evaluation used all 247 eligible existing synthetic sections from eight deformation plans. There were 123 predeclared support-matched scored pairs; 81 sections lacked any blind mapped candidate within 1.5 mm, 38 lacked a support-matched wrong candidate, and five lacked enough common interior. The full-intensity ranker preferred the physically better candidate in **84/123 (68.3%)**, below the required 75% and below the identically trained support-only ranker's **88/123 (71.5%)**. Setting intensity to zero at evaluation also gave 88/123; swapping the source image and its features while keeping the original predicted pose/map gave 83/123. These controls show no demonstrated incremental atlas-intensity benefit in this conditional test. The zero-intensity intervention is out of distribution for the full-trained ranker, and the swap holds the original-image map fixed; neither control proves that the whole pipeline ignores the image.

Plan-equal selected mapped-tissue error was **3.273 mm** for frozen 122, **3.191 mm** for full intensity, and **3.222 mm** for support-only. Thus full improved by only 0.082 mm versus 122 and 0.031 mm versus support, short of the required 0.30 and 0.15 mm. Rigid-gauge error did not regress (3.277 to 3.205 mm), but only five of eight synthetic plans were non-worse than 122; six were required. The selected-action analysis is coherent but limits interpretation: full changed the selected candidate slot in 100/247 synthetic sections and original/corrected action in 22/247, whereas the ranker was trained on corrected maps and its evidence was added equally to both actions. That was predeclared, not an evaluator bookkeeping error.

On inherited **weak-affine**, not expert, real DEV references, donor-equal five-point discrepancy changed from 0.922 to 0.985 mm for coronal (6 donors, 64 sections) and from 2.004 to 1.910 mm for sagittal (8 donors, 158 sections). The real guardrails passed, but support-only was similar or better (0.984 and 1.876 mm). Full changed selected slot in 7 coronal and 68 sagittal sections, with no original/corrected action changes. These weak labels do not establish real anatomical accuracy.

**Decision:** stage-1 gate failed (six of 13 conditions passed). Do not promote 124, feed its score back into direct pose probabilities, calibrate uncertainty from it, or claim acquired-oblique/DeepSlice performance. The full and support controls' similarity is consistent with a support/deformation shortcut and inadequate image-specific internal-anatomy discrimination, rather than evidence that more presentations of this same objective would solve selection. The next targeted work must supply an image-dependent internal correspondence learning signal, test it on blind support-matched candidates with source perturbation controls, and only then connect fitting loss to the direct probabilistic pose head. The large no-near-candidate fraction also means candidate recall needs independent improvement; a better ranker cannot repair an absent correct candidate.

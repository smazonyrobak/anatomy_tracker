**Decision: neither adapted checkpoint advances.** The [predeclared matched comparison](SHARED_REAL_MAP_316_PROTOCOL_20261015.md) completed 600 batches per arm from exactly the same 309 checkpoint, acquired sections and independently sampled synthetic cuts. The map-only arm updated local-mapping modules; the treatment also updated the shared image encoder/lateral features. Pose/probability heads stayed frozen, although the shared features could indirectly move their predictions. The readout used the fixed 600-batch endpoint; neither the 300-batch checkpoint nor the AL1A guard was used to choose an endpoint.

The read-only [independent receipt and raw-row audit](../../training/verify_shared_real_map_316.py) exited with `verified: true`. Both adaptations improve the AL1A map RMS by about **3.0% against zero field** and **7.0% against the unchanged parent**, but the preregistered gate required at least 10% against *both*: an AL1A mean no higher than 0.094947 mm. The map-only and shared models miss that limit by about 0.00738 and 0.00736 mm. The shared treatment beats map-only by just **0.000016 mm**, far below its required 0.005-mm attributable advantage, while its blind plane predictions worsen slightly. Synthetic exact-pose mapping improves by about 3%, not enough to repair the approximately 2.3-mm first-choice error. All safety-retention and image-permutation clauses pass, but none substitute for the failed primary map gate.

The fixed-plane image-permutation check gives AL1A mean RMS 0.107351 mm for map-only and 0.107578 mm for shared; each original acquired image beats its cyclically permuted replacement in 47/52 and 48/52 sections. This indicates that their outputs still depend on section-specific image content. The 309 parent already showed a similar 0.005394-mm permutation penalty, so the check does **not** establish that the adaptation created a stronger anatomical cue. It rules out only a plane-conditioned, image-independent explanation of the final maps. The [315 no-training image assay](ACQUIRED_FIELD_IMAGE_SIGNAL_315_RESULT_20261015.md) found a modest atlas-image signal for the human field; 316 shows that this small labeled cohort and current modules did not turn it into a useful cross-animal map gain.

The AL1A fields and planes are approximate human VisuAlign work on one repeatedly inspected, near-coronal TRAIN animal; they are not exact independent biological truth. The 64 synthetic cases are a reused TRAIN-inner panel. Their saved records omitted true reflection and individual PSF, so the exact-pose map readout uses an oracle reflection inferred from saved truth and a common 312 fixed PSF approximation. These limitations are shared across compared checkpoints. No physical steep-oblique, unseen-animal, calibrated uncertainty, electrode-region probability, GUI-readiness or DeepSlice claim follows.

**Process correction:** stop this real-field adaptation branch rather than extending batch count, tuning a map loss, or layering a pose-feedback loop onto it. The frozen 309 full-sphere panel has about 1.54 mm of first-choice-versus-top-16 regret, versus roughly 0.1 mm of exact-pose local synthetic residual; the blind candidate choice is the dominant measured error. Earlier scalar, rendered-atlas, local-fit and direct map-gradient selectors failed their own all-candidate gates. The next substantial investment needs *new, verifiable information*: independently grounded physical-cut pose/deformation labels (especially oblique), or a mechanistically different spatial representation that first demonstrates a tail-safe blind-choice gain on fresh full-sphere cuts and retains donor-separated acquired-image behavior. A rough electrode-insertion angle is not a section-normal label. Do not enlarge training or touch the sealed biological calibration/final sets and DeepSlice benchmark to compensate for this gate failure. The single-slice model must eventually expose ambiguity/abstention for low-information fragments rather than claim a perfect plane from every damaged image.

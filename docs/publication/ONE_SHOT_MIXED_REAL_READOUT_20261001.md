## Physical-metric audit after the 11 mm concern

The 11.44 mm figure was independently recomputed from saved physical-µm states and is **not** a unit conversion or 192-to-256 resize error. It is nevertheless a misleading *anatomical* summary: four of the five sampled locations are full-canvas corners, and **none of the 172 corner samples across the 43 eligible sections contains visible tissue**. The fifth, central sample contains tissue in 36/43 sections. Thus this score is useful for global frame geometry, not as a brain-landmark error or electrode-site error.

Using the saved selected pose/reflection, the exact synthetic tissue-to-CCF target field, and only pixels with saved visible support >0.25 gives **4.41 mm four-synthetic-identity-equal mean tissue-placement error** (4.42 mm pooled across the 43 sections). The best of the 16 saved pose/reflection branches, selected with ground truth and therefore unavailable at inference, still gives **2.12 mm identity-equal mean** on those same tissue pixels. Selected frame-centre error is **2.81 mm identity-equal mean**; selected plane-normal error remains about **48 degrees**. The selected reflection agrees with the synthetic target in only 21/43 cases. At the true pose, the unwarped frame is just ~57 µm from the saved target field on visible tissue, so the millimetre-scale discrepancy is primarily global pose/branch failure rather than the generator's local deformation. These numbers describe pose-only mapping; they do not include the network's predicted local correction. They are severe despite the full-canvas metric overstatement and remain internal synthetic-development results, not biological-animal validation.

The training objective also has a concrete mismatch: it ranks eight modes using the **target** reflection, while inference chooses among joint mode×predicted-reflection scores. More importantly, the mapping loss substitutes the **target pose** into the selected mapping branch. It can train shared image features and the local mapper, but it does **not** send atlas-fit gradients through that branch's predicted pose. This training stage therefore has not implemented the central fitting-to-coordinate learning requirement. Correcting the reported metric and the training/inference mismatch is more urgent than treating the 11 mm number as a direct anatomical-landmark result or scaling this recipe unchanged.

The local mapper also failed to show a useful held-out correction. On those 43 eligible synthetic development sections with the **true pose supplied**, the model's map was 59.09 µm versus 58.98 µm with zero added warp. A separate fresh-draw check, `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_fresh_fitter_001`, found 108.33 versus 108.29 µm over 64 distinct eligible plane draws, with only 51.6% improved. These fresh draws reuse TRAIN synthetic deformation bases and are diagnostic, not independent-animal validation.

There is nevertheless a reason to test fit-to-pose feedback: `I:/AnatomyTracker/runs/one_shot_full_pose_capture_001` found the true atlas fit better than every ±250 µm AP/DV/ML shift on 43 eligible sections. Starting close to truth, a step down the fit gradient reduced actual five-point pose error in 79–97% of the seven tested perturbation types; the opposite-gradient control almost never helped. This is a **local** directional result, not evidence that the current 5–11 mm candidate errors can be corrected globally.

Decision: do not scale the old training recipe, calibrate probabilities, run DeepSlice, or present the GUI integration as qualified. The next controlled continuation compares the same model/checkpoint and exactly matched fresh training draws with versus without bounded atlas-fit gradients into the pose head, while jointly repairing 16-branch ranking. Its oracle-guided near-truth bridge is a curriculum diagnostic, not the final deployed prediction loop; success must be demonstrated on eligible arbitrary-plane development sections without weakening the real-donor result, and the fitter itself still needs improvement.

**Decision: FAIL, both arms.** Best new-v7 first-choice gain is only 0.083 mm, versus the required 0.300 mm; top-16 capture gains only 2/64 rather than at least 0.05. Every checkpoint chose a free-pose branch on all 224 cases. On the new v7 panel, the highest-scored anchor error changed from 3.836 mm to 3.722 mm (shared final) or 3.670 mm (untied final), while the oracle-best anchor was already around 1.76–1.79 mm. The median anchor-versus-free score gap remained about −2.6 log units. Shared versus untied final projections were nearly tied; this does not establish a useful advantage of either. The 221 framed batch-400 improvement is real within that panel but does not rescue the predeclared fresh-v7 gate. Training losses and slight truth-chosen oracle gains cannot substitute for blind first-choice accuracy.

The failure is more specific than "not enough training": anchors contain plausible geometry, yet an image-only score continues to rank the wrong branch. It is an inference, not a proven causal attribution, that a candidate-specific atlas/fit signal is needed to reject anatomically inconsistent planes. Do not extend this correction, promote its checkpoint, calibrate its probabilities, alter GUI inference, or run DeepSlice benchmarking. Next development should target the user's actual loop: predict candidate plane → render corresponding atlas tissue → fit local deformation → penalize an implausibly hard fit → revise plane and warp jointly, with a matched direct one-pass comparator rather than assuming iteration wins. Evaluate that on independent sections and genuinely animal-separated acquired data before scaling.

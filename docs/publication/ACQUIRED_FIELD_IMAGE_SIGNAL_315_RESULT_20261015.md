# 315: acquired local correction has visible atlas-image signal

The frozen [protocol](ACQUIRED_FIELD_IMAGE_SIGNAL_315_PROTOCOL_20261015.md) ran without a checkpoint or optimizer update on all 52 valid `AL1A` TRAIN-inner Lupori sections. At each supplied approximate human plane, it compared the observed fluorescence image with the same bound atlas-average finite-thickness rendering under zero field, the affine-free human VisuAlign field, its sign reversal, and the raw full human field. All four renderings used the same eroded source-tissue and common-atlas-support pixels; all 52 had ≥400 such pixels. Higher scores are better. These are within-one-animal appearance checks, not independent physical deformation truth.

The affine-free human-minus-zero mean/median was +0.01854/+0.01612 Pearson and +0.001387/+0.001618 MIND. Both scores passed the predeclared descriptive gate: ≥35/52 sections prefer human over both zero and sign-reversed fields, with positive median margins. The raw full human field did somewhat better than the affine-free field, so some extra benefit lies in the removed affine/pose component; that does not erase the positive locally representable signal. Together with [314](ACQUIRED_MAP_314_RESULT_AND_DECISION_20261015.md), this narrows the failure: a human local correction **can** improve alignment under the current atlas-image representation, while the current 309/312 learned fields do **not** beat zero displacement on these acquired sections. It does not show that a learned field can recover the correction from an unseen animal, that the weak plane is exact, or that feedback from a learned map should already update pose. The next bounded intervention should change the image-to-local-correspondence learning pathway and expose it to real field supervision with independent synthetic arbitrary-plane rehearsal; compare frozen-encoder map adaptation to shared-encoder-plus-map adaptation on matched draws. Require a meaningful AL1A improvement beyond zero with pose and synthetic-map retention before scaling or letting map-fit gradients influence pose.

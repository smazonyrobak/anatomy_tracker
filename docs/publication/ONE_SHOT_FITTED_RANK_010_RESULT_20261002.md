At the exact reference plane and reflection, **zero local displacement scores 0.139 mm** while the learned local map scores **0.186 mm**; it improves 26 sections and worsens 159, and worsens the mean in all eight synthetic identities. Thus the fitter has not learned a useful correction even when pose is supplied. Fitted rigid selection regret relative to the top-eight reference-best increases from 1.641 to 1.739 mm, whereas the frozen protocol required at least a 25% decrease and a 0.5 mm mapped-error improvement. Only 30/185 rigid choices improve and 35 worsen; unchanged choices account for the remainder. This is an efficacy failure, not a marginally missed deployment threshold.

On 64 separate real DEV images from six donors, fitted selection moves farther from the existing **weak Allen affine** references: 0.619 versus 0.571 mm donor-equal five-point error. These labels are not blinded expert truth, and their near-coronal acquisition does not validate arbitrary oblique planes. The eight synthetic DEV identities are deformation instances of one atlas, not independent biological animals. The 71 low-support synthetic rows remain in the raw output as unscored/abstention candidates.

The in-run loss decreased, and over batches 10,001–12,000 its **mapped fitted** diagnostic averaged 2.249 mm versus 2.382 mm for the **rigid image prior**, while top-eight reference-best averaged 1.030 mm. The first two training diagnostics are not the same metric and cannot establish a ranking gain. The matched frozen DEV comparison above shows that neither selected pose nor mapping generalized usefully; appearance, synthetic-identity differences and optimization remain possible causes. Across the matched development panels, 010 also did not materially improve proposal capture over 009 (all-32 reference-best 1.072 versus 1.082 mm). A better reranker alone cannot solve the remaining global pose error.

**Correction to the frozen training monitor:** `real_weak_prior_five_um` in `train_one_shot_fitted_ranking_010.py` indexes the branch from the real batch row but gathers the error from synthetic row 0, because it does not slice `five[synthetic:]` before `gather`. Its apparent ~9.8 mm training mean is invalid; the real contribution to `direct_loss` is indexed correctly. Independent inference from the exact frozen checkpoint on the first and last 12 scheduled real TRAIN sections gives 0.142–0.760 mm five-point errors against their weak Allen affines; the last 12 contemporaneous log entries instead say 2.767–14.916 mm. This small spot-check does not establish whole-cohort accuracy. The source and raw logs remain unchanged for provenance. The independently recomputed DEV results and the previously verified 11.21 mm synthetic case are unaffected.

**Decision:** Do not promote this checkpoint or call its normalized scores probabilities. Do not extend the same fitted scorer unchanged. The next training stage must make the correct-plane local map genuinely beat zero displacement and improve coarse plane capture, while retaining a direct physical pose anchor; only then should fitted anatomy and deformation difficulty be trusted to teach pose. Candidate-head spatial compression (256-pixel input to a 4×4 pose grid) and full-canvas deformation penalties on sparse tissue are plausible mechanisms to test, not established causes. Keep the standalone random-initialized checkpoint lineage and strict donor provenance. Reassess on independent development material before any public DeepSlice comparison or GUI promotion.
